# Target policy — what your 30-day number actually is

Set by the board on 28 Oct 2026. Every kit's `07-measurement.md` follows this.

| Your product sells to | Your 30-day target |
| --- | --- |
| **Consumers** (a person signing up for themselves) | **1,000 signups** |
| **Businesses** (a shop, restaurant, school, clinic, agency, studio, PG, startup) | **10 customers** |

That is not a typo. **Ten.**

---

## Why the B2B number is ten and not a thousand

Because a thousand is the wrong shape of number for a business product, and chasing it makes students do worse work.

A consumer app can be handed to a thousand strangers in a month; a scroll-stopping Reel does it in a weekend. A product sold to restaurant owners cannot. One person, working the phone and the pavement for thirty days, can have a real conversation with maybe two hundred owners. Ten of them paying attention, onboarded, and using the thing weekly is a genuinely good month and the beginning of a business.

The failure mode the old target produced was predictable: to make a four-figure headline, you bolt a free tool onto the side of a B2B product, count its anonymous uses as "signups", and report 1,040. The number looks like traction and contains none. Worse, it consumes the month — you spend week three building a calculator instead of visiting the owners who would have paid you.

So: **ten real businesses.** Harder than it sounds, and worth more than a thousand of anything else.

---

## What counts as one of the ten

All four of these, or it does not count:

1. **A real business, not a friend doing you a favour.** A restaurant with customers. A tutor with students paying her.
2. **Onboarded** — their actual data is in your product. Their menu, their beds, their students. Not a demo account you filled in for them.
3. **Used it in the last seven days** without you asking them to.
4. **You know their name and have their phone number.** If you can't call them, they are not a customer yet.

**Paying is not required.** A restaurant that connected its Google account and replies to reviews through you every week counts, free plan or not. Ten free-but-active beats three paying-but-dormant, in month one.

**Do not count:** waitlist emails, free-tool uses, demo bookings, a WhatsApp reply saying "sounds interesting", anyone who signed up and never entered data, or your own test accounts.

---

## What counts as one of the thousand

Per kit — your `07` names your specific one, because "signup" means different things for a browser game and a Mac app:

- an account created, or
- an app installed, or
- a session started, where the product needs no account

One rule holds everywhere: **report the activation number next to it, always.** Installs with no first launch, signups with no first action — those are not users, and `07` tells you which second number to put beside the headline. A thousand signups with eighty activations is a worse month than four hundred with three hundred, and the board wants to be able to tell the difference.

## Not sure which one you are?

**Who pulls out the card — a person spending their own money on themselves, or someone spending their business's money to make the business work better?**

Edge cases, decided:

| Product | Policy | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tool sold to individual tutors, freelancers, creators | **B2B — 10** | She's buying it to run her teaching business |
| Interview / exam / career prep | **B2C — 1,000** | A student spending on herself |
| Developer tool, free, individual installs | **B2C — 1,000** | The developer adopts it personally, no procurement |
| Dev tool sold to engineering teams with a seat count | **B2B — 10** | A team decision with a budget |
| Marketplace with both sides | **B2B — 10 supply-side** | Supply is the hard side; demand follows |
| Campus/community app, free to students | **B2C — 1,000** | |
| Anything sold to a school, hospital, or company | **B2B — 10** | |

If your kit's `01-positioning.md` and this table disagree, the kit wins — it was written against your actual product. Tell us and we'll fix the table.

## Stretch beyond your target

Ten is the floor, not the ceiling. Several wave-1 B2B kits run plans that aim well past it — CartPing's plan targets 150 stores, HAUS HOME's 140 PG owners — because the distribution map genuinely supports it. Those stay as written. The ten is what you are held to; the plan is what you're aiming at.

The same holds the other way: if you are B2C and you clear 1,000 in twelve days, don't coast for eighteen. Go read the retention number in your `07`, which is the one that was always going to matter more.
