# The First Story — Positioning

**Live:** https://thefirststory.live/ · **Category:** Name discovery / cultural reading · **Profile:** https://shipped.money/p/the-first-story

The actual headline checked 29 October is **“What if choosing a name meant discovering a story?”** The CTA is **“Explore Names”**, pointing to `/discover`. Free discovery is live. The homepage promotes a **₹499/30-day Pass**, but [the Pass page](https://thefirststory.live/premium) explicitly says **“Preview only. Payment will be available in a future release.”** Do not sell or count that as a live paid product.

Your first user is a parent-to-be who has twenty names in a family chat but wants to understand the language, source and story behind a few of them. They need a considered shortlist, not another thousand-name list.

**Pitch:** Explore an Indian name through its language, source and cultural context before adding it to your shortlist.

**Short version:** Fewer names. Better reasons to keep them.

## Your wedge

One name, its native script, an inspectable source and a reason it fits the family's preferences. The discovery archive visibly distinguishes “Source reviewed” and “Editorial review,” and labels pictures artistic rather than historical depictions. Preserve those differences. Don't market every entry as equally verified or turn a dictionary sense into an invented life story.

[Behind the Name](https://www.behindthename.com/) provides openly accessible name etymology/history at no payment for public browsing. A family can also keep a shortlist in a normal notes app. The First Story earns attention through Indian languages, traditions and contextual pathways—not a claim that other sites contain no stories or that a name determines destiny.

## The uncomfortable bit

Some archive entries cite specific records/dictionaries, while others have broader editorial context. The real test is whether a reader can inspect the source and see exactly what it supports. A character's story, a name's lexical meaning and a modern naming recommendation are different claims.

The public homepage, discovery and Pass routes were read; no account, shortlist save or payment was completed. Keep the B2C target at 1,000 meaningful first discovery sessions, with source opens and saved shortlists beside it. A family member clicking the same link repeatedly is not a new person, and a preview Pass interest is not a sale.
