# Maison Vérité — Copy pack

Never label a real seller's item fake or genuine in promotional copy. Use your own/permitted images and label sample output. A red-flag check is not a certificate or a guarantee.

## 1. Warm test invitation

> Would you inspect a sample photo-check report and tell me what you think it means? I built Maison Vérité to flag visible details worth a closer look before a resale purchase. It does not certify authenticity. Three checks come with a free account; no need to buy anything or upload a seller's private images for my benefit. I'd like to know whether the next-photo request is useful and the limitation is clear: https://www.maisonverite.in/.

One follow-up after seven days: “Closing the loop on the sample report. If it isn't useful now, no problem; I won't keep nudging. If you looked, did any wording sound more certain than the photos justified?”

## 2. Owned-feed post

> A blurry logo photo doesn't need a confident verdict. It needs a better photo.
>
> Before paying for a second-hand item, I want to know which detail is visible, which isn't and what I should ask the seller to show next.
>
> I built Maison Vérité for that first red-flag pass. It is not an authenticity certificate, a brand service or a promise that an item is genuine. Three checks with a free account; Pro is $19/month.
>
> Try a sample first: https://www.maisonverite.in/.
>
> What would you need to see before you trusted a resale listing?

## 3. Value-only reply

> A photo can be too unclear to support a conclusion. I'd ask for a better close-up of the specific detail and check the seller/provenance independently. An AI image result shouldn't be your only reason to transfer money.

Use only where relevant. Don't accuse a seller, repeat unverified brand-identification rules or imply a particular inspection guarantees the purchase.

## 4. Organizer invitation

> Could I run a free small session on what resale photos can and can't show? We'd use owned sample images, practise asking for clearer details and distinguish a red-flag prompt from professional authentication. I build Maison Vérité and would disclose that. No buying, uploading private listings or public verdicts on sellers. May I send you the outline?

## 5. Approved broadcast

> A small free session on reading resale photos without overclaiming: visible detail, unclear detail and the next question to ask. We'll use samples, not someone's purchase dispute. Maison Vérité is an optional photo-red-flag demo, not authentication: https://www.maisonverite.in/. Reply to the organizer for confirmed details; no purchase required.

## 6. Peerlist copy

**Tagline:** Photo-based resale red flags, with uncertainty kept visible.

> Maison Vérité helps users inspect photos and identify details worth another look. It doesn't certify authenticity or replace a qualified review. Three checks with a free account; Pro $19/month. I'd like feedback on whether a first-time reader understands an unclear-photo outcome and the next question to ask, rather than treating a polished report as a certificate.

## 7. Product Hunt maker comment

> The useful result here isn't a confident yes/no. It may be “this photo isn't enough.” I built a first-pass red-flag assistant, not an authenticity service, and I want that boundary to survive screenshots and sharing. Please start with a sample and tell me which line sounds stronger than the evidence supports. No vote or purchase is expected.

## 8. Editor pitch

**Subject:** A free worksheet on the limits of resale photos

> I wrote a short worksheet: what is visible, what isn't, what additional photo to request and what needs a qualified review. Could it fit a future community edition? I can send the full piece with owned sample images and disclose that I build Maison Vérité. No paid feature, buyer list or claims about a real seller.

For repeat posts use different uncertainty lessons, not more spectacular fake-detection claims. Don't imply a brand recognizes your report, and don't copy another service's guarantee into your own marketing.
