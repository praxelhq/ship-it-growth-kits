# Comic World — Positioning

**Live:** https://comicworld.me/ · **Category:** Content and media · **Profile:** https://shipped.money/p/comicworld

## The thing to face first

The site is live now, despite the roster’s old down label. The important problem is privacy and payment clarity: current terms say six-page comics are always public on Explore; a twelve-page comic can be kept off. Story planning is free, but drawing is paid. Do not promote a private family keepsake without explaining that boundary.

## What is actually on your page

Checked 29 October 2026: **“Your story. As a comic.”**. The next explanation is a spoken or typed personal story, approved characters and an editable storyboard before the comic is drawn. The action is **“Start my comic”**. Current terms say story planning/style/characters/storyboard editing are free. Drawing is paid once for six or twelve pages; the exact amount is shown before payment and was not established by this audit. These are observations from [your public product page](https://comicworld.me/), not a completed purchase or a promise that every authenticated flow works.

The homepage CTA points to /create, which opens “Tell me your story”. The terms require adults 18+, Google sign-in to draw, permission for pictured people and limits on public figures/copying. The gallery calls its examples real stories, but those are the product’s claims, not independently verified customer testimonials. No comic was generated or bought here.

## Your first person

An adult who already tells a harmless personal first-project story publicly and wants to turn it into a comic they are comfortable sharing. Start there rather than inviting private relationship stories into a public-by-default output.

Do not broaden this to everyone who might someday enjoy the category. Find twenty people with this exact situation, watch five use the product, then repeat the invitation that produced an unprompted return.

## The pitch

> Plan a comic from your own story, approve the storyboard, then choose whether to pay to have it drawn.

For a bio or a short reply:

> Your personal story, storyboard first; paid drawing with visibility explained.

## Your opening use case

Use your own harmless public anecdote with no confidential details or unconsenting faces. The first useful moment is editing the storyboard before drawing, not paying and hoping. Choose visibility only after understanding the current six/twelve-page distinction.

## The real alternative

[Storyboard That](https://www.storyboardthat.com/pricing) has a Free edition with two storyboards per week and a three-cell layout; its free storyboards are public. Its page displayed an introductory offer but not a reliable full individual amount in this fetch, so no paid comparison is invented. Comic World’s narrower job is narrated personal-story planning and AI drawing after approval, not a claim that all comic tools are private or expensive.

## What you need to prove

Record the actual story-to-storyboard flow using your own non-sensitive anecdote. Show the price and visibility choice before any payment. Verify the delivered page count and included redraws with a real internal test before promising the purchase experience publicly.

**Your board target stays 1,000 signups in 30 days.** Your product-specific counting rule is in `07`. The channel forecast in `02` is a planning scenario, not evidence that you will reach it. If your actual ceiling is lower, report the shortfall and the active people you did help; never reclassify visits or repeat sessions to fill the gap.
