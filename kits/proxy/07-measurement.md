# Proxy — Measurement

**B2C target:1,000 new consumer accounts in 30 days.** Report **completed first rehearsals** beside signups, then voluntary second-day practice. A wishlist address, scorecard image view or repeated login isn't another signup. One free scenario per day is a product limit, not a request to pressure a daily streak.

Activation is completing one free Work scenario and reviewing its feedback. The stronger usefulness signal is the user identifying a sentence they'd change. Don't treat an AI score as a validated psychological measurement or a real-world outcome.

## Honest arithmetic

At an assumed 20%visitor-to-account rate,1,000 accounts needs 5,000 qualified visitors. At 60%first-practice completion, that yields 600 activations. These are test assumptions, not observed benchmarks. Your eight posts,20 warm invitations, one conditional 15-seat circle and uncertain directory launch plausibly form a 10–35 completed-rehearsal experiment. They don't establish 5,000 visits. Add a new partner's reach only after actual acceptance and remove overlapping users.

## Weekly decisions

|Day|Check and response|
|---|---|
|7|If fewer than five of ten willing testers finish a free Work loop, observe the failing step before sending more invitations. Any privacy leak or misleading feedback claim requires correction before expansion.|
|14|Count accounts, completed practices and actual circle attendance. If users don't understand the free limit or think voice is available, fix those expectations.|
|21|Who returned without reminders? Ask what sentence they changed and what feedback they rejected. No repeat value: narrow the scenario rather than add more scores.|
|30|Report accounts, activations, repeat practice, paid readiness/purchases/refunds and hours. State target shortfall and next test clearly.|

The thresholds are experiment decisions, not clinical or industry norms. Do not publish private rehearsal content as proof of growth. A user choosing not to have a risky conversation after practising may be exercising good judgment; the product shouldn't force a real-world action to count as valuable.
