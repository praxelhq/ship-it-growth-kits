# Scene Kya Hai? — Positioning

**Live:** https://www.scenekyahai.xyz/ · **Category:** Mood-led local discovery · **Profile:** https://shipped.money/p/scene-kya-hai

The actual browser headline checked 29 October is **“SceneKyaHai?”** The CTA is **“Let's make questionable plans.”** It opens an eight-step curation flow. A built-in example was used to inspect the next question: starting area/city in India, with manual entry or optional location detection. No actual location, completed recommendation or payment was submitted.

Your first user is an adult in one genuinely supported Indian city who has a free evening but can't turn “I want to do something” into a choice. They need a relevant idea they can verify, not a booking guarantee or a push toward reckless behaviour.

**Pitch:** Turn a mood and starting area into a scene you can inspect and choose for yourself.

**Short version:** Less scrolling, one idea worth checking.

## The wedge

One mood, a broad starting area and a result with an understandable reason. The footer says **“A scene, not an itinerary”** and **“Your call, always.”** Keep those limits. The product must show its source/freshness and whether it knows a real venue, opening hours, cost or availability.

[Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/about/) already provides public place discovery, lists and navigation without a consumer browsing fee. Scene Kya Hai earns attention through narrowing choices around mood, if the actual result is useful. It does not replace checking the venue's current details or making a reservation.

## The uncomfortable bit

The landing page's personality is clearer than the offer. No price/free allowance or full privacy explanation was visible before the curation entry. Explain those before asking for location or the rest of eight steps. Don't advertise a one-tap instant plan when the actual path is longer.

The initial browser steps work; the completed result and any account/paid boundary are untested here. Keep the B2C target at 1,000 first meaningful curation sessions, with completed results and voluntarily confirmed useful choices beside it. A location button click is not activation, and a generated scene isn't a real outing.
