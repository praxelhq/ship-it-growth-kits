# Skill Prep — Landing page conversion fixes

**Do these before you drive traffic.** You have 31 days and then your audience sits an exam, so there is no second attempt at this.

**Good news first: your page is well built.** Clear H1 (*"Think faster. Decide sharper."*), a subheadline that names all five skills, no dead `href="#"` links, a real FAQ, a transparent free tier, and *"Free forever plan · No card needed"* right under the CTA. And you have one asset nobody else in this cohort has.

---

## Fix 1 — Protect the hero question. It's the best conversion asset in the cohort. 🔴

You have a **live, ungated question in the hero**: *"Try one · Speed math 1 / 5 · 47 × 11 = ?"* with four tappable answers and *"Tap an answer."*

No signup. No email. No card. The visitor is **using your product four seconds after landing.** Nobody else in this cohort of 307 projects has anything this good, and it should convert at 15–25% rather than the 2–3% a normal landing page manages.

**So this fix is mostly "don't break it":**

- **Never put a modal, cookie banner or newsletter popup over it.** That single mistake would cost you more than every channel in `02` could earn.
- **Make sure it's the first thing visible on a phone**, above everything else, with no scroll. Your traffic is Instagram and Telegram — overwhelmingly mobile.
- **Let her finish all five.** A question-2 signup wall turns your best asset into a bait-and-switch. Ask after question 5, when she's invested: *"That's 4 of 5. Keep your streak →"*
- **Count down inside it.** *"31 days to CAT. Question 1 of 5."* The deadline is your most motivating copy and this is the highest-attention pixel on your site.

**Improve it in one way only:** after she answers, show the *method* in one line — *"Split the digits, add in the middle: 4_7 → 4+7=11 → 517."* Right now she learns whether she was right. With the method she learns something, and **learning something is what makes her share the link.**

---

## Fix 2 — Delete "Pricing announced at launch" 🔴

It's on your live pricing page where a price should be.

To a visitor deciding whether to start a daily habit, that sentence reads as: *the free thing I'm about to build a 30-day streak on is going to be taken away from me.* Your FAQ answers it well — *"Levels 1–3 of every skill and one daily session are free forever"* — but only for the minority who scroll that far.

**Replace the pricing block with:**

> **Free — forever.** Levels 1–3 in every skill · one daily session · streaks, XP and tips
> **Pro — ₹[number]/month.** All 10 levels · unlimited practice · progress analytics · AI feedback on guesstimate reasoning
>
> *The free tier is free permanently. It is not a trial.*

**Pick a number today.** ₹199–₹299/month is right for this audience — it is what one coaching mock series costs and well under what any institute charges. If you genuinely don't want to sell yet, show only the free plan and label Pro "coming soon" with a date. **Either is better than an unanswered question next to a price.**

---

## Fix 3 — Put CAT and the countdown above the fold 🔴

Your badge says *"For CAT, XAT & consulting interviews"*, which is correct and far too quiet.

**You have the best timing of any project in this cohort — [29 November, 2.84 lakh registered candidates](https://www.mbauniverse.com/cat/date-and-timings) — and your page doesn't mention the date.**

Make the deadline the headline:

> **31 days to CAT 2026.**
> # Think faster. Decide sharper.
> ### Five minutes a day on the two things that actually cost you marks: calculation speed and careless mistakes.

Automate the counter so it's right every morning, and switch it to XAT (early January) on 30 November so the page isn't stale the day after your audience's world ends.

**Why this matters more than it looks:** every visitor arrives from a channel where she was already thinking about the deadline. Matching her state of mind in the first line is the cheapest conversion you will ever buy.

---

## Fix 4 — Settle on one name, today 🟠

Your page says **Practical Skills**. Your domain is **skillprepp.space**. The cohort roster calls you **Skill Prep**. Three names for one product.

A student who hears "Practical Skills" in a Telegram group and searches it will not find you. Worse, your Instagram captions, Reddit posts and Telegram channel are all about to point at a brand name that doesn't match the URL.

**Pick "Skill Prep"** — it matches the domain, and the domain is what gets pasted. Then change it everywhere on the page, including the nav. Fifteen minutes, and it stops a slow permanent leak in every channel in `02`.

---

## Fix 5 — Add an `og:image` 🟠

You don't have one. Every time your link is pasted into Telegram, WhatsApp or Instagram DMs, the preview card renders with no picture.

**Your distribution is almost entirely links pasted into chat apps.** This is a direct tax on your primary channel and it's twenty minutes of work.

Make the image do the selling: a 1200×630 card showing `47 × 11 = ?` with the four answer tiles, the words **"Five minutes a day"**, and `skillprepp.space`. Someone will solve it in the preview and tap through.

---

## Fix 6 — Make "it brings your mistakes back" a hero claim 🟠

Your how-it-works section says it well — *"Mistakes come back"*, *"Hit 80% and you level up; the timer tightens. Struggle and it eases off."*

**That mechanic is your only real differentiator against a free Telegram question channel**, and it's below the fold while generic claims are above it.

Put one line in the hero, under the subheadline:

> **Get one wrong and it comes back three days later. That's the whole idea.**

Specific, mechanical, and immediately recognisable to anyone who has lost the same three marks in four consecutive mocks.

---

## Fix 7 — Add the one-line Telegram ask at the end of every session 🟠

After the daily session completes, one line:

> **One question a day on Telegram →**

**This is the most valuable thing you can build this month and it isn't a growth feature — it's survival.** Every channel in `02` is rented and closes on 29 November. A Telegram subscriber is yours in December, and in January she has interview calls and needs guesstimates again.

Same for email: ask for it after a completed session, never before.

---

## Fix 8 — Three small things 🔵

- **Streak visible on the landing page for returning visitors.** *"You're on a 6-day streak. Today's session →"* A returning visitor should see her streak before she sees any marketing. It's the strongest retention mechanic you have.
- **Check the five-minute claim is literally true.** Time a real session with a stopwatch. If it's nine minutes, either change the product or change the number — this audience is counting minutes in their last month and will notice.
- **Test on a cheap Android phone on mobile data.** Your audience is 284,000 people across every tier of city in India, not Instagram-ad-optimised metros. If the first question takes six seconds to paint, you lose them before the hero can work.

Then **stop and change one thing at a time.** You have four weeks and no room to confuse yourself about what worked.

---

## The one test to run today

**Give your phone to someone who has never seen the site, on mobile data, and say nothing.** Watch whether they tap an answer within ten seconds.

If they do, your page works and your whole month is a traffic problem — which `02` and `06` solve. If they don't, nothing else in this kit matters, and the hero question is where you spend the day.
