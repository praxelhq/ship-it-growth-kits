# Skill Prep — Video scripts and shot lists

Scripts and shot lists only — **you record these on your phone.**

You have the easiest video job in this cohort: **a mental-maths trick is the most natively shareable 15 seconds on the internet.** It needs no founder story, no product demo, no face. It teaches something in one breath and the viewer immediately tests it on themselves, which is why this format travels.

**This is a volume channel. Make twenty of these, not two.** Vertical 9:16, captions burned in, under 20 seconds, posted daily to Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts.

---

## Format A — The trick reel (your workhorse; make 20)

**Length:** 12–18 seconds · **No face needed. No voice needed.** Hand, paper, marker.

### Template

| Time | On screen | Text overlay |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–2s | The problem, written large | **`47 × 11 = ?`** |
| 2–4s | Beat. Nothing moves. | **"3 seconds."** |
| 4–10s | Your hand writing the method, step by step | **"Split the digits → 4 _ 7"** then **"Add them → 4+7 = 11"** |
| 10–14s | The answer circled | **`517`** |
| 14–18s | Static end card | **"31 days to CAT. 5 min a day. Link in bio."** |

### The twenty tricks, in the order to post them

Start with the ones that feel like magic — those get shared. Save the duller-but-useful ones for week 3 when you have an audience.

1. **×11 two-digit** — split and add in the middle
2. **Squares ending in 5** — 65² → 6×7=42 → 4225
3. **Difference of squares** — 53² − 47² → 100 × 6 = 600
4. **×5** — halve it, add a zero
5. **×25** — divide by 4, add two zeros
6. **Percentage ↔ fraction table** — 1/6 through 1/16 (this one as a 20s carousel-style reel)
7. **×9** — ×10 then subtract the number
8. **Two-digit squares near 50** — 48² → 2304 via (50−2)²
9. **×11 three-digit** — add digits in pairs: 234 × 11 → 2574
10. **Divisibility by 7** in two seconds
11. **Successive percentage change** — 40% up then 25% down = 5% up
12. **Approximating square roots** for DI
13. **Ratio-to-percentage conversions** worth memorising
14. **Reading a DI table in the right order** (read the question first — most people don't)
15. **The guesstimate opening** — always start from population or households
16. **Compound interest approximation** for 2 years
17. **Finding the unit digit** of a large power
18. **Time-speed-distance** when two numbers are the same
19. **The "not" trap** — the single most expensive word in a question stem
20. **Averages shortcut** — deviation from an assumed mean

### Shot list for Format A

1. **Overhead shot of paper.** Phone propped on a stack of books, lens pointing straight down. Daylight from a window, not overhead room light — room light puts your hand's shadow across the page.
2. **Black marker on white unlined paper.** Thin pen is unreadable on a phone screen.
3. **Write at normal speed, then speed up 1.5–2× in editing.** Writing slowly looks like a lecture; sped-up writing is satisfying.
4. **Hold the final answer on screen for a full two seconds.** People screenshot this frame. It's your most-shared asset.
5. **No music with lyrics.** A simple beat or silence. Captions carry the whole thing.

### Rules

- **Teach it completely in the video.** Don't say "full method at the link". The ones that get shared are the ones that were useful on their own.
- **The first two seconds are the whole video.** A bare `47 × 11 = ?` stops the scroll better than any face or hook line, because it's a challenge, and this audience cannot not try it.
- **No founder face, no product shot, no logo** until the end card.
- **One trick per video.** Two is one too many.
- **Caption carries the trick in text** (see `03` #3), because this gets screenshotted more than watched.

## Format B — "The mistake that cost me 4 marks" (make 5)

**Length:** 20–30 seconds · Your face, straight to camera.

These exist to build trust that you're an aspirant, not a marketer. Post two a week.

### Script template

> **(0–4s, your face)** "I lost four marks in my last mock on a question I'd solved correctly."
>
> **(4–12s, the question on paper)** "It said 'which of these is NOT true'. I found the true one and ticked it. Thirty seconds of work, perfect method, wrong answer."
>
> **(12–22s, back to your face)** "I've now done this four times. It's not carelessness exactly — it's that I read the stem once at the start and then stopped looking at it while I was solving."
>
> **(22–30s, end card)** "Fix: circle the question word before you start. Every time. 31 days left — this is the kind of thing that's actually still fixable."

### The five mistakes

1. **The "NOT" / "EXCEPT" trap** in the stem
2. **Answering the wrong quantity** — solving for x when it asked for 2x
3. **Carrying a unit error** through DI
4. **Rounding too early** in a multi-step approximation
5. **Spending six minutes on a question you should have skipped** — the most expensive mistake in the exam and nobody films it

**Why these work:** they're confessions, which is the one register coaching marketing never uses. They also make the *product* obvious without mentioning it — a tool that brings your mistakes back is the natural conclusion of this video, and you don't have to say so.

## Format C — The 5-minute session, filmed once

**Length:** 40–55 seconds · **Where:** landing page, Peerlist, reply to "how does it work"

One honest screen recording of a real daily session.

> **(0–6s)** "This is the whole thing, five minutes, nothing cut."
> **(6–20s)** Screen recording: today's session, ten questions, mixed skills. Timer visible.
> **(20–32s)** "It mixes all five skills rather than blocking one, because the exam makes you switch and blocking makes you worse at switching."
> **(32–44s)** A wrong answer, then the method appearing. "Got that wrong. It'll come back in three days — that's the part that actually matters."
> **(44–55s)** The streak and the end card. "Free forever tier. skillprepp.space."

**Use a session where you get two wrong.** A perfect run proves nothing and looks staged.

## Format D — The countdown reel (post on 5 specific days)

Fifteen seconds, one trick, and a number that's doing the real work.

Post on **day 21 ("21 days to CAT")**, day 14, day 7, day 3, and the night before.

> **(0–3s)** Giant number: **`7 DAYS`**
> **(3–11s)** One trick, written out
> **(11–15s)** "Seven days. Speed and errors are the only things that still move. 5 min a day — link in bio."

**The 7-day and 3-day ones will be your highest-engagement posts of the month.** Panic peaks there, and a short honest "here's what's still fixable" message is genuinely kind as well as effective. Write them to be useful to someone who doesn't download anything.

## Posting schedule

| Week | What | Cadence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | Tricks 1–7 (the magic ones) + Format C once | Daily |
| **2** | Tricks 8–13 + 2× Format B | Daily |
| **3** | Tricks 14–18 + 2× Format B + Format D (day 21, day 14) | Daily |
| **4** | Tricks 19–20, re-post your best performer, Format D (day 7, day 3, night before) | Daily |

**Re-post your best reel.** Instagram shows a re-upload to a fresh audience, and with a trick video that genuinely works, the second run often beats the first.

## Production rules

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Vertical 9:16, captions burned in** | Muted autoplay is the default everywhere you post |
| **Overhead paper shots, daylight, black marker, unlined paper** | Readable on a phone; nothing else matters |
| **Under 20 seconds for Format A** | Completion rate is the whole algorithm |
| **Hold the answer frame for 2 full seconds** | It's the screenshot, and screenshots are shares |
| **One trick per video** | |
| **The trick must be complete without the link** | This is why they get shared, and why you're welcome in the comment sections |
| **Same caption structure every time** (`03` #3) | Trick in the first two lines, countdown, then the link |
| **Post daily, not perfectly** | With an audience of 284,000 and 31 days, one breakout reel is worth more than twenty good ones. You get breakouts by posting volume. |

**Make the first seven Format A reels before you post any of them.** Batch-record on day 6 — eight tricks in one sitting takes ninety minutes and then you're never scrambling for a post at 9pm, which is exactly when daily content dies.
