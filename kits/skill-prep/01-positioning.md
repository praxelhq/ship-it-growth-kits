## Start here: you have the best timing of any project in this cohort, and it expires on 29 November

**CAT 2026 is on Sunday 29 November 2026.** [2.84 lakh candidates are registered](https://www.mbauniverse.com/cat/date-and-timings). Admit cards release on 4 November.

Today is 29 October. **Your thirty-day window ends on exam day.**

You sell daily five-minute training in speed math, data interpretation, logical reasoning, guesstimates and puzzles — which is, almost exactly, the CAT Quant and DILR sections. There are **284,000 people in India right now in the final month of preparing for precisely that**, and in the last four weeks before CAT what every single one of them wants is short daily practice that keeps the mental machinery warm without burning the day.

No other project in this cohort has an audience this large, this well-defined, or this urgent. **Everything in this kit is built backwards from 29 November.**

One consequence you have to accept: **after 29 November this audience evaporates overnight.** `01` through `06` are about the sprint. `07` is about not having a dead product on 1 December — and the answer is already in your own copy, because the same five skills are what consulting interviews test, and CAT results in January turn 284,000 aspirants into roughly 50,000 people with interview calls who need GD, WAT and PI prep.

## Who your first 1,000 users actually are

**The CAT 2026 aspirant with 31 days left, who is past learning new concepts and is now trying to get faster and stop making careless mistakes.**

In priority order:

1. **The serious-but-not-elite aspirant in the final month.** She's done the syllabus, she's taking mocks, and her score is stuck because of calculation speed and silly errors rather than ignorance. **This is the overwhelming majority of 284,000 people and it's exactly what you're built for.** Your own "mistakes come back" mechanic is the single most valuable feature for her.
2. **The working professional taking CAT.** Two hours a day at most, usually late at night, needs practice that fits a commute. "Five minutes" is written for her and she's the most likely to pay.
3. **The first-time aspirant who is behind** and knows it. Wants something that tells her what to do today rather than a 400-question PDF. Your adaptive levelling — *"hit 80% and you level up; the timer tightens"* — is the honest answer to "where do I even start".
4. **Consulting-interview candidates** at B-schools, who need guesstimate and speed-math reps. **Smaller audience, but they don't disappear on 30 November**, which makes them disproportionately important to month 2.

**Who is not your user, this month:** the 99.5-percentiler. She has her own system and doesn't need you. Don't write copy for her — write for the person at 85 who wants 95, because that's where the volume and the gratitude both are.

## The one-line pitch

Use the deadline. It's real, it's shared, and it's the most motivating thing you could possibly say:

> **31 days to CAT. Five minutes a day on the two things that actually cost you marks: speed and careless mistakes.**

Generic version, after CAT:

> **Daily five-minute training in the quant, data and reasoning skills that B-schools and consulting firms test.**

For WhatsApp, where length kills:

> **5-min daily speed math + DI + LR drills. Adapts to you, brings your mistakes back. Free forever tier: skillprepp.space**

## The wedge use case

**The 47 × 11 question in your hero.**

You already have the best conversion asset in this entire cohort and I don't think you know it: a **live question in the hero** — *"Try one · Speed math 1/5 · 47 × 11 = ?"* with four tappable options and *"Tap an answer."* No signup, no email, no card.

That is the whole product in four seconds. A CAT aspirant lands, taps 517, gets it right, feels the small hit of competence, and is already using the thing.

**Protect this above everything else on your site.** Don't move it below the fold, don't gate it, don't put a modal over it. Most of this kit's conversion advice is just: send traffic to that question and get out of the way.

## Why someone picks you over the alternatives

The honest competitive set:

1. **Her coaching institute's material** (iQuanta, Cracku, TIME, CL). Free, trusted, and enormous — hundreds of questions she'll never finish. **You beat it on being five minutes instead of two hours**, and on adapting. You do not beat it on volume and shouldn't try to claim you do.
2. **A free question channel on Telegram.** She's already in four of them. They send 10 questions a day into a feed she has muted. **You beat them by tracking what she got wrong and bringing it back** — which no Telegram channel can do.
3. **Mock tests.** These are her main instrument and they are not your competitor; they're your complement. A mock is three hours and tells her *that* she's slow. You're five minutes and make her faster. **Say this explicitly** — "this is not a mock, it's the daily reps between mocks" — because it positions you alongside her existing routine instead of against it.
4. **Nothing.** Enormous share. Most aspirants in the final month do mocks and analysis and nothing in between.

## Your two real weaknesses

**1. "Pricing announced at launch."** It's on your live pricing page. To a visitor deciding whether to invest a daily habit, an unannounced price reads as *the free thing I'm building a streak on is going to be taken away from me*. Your FAQ fixes it — *"Levels 1–3 of every skill and one daily session are free forever"* — but only if she scrolls. See `04` Fix 2.

**2. You have three names.** The page says **Practical Skills**, the domain is **skillprepp.space**, and the cohort roster calls you **Skill Prep**. A student who hears one and searches another finds nothing. **Pick one today.** Given the domain and the audience, "Skill Prep" is the one to keep — but what matters is that it's one.

## What NOT to say

- **Don't say "aptitude test preparation platform".** Say "five minutes a day" and name the skills. Your own subheadline already does this well.
- **Don't claim a percentile improvement you can't evidence.** This audience has been lied to by coaching marketing for two years and is sharp about it. "Three marks from fewer careless errors" is credible; "boost your percentile by 10" is not.
- **Don't position against mocks.** Position between them.
- **Don't let the consulting angle lead this month.** It's in your badge and it should stay there — but 284,000 CAT aspirants with a 31-day deadline outrank a few thousand B-school students, and the CAT framing is what makes every channel in `02` work.
- **Don't promise a feature that arrives after 29 November.** Your audience's entire world ends that day; anything shipping in December is irrelevant to them.
