## 1. Reddit — r/CATpreparation, post #1 (week 1)

**Title:** `The five multiplication shortcuts that saved me the most time in mocks (with the 30-day drill plan I'm using)`

Post 9–11am or 8–10pm IST. Reply to every comment for four hours.

> 31 days left, so this is the stuff I wish I'd drilled in August instead of October.
>
> My quant score was stuck at the same place across four mocks and it wasn't because I didn't know the concepts. It was two things: I was slow on arithmetic I should do in my head, and I was losing 3–6 marks a mock to careless errors on questions I'd actually solved correctly.
>
> **The five shortcuts that moved it most:**
>
> **1. ×11:** split the digits and add them in the middle. 47 × 11 → 4 _ 7, middle is 4+7=11, so 517. Carry if it's over 9. Works on anything two-digit, takes a week to become automatic.
>
> **2. Squares ending in 5:** 65² → 6 × 7 = 42, stick 25 on → 4225. Every single time.
>
> **3. ×5 is ÷2 then ×10.** 86 × 5 → 43 → 430. Sounds trivial, saves two seconds a go, and you do it forty times a mock.
>
> **4. Percentage ↔ fraction table.** Know 1/6 through 1/16 as percentages cold. DI sections are built on these and most people recompute them every question.
>
> **5. Differences of squares:** 53² − 47² → (53+47)(53−47) → 100 × 6 → 600. Shows up far more than you'd expect.
>
> **The drill plan:** five minutes, every day, no exceptions, mixed across speed math / DI / LR rather than one skill at a time — blocking one skill makes you better at that skill and worse at switching, which is what the exam actually tests. And **re-do the ones you got wrong three days later**, because an error you don't revisit is one you'll make on 29 November.
>
> I ended up building a thing that does exactly this routine (link in comments, I'm the founder, so judge it accordingly). But the five shortcuts above are free and they're most of the benefit — a timer and a notebook does the rest.
>
> What's your biggest careless-error pattern? Mine was reading "not" in the question stem and answering anyway.

**First comment, from you:** `Tool I mentioned, free forever tier, no card: skillprepp.space — the hero has one question you can try without signing up.`

**Why this survives:** it is a complete, useful post with the link removed, it discloses the conflict, it tells people they don't need the product, and it ends with a question that makes the comment section work.
