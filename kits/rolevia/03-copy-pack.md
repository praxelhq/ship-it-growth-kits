## 4. Reddit — r/CATpreparation or r/developersIndia (the useful post)

**Title:** `What actually happens in a GD elimination round (and the three things that get you cut)`

> I've now sat in on about forty group discussions as a moderator, so here's what the round actually looks like, because I couldn't find this written down anywhere when I needed it.
>
> **The format.** Seven to nine candidates, one topic, usually 15–25 minutes. A moderator who mostly says nothing. Sometimes a minute of prep, often none. Eliminations straight after.
>
> **What gets you cut, in order of how often I see it:**
>
> 1. **You don't speak in the first four minutes.** By minute five the discussion has a shape and three people own it, and entering gets harder every second. This is the single most common way to fail and it has nothing to do with what you know.
> 2. **You speak a lot and add nothing.** Repeating someone else's point with more words reads worse than silence to an evaluator with a scoring sheet.
> 3. **You win an argument and lose the room.** Talking over someone successfully is marked down. It's a group discussion, and they're checking whether you can be in a group.
>
> **What works:** enter in the first ninety seconds even if it's only to frame the question. Build on a named person's point — *"adding to what Rahul said"* — because it shows you were listening, which is half of what's being scored. And if the discussion is going nowhere at minute twelve, summarise it. Whoever summarises usually gets credit for the whole thing.
>
> I built a tool for practising these with real people, because you genuinely cannot practise a GD alone (link in comments, I'm the founder, take it as you like). But the three points above are free and they're most of the benefit.

**Why this survives moderation:** it is a complete, genuinely useful post that works with the link removed. Put the link in the first comment.
