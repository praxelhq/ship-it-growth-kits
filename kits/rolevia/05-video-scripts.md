# Role.via — Video scripts and shot lists

Scripts and shot lists only — **you record these on your phone.**

You have an advantage no other project in this cluster has: **your product is nine people in a room, which is inherently filmable.** A screen recording of a nine-tile grid mid-discussion is self-explaining in two seconds. Use that.

Everything vertical (9:16), captions burned in — this audience watches muted.

**Consent, before anything else:** you cannot record a GD session with real students in it without asking every participant first. Ask at the start, get a clear yes from each, and blur or crop anyone who declines. One complaint about being filmed without permission ends your relationship with that campus.

---

## Video 1 — The hook (your most important asset)

**Length:** 20–28 seconds · **Where:** LinkedIn, Reels, WhatsApp status, the landing page hero

### Script

| Time | On screen | You say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–4s | Your face, close | "You can prepare for an interview alone." |
| 4–7s | Still your face, beat | "You cannot prepare for a group discussion alone." |
| 7–13s | **Screen recording: nine video tiles, a real discussion, two people clearly talking over each other** | "Nine people. One topic. Twenty minutes. Eliminations after." |
| 13–19s | Same grid, highlight one quiet participant | "Three people in this room didn't speak for four minutes. All three were waiting for a gap." |
| 19–24s | The scored report appearing — dimensions visible | "Afterwards you get told exactly how you came across." |
| 24–28s | Your face. URL in caption | "Free seat, Thursday 8pm. rolevia.xyz" |

### Shot list

1. **Talking head, 0–7s.** Phone propped at eye level, window light, quiet room. Audio matters more than video.
2. **The nine-tile grid, 7–19s — this is the video.** Record a real session (with consent). You want visible overlap and visible hesitation; that's the product's reason to exist. If you must stage it, stage it with nine real people having a real discussion, not actors reading.
3. **The report, 19–24s.** Zoom so the scored dimensions are readable on a phone. **Use a real middling score** — a 68 is more persuasive than a 95.
4. **Talking head close, 24–28s**, URL captioned for the last four seconds.

### Rules

- **The first seven seconds are the whole video.** That asymmetry — can prepare alone / cannot prepare alone — is the sharpest thing you have. Don't bury it behind "Hi guys, so I built...".
- **Show the overlap.** Two people talking at once is the entire argument for why an AI can't do this.
- **Caption the URL, never speak it.**
- No logo intro, no music under speech.

## Video 2 — The explainer (what actually happens in a GD)

**Length:** 55–75 seconds · **Where:** YouTube, landing page, DM to any TPO, reply to "how does it work"

This is the one that gets shared by people who aren't your users yet, because it's useful on its own.

### Script

> **(0–6s, your face)** "I've moderated about forty group discussions. Here's what gets people cut — it's not what they say."
>
> **(6–18s, grid footage, timer visible)** "Nine candidates, one topic, twenty minutes, a moderator who says nothing. Eliminations straight after."
>
> **(18–32s, grid with one participant highlighted, silent)** "Number one reason people fail: they don't speak in the first four minutes. By minute five the discussion has a shape and three people own it. Entering gets harder every second. This has nothing to do with knowing the topic."
>
> **(32–44s, cut to someone talking at length)** "Number two: speaking a lot and adding nothing. Repeating someone's point with more words scores worse than silence."
>
> **(44–56s, two people overlapping)** "Number three: winning an argument and losing the room. It's a *group* discussion. They're checking whether you can be in a group."
>
> **(56–70s, the scored report)** "What works: enter in the first ninety seconds even if it's just to frame the question. Build on a named person's point. And if it's going nowhere at minute twelve, summarise it — whoever summarises gets credit for the whole thing."
>
> **(70–75s, your face, URL captioned)** "You fix this with reps, not advice. Free seat: rolevia.xyz"

### Shot list

1. Talking-head bookends, 6s each.
2. **Four distinct clips from real sessions**, one per failure mode. The silent participant is the most valuable four seconds of footage you will ever shoot.
3. On-screen text for each numbered point — people watch this muted and screenshot the list.
4. Keep the report shot real.

**Post this one on YouTube too, with the title `What actually happens in a GD elimination round`.** It's an evergreen search query with no good video answer and it'll still be sending you users in February.

## Video 3 — For the TPO (not for students)

**Length:** 50–60 seconds · **Where:** attached to the email in `03` #1, and the LinkedIn DM

Different audience, different video. A placement officer doesn't care about your scroll-stopping hook. He cares whether this is safe, free, and less work than what he's doing now.

### Script

> **(0–8s, you, formal, seated)** "Good morning. I'm [name], a student at [campus]. This is a free group-discussion practice tool for your final-year batch — I'll show you exactly what a session looks like in under a minute."
>
> **(8–22s, the booking page with a filled schedule)** "Students take a seat in a scheduled session. Nine per room, twice a week, nothing to install — it runs in a browser on a phone."
>
> **(22–38s, grid footage)** "A moderator runs it. Twenty minutes, one topic. Topics include current affairs, technology and abstract subjects, same as a real placement round."
>
> **(38–50s, the report)** "Each student gets a scored report afterwards — communication, clarity, and whether their contribution moved the discussion. You can see which students need more practice before the real round."
>
> **(50–60s, you)** "It's free for your batch this season, and I'll moderate the first two sessions myself. rolevia.xyz, or just reply to this email."

### Rules for this one

- **Shirt, quiet room, no jump cuts, no music.** You are talking to a college administrator and the register is formal.
- **"You can see which students need more practice" is the line that sells it** — that's his problem, not the student's.
- **Say "free" twice and "nothing to install" once.** Those are his two objections.
- Under a minute. He will not watch two.

## Video 4 — Session highlights (weekly, 25–35 seconds, with consent)

Once you've run real sessions, this is your cheapest and most convincing content. One per week.

**Format:** 15 seconds of genuinely good discussion from a real session, then the participant's score, then one line from them about it.

> **(0–15s, grid)** A student making a sharp point and two others building on it.
> **(15–22s, report)** Her score.
> **(22–32s, her, if she's willing)** "I'd never been in one before. I spoke twice in the first session. This was my fourth."

**That last sentence is the best marketing you will ever have**, and it only exists if you run sessions and ask. Ask every week.

## Video 5 — Daily topic clips (8 of them, 25 seconds each)

Volume content for weeks 2–4. Read a real GD topic, give a 15-second opening, invite people to the next session.

Topics your product already uses:

1. *"Should AI development be slowed down for safety?"*
2. *"Deepfakes: how should society respond?"*
3. *"Will AI tutors replace classroom teaching?"*
4. *"Is data privacy the price we pay for AI progress?"*
5. *"India's push for domestic manufacturing"*

Plus three evergreen placement-round staples: work from home vs office, should voting be compulsory in India, is social media a net negative for teenagers.

**Structure every time:** read the topic (4s) → "here's how I'd open, and why" (15s) → "we discuss this one Thursday, 8pm" (5s).

**Why this format earns its place for you specifically:** every clip is an invitation to a *specific session at a specific time*, which is the only ask that fills rooms. Unlike every other project in this cluster, your content can't just drive signups — it has to drive attendance.

## Production rules

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Get consent from every participant before recording** | Non-negotiable. One complaint ends a campus relationship. |
| **Vertical 9:16, captions burned in** | Muted autoplay everywhere you post |
| **The nine-tile grid is your signature shot** | Nobody else in this market has it. Use it in every video. |
| **Show real scores, including mediocre ones** | A 68 proves the scoring is honest; a 95 proves nothing |
| **Every student-facing video ends with a day and a time, not a signup** | Rooms need attendance, not registrations |
| **Video 3 is formal; everything else is not** | Different audience, different register |

**Make Video 1 and Video 3 first** — the hook carries LinkedIn, and Video 3 carries the TPO outreach that `06` builds the month around. Video 2 is the evergreen asset. Videos 4 and 5 only exist once sessions are running.
