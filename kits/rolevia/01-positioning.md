## Start here: you own the one round nobody else prepares anyone for

Your own H1 is three promises:

> **"Know the company. Prepare for the role. Own the room."**

The third one is the business. **Ten other projects in this Ship It cohort are building case-interview practice. You are the only one with a group discussion product.** The GD Arena — individual GD practice *and* live sessions with real people, in groups of seven to nine with a moderator — is the thing none of them have and none of them can quickly copy, because the hard part isn't the AI, it's getting nine people into a room at the same time.

That is also the thing most likely to be ignored in favour of the AI mock interview, because the interview is easier to explain. **Don't.** Interview practice is a crowded category where you are one of a dozen. GD practice is a category of one.

## Who your first 1,000 users actually are

**The Indian student facing a GD elimination round in the next three weeks, who has never done one and knows there is no way to practise alone.**

In priority order:

1. **Candidates in company processes that use GD as an elimination round.** Your own research data names them: analyst processes with "group discussion with eliminations", assistant-manager processes with "a written elimination test, a group discussion, a technical interview". This is a genuine terror — you can prepare for an interview by talking to yourself, and you cannot prepare for a GD that way at all. **This person is your wedge and she has nowhere else to go.**
2. **Campus placement cohorts at non-IIM B-schools and engineering colleges.** GD rounds are far more common in mass-recruiter processes than in consulting processes, which means your audience is the much larger tier-2 and tier-3 campus population, not the twelve elite campuses everyone else in this cluster is fighting over.
3. **Final-year engineering students** in placement season facing mass recruiters who screen with GD. Enormous, underserved, and reachable through placement cells.
4. **MBA students preparing for company-specific interview rounds**, who come for the company research and stay for the GD. Your company profiles with collected candidate reports are genuinely useful here — and honest in a way almost nothing in this market is.

**Who is not your user:** the McKinsey-shortlist consulting aspirant. She doesn't have a GD round and ten other products want her. Leave her to them.

## The wedge use case

**"My GD round is on Saturday and I have never been in one."**

Everything flows from that sentence. She cannot simulate it with a friend, she cannot do it with ChatGPT, and watching a YouTube video of a GD teaches her nothing about being interrupted by seven people.

What she needs, in order: *what actually happens in the room* → *one practice run that doesn't count* → *honest feedback on whether she spoke enough and whether what she said mattered*.

**Make booking a GD slot the single most obvious action on your site.** Not the mock interview. Not the company search. The slot.

## Your real problem is liquidity, not signups

This is the most important paragraph in your kit.

**A live GD needs seven to nine people in the same room at the same time.** That means a thousand signups spread thinly across a month produce *no usable sessions*, while sixty signups who all show up at 8pm on Thursday produce six excellent ones.

So your growth model is not "acquire users". It is **"acquire cohorts and fill scheduled slots."** Concretely:

- **One campus at a time, not one student at a time.** A placement cell that forwards your link gives you 80 people on the same calendar, in the same timezone, with the same deadline.
- **Publish a fixed weekly schedule** — "Tuesday and Thursday, 8pm IST, every week" — rather than on-demand booking. A specific time people can plan around fills; an empty booking page with no sessions in it convinces the visitor the product is dead.
- **Seed every early session yourself.** Be the moderator. Nine strangers in a room with no host will produce one bad session and nine people who never come back.
- **Your solo GD practice is the airbag.** When a live room can't fill, individual GD practice — one topic at a time, scored — is what stops the visit being wasted. Keep it one tap from the booking page, framed as *"practising now while you wait for Thursday"*, never as the main product.

**This is why `06` front-loads campus placement cells over everything else**, and why your `07` measures *filled sessions*, not just signups.

## Why someone picks you over the alternatives

1. **Nothing else exists for GD.** This is a rare position. Your competition is a WhatsApp group of friends who agree to practise and then don't.
2. **It's real people.** An AI cannot reproduce being talked over, or the decision about when to enter a discussion — which is 80% of what a GD actually tests.
3. **Scored, consistently.** Your reports score across named dimensions (communication, clarity and the rest), so her second session is comparable to her first.
4. **Your company research is honest, and honesty is rare here.** Your own copy says *"No collected public interview reports exist for this company"* and *"inferred, not company-confirmed"* rather than inventing plausible-sounding rounds. Every competitor in this market fabricates confidence. **Make that discipline a visible selling point** — "we tell you when we don't know" is a genuine differentiator to a student who has been burned by confident nonsense.

## The two objections that decide the sale

1. **"Will there actually be anyone in the room?"** The killer. An empty-looking schedule confirms the fear. Answer it with a visible, fixed weekly calendar and a count of upcoming confirmed seats — and by never showing a booking page with zero sessions on it.
2. **"Is my CV safe?"** You currently warn, in your own words, that AI features send CV text to Google's Gemini API free tier and that *"Google may use free-tier data to improve its products, so leave out sensitive details you don't want shared."* That is admirably honest and, as written, frightening. `04` Fix 4 keeps the honesty and removes the fear — mainly by making CV upload clearly optional and letting the whole product work without it.

## What NOT to say

- **Don't lead with "AI interview practice".** That's the crowded category where you're one of twelve. Lead with GD.
- **Don't say "AI-powered interview preparation platform".** Say "practise a group discussion with real people".
- **Don't promise company-specific rounds you don't have evidence for.** Your product is already careful about this; your marketing must be too. It's your most defensible asset.
- **Don't show a booking page with no sessions.** One empty calendar costs you the user permanently.
- **Don't say "Proposed prices to test, not validated market rates" to a paying customer.** It's on your live pricing page right now. See `04` Fix 1.
