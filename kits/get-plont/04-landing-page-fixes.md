# Get Plont — Landing page fixes

Home, signup, privacy and terms were read on 29 October 2026. We did not test email delivery, plant creation or notification delivery. Do not call those flows broken from this inspection; test them with real willing adults.

## 1. Finalize the trust pages and remove contradictions

The [privacy](https://www.getplont.app/privacy) and [terms](https://www.getplont.app/terms) still say they are a working draft pending review. The terms mention keeping a password private; the actual login says one email and one code, no password. Before promoting, review the operational statements with the owner and publish an accurate current version. This kit does not provide legal text for adoption.

Replace the incorrect account instruction with the true product instruction:

> Use your own email address. Do not share sign-in codes or account access.

Keep the effective date and clear provider disclosures. Do not simply remove a draft label while leaving unresolved statements underneath.

## 2. Tell an existing plant owner what to do first

Current headline: “Life grows better with a little green.” Current main action: “Find your green.” That is warm but can sound like shopping.

Replacement:

> **Remember the next check for the plants you already own.**
>
> Add your first two plants free. Keep their conditions and care history together, with reminders to look again.
>
> **Add my first plant**
>
> Two plants free. Identification is a suggestion; confirm the match before relying on it.

Keep the calm visual direction. The plant record and next check should lead; collection browsing is secondary.

## 3. Demonstrate a real plant record

Current page offers exploration by light, care level and pet safety. Show one actual record with its last check and next check, using your own plant.

Caption:

> **A record, not a guarantee.** This is my plant and the conditions I entered. Your home may be different.

Avoid “never kill a plant again.” Do not imply a photo can establish whether ingestion is safe for a pet. The terms already state the limits; preserve them at the decision.

## 4. Show where photos and location go

The privacy page says scan photos go to an identification provider and location is optional. Put that explanation at the scan/location controls rather than inventing an offline badge:

> A photo is sent to our identification provider for a suggested match. Save it only if you want it in your plant record. You can enter a city yourself or leave location blank.

Confirm actual retention and deletion behaviour before promising it. Mask emails, home details and personal notes in analytics.

## 5. Test the email-code handoff

The checked signup URL is `/login?mode=signup`; its page says “One email, one code. No password to remember.” Ask three adults to start on mobile data, receive a code, return from email, add a plant and set a check. Test expired/wrong codes, browser return, photo denial and manual plant choice.

Recovery copy:

> Code expired? Request a new one for the same email. You do not need to create a second account.

Only promise preserved input if the implementation actually preserves it. If the essential path fails, pause invitations until fixed.

## 6. Explain free versus Premium without guessed prices

The homepage says two plants free; the terms refer to optional Premium with price at checkout. Make the free allowance visible near the CTA and the upgrade boundary visible before adding a third plant. Do not invent a Premium price.

Replacement:

> Start with two active plants and reminders free. Before an upgrade, review the current price, renewal period and tax-inclusive total. You can keep learning the routine without buying today.

Test cancellation and reminders before making a claim about their exact controls.

## 7. Verify mobile and the preview

No visual/loading audit was run here. Test 360px, enlarged text, keyboard navigation, photo upload on a slow connection and notification denial. A person must still be able to save a plant without sharing location or accepting push if that is the actual product contract.

Suggested title: “Plont — your first two plants and their next care check.” Description: “A simple plant record, care history and reminders. Two plants free.” Preview it privately before a nursery prints or forwards a QR.
