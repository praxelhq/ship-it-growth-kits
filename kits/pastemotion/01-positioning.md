# PasteMotion — Positioning

**Live:** https://www.pastemotion.dev/ · **Category:** Developer tool · **Profile:** https://shipped.money/p/pastemotion

---

## Who your first 1,000 users actually are

Not "developers". That is 30 million people and none of them are looking for you.

Your first 1,000 are **the developer who is 40 minutes from a demo and needs a loading spinner that does not look like a bootstrap default.** They are:

1. **Frontend devs at small teams and agencies** shipping client work, who have no designer and no motion budget. They solve this by Googling `css loader animation` and copying the first CodePen they find. You are a better version of that habit.
2. **Indie hackers and AI-assisted builders** shipping a SaaS landing page this weekend. Claude or Cursor wrote their app; it did not write them a gradient button that feels good. They search, they copy, they ship.
3. **Students and bootcamp grads** building portfolio projects who want their project to look expensive.
4. **React/Tailwind devs** specifically — your copy-as-React and copy-as-Tailwind output is the thing competitors do worst.

What unites them: **they are not shopping for a library. They are stuck on one specific element, right now, and they will take the first thing that works.** That is a search-intent business, not a brand business.

## The one-line pitch

> **325 production-ready web animations you can preview, tweak and paste into any codebase as HTML, CSS, React or Tailwind.**

Shorter, for comments and bios:

> **Copy-paste animations for the web. Tweak it live, copy it as React or Tailwind, ship it.**

## The wedge use case

**Loaders.** You have 90 of them — more than any other category, and it is the single highest-intent thing a developer searches for in this space. "I need a loading spinner" is a need with a deadline attached.

Everything in month one should point at loaders first, then buttons (74), then cards (46) and toggles (47). Do not market "325 animations". Market "90 loaders" and let people discover the rest. A specific number against a specific need converts; a catalogue does not.

## Why someone picks you over the alternatives

Be clear-eyed: Uiverse.io, Animista, Aceternity UI and Magic UI are all in this space and most are better known. Your three real differences, in order of strength:

1. **Multi-format copy.** HTML/CSS/JS, one all-in-one file, a React component, *or* Tailwind — with the customiser's tweaks already applied. Most competitors give you CSS and leave the React port to you. **This is your headline advantage and your site currently buries it.**
2. **Production-honest badges.** `GPU-friendly`, `No JS`, `Reduced-motion ready` on every item. No competitor labels accessibility and performance per snippet. For anyone shipping to real users, this is the difference between "fun" and "usable".
3. **The customiser.** Change colour and speed, watch it live, then copy. The alternative is paste-then-fiddle-in-devtools.

## What NOT to say

- Don't say "beautiful animations". Every competitor says it; it is unsearchable and unprovable.
- Don't lead with "325". Lead with the category the person needs.
- Don't position against Framer Motion or GSAP — they are libraries, you are snippets. Positioning against them makes you look small. Position against *copying a random CodePen*.

## The emotional truth to lean on

The feeling you are selling is **"that's done, next."** Not creativity. Relief. Your copy should sound like a colleague handing someone a working file, not like a design brand.
