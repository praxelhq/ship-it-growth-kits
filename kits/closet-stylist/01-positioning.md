# Closet Stylist — Positioning

**Live:** https://closetstylist.in/ · **Category:** Mobile app · **Profile:** https://shipped.money/p/closet-stylist

**Start with someone who repeats the same two outfits despite owning enough clothes for the week.** A student packing lightly for campus is a better first user than a professional stylist managing clients. Your first promise is one outfit from clothes already owned, not a shopping recommendation or a body assessment.

The actual headline is **“Your next favourite outfit is already in your closet.”** The page describes adding clothes, reviewing editable suggestions, finding combinations and recording wear/repeat rules. **“Start Free”** links to `/signup`. Browser rendering confirmed **“Create your wardrobe.”**, email/password and Google options, with a free limit of ten pieces. Account creation and private wardrobe operations were not completed in this research.

The public plans are **Free: ten photos, no expiry; Plus: 50 photos for ₹199/month; Max: 150 photos for ₹299/month**. Don't sell ten outfits as the free limit or assume taxes are included: the published draft says tax treatment is not verified. Saved photos upload; “analyzed on your device” does not mean the wardrobe is stored only on the phone.

## Your sentence

> Put five things you already wear into a small digital wardrobe and choose tomorrow's outfit from them.

Short bio: **More combinations from the clothes you already own.**

Use two tops, two bottoms and a layer/accessory for the first test. Photograph garments alone, check suggested categories/colours, save only what the user chooses, and inspect one actual combination. Ten photos should be enough to test value before an upgrade. Do not make a user catalogue their whole cupboard to discover whether suggestions are useful.

## Why would someone choose you?

[Whering's official site](https://whering.co.uk/) advertises a free digital wardrobe with styling, outfit planning and wear statistics; it also points to its newer site. Your pitch cannot be “the first wardrobe app” or “everyone else charges.” A phone album plus a calendar is another no-new-subscription alternative. Your difference must be a fast, editable first wardrobe and a useful repeat-history loop that a real user can maintain.

Don't promise money saved, sustainability impact or perfect taste from a suggested outfit. See whether someone actually wears a combination and returns to record it. Suggestions can be wrong; editing is part of the product, not an embarrassment to hide.

## Fix trust before recruiting photo uploads

The rendered [privacy page](https://closetstylist.in/privacy-policy) is explicitly **“Draft for owner review”** and **“not a final legal policy.”** It says support/request handling still needs operational confirmation and there is no self-service account deletion/export. This is a real trust gap. Publish an accurate owner-reviewed notice and establish a working request route before asking strangers to upload their wardrobe. Don't copy personal contact details from a draft into your marketing.

The target remains **1,000 individual accounts**, with **five reviewed saved items plus one useful outfit** as activation. Signups without a saved wardrobe don't prove people have solved the morning decision. Duplicate roster submission gets this one folder, not another count.
