## The thing that matters most, before anything else

Your install flow is currently:

> *Download a zip → unzip it to a folder you'll keep → open `chrome://extensions` → turn on Developer mode → Load unpacked → open WhatsApp Web → get a free Gemini API key from Google AI Studio → paste it into the extension → connect.*

**That is eight steps, two of which (Developer mode, API key) will stop most normal people cold.** Your product solves a mainstream consumer problem, and your installation is for developers.

Everything in this kit is built around one judgement: **until the Chrome Web Store listing is approved and the Gemini key is optional, your realistic audience is technical people only, and you should market to technical people only.** Marketing a dev-install product to a mass audience produces a lot of visits and almost no users.

**Two things to chase in parallel with all the marketing below:**
1. **Chase the Chrome Web Store approval weekly.** It is the single highest-value thing that can happen to this product.
2. **Make the Gemini key optional** — a hosted free tier with a low limit, with "bring your own key" as the power-user upgrade. The key-paste step costs you more users than any marketing can win back.

## Who your first 1,000 users actually are

**Today (dev-install reality): people who are already comfortable with `chrome://extensions`.**

1. **Indian working professionals in 10+ WhatsApp groups** — work groups, society groups, family groups, college groups — who are technical enough to side-load an extension. Developers, PMs, analysts, founders.
2. **The group admin / organiser.** The person who runs the badminton group or the society group. They feel the pain most and they tell everyone.
3. **People who've muted groups and now feel anxious about it.** This is the real emotional state: not "too many messages" but **"I muted it and now I'm scared I missed something important."** Your product is anxiety relief.

**After the Web Store listing:** the above, plus anyone in India with a busy family group — which is a very large number.

## The one-line pitch

> **Your WhatsApp groups, summarised: what was discussed, what was decided, and what you need to do.**

Shorter:

> **200 messages become a short list.** *(this is already on your page and it's the best line you have)*

## The wedge use case

**The 8pm recap.**

Not "WhatsApp summarisation" — the specific moment of opening your phone at the end of the day to 400 unread messages across six groups and having no idea which three matter. Your evening recap is the product; the hourly summaries are the mechanism.

Secondary wedge, and arguably stronger: **"Needs reply"** — messages you haven't answered, ranked by priority. That's not summarisation, that's guilt management, and people will pay for guilt management.

## Why someone picks you over the alternatives

1. **Nothing else does this.** WhatsApp has no summarisation. There are no meaningful competitors for group recaps on WhatsApp Web.
2. **The privacy model is genuinely good and genuinely explainable.** Groups only, off by default, last 30 days only, each message summarised once, your own key, stays in your browser, never reads DMs or OTPs, never sends a message. That is a better privacy posture than most funded products and **it is your strongest marketing asset, not a legal section.**
3. **Actions, not just summaries.** Plans become calendar events, reminders fire an hour before. That's the difference between interesting and useful.

## The objection you must answer first, every time

**"Will this get my WhatsApp banned?"**

It's in your FAQ. It needs to be answered in your first paragraph everywhere, because it is the first thing a user thinks and the first thing a Reddit commenter will say. Have a clear, honest, specific answer ready — and if there is genuine risk, say so plainly. This audience will forgive risk disclosed; they will not forgive risk hidden.

Same for: *"Where do my messages actually go?"* Your answer — *"messages from your selected groups are sent to Google's Gemini under your own key"* — is honest and well-phrased. **Lead with it.** Burying it looks like hiding it; stating it up front looks like confidence.

## What NOT to say

- Don't say "AI-powered WhatsApp assistant". It triggers every privacy alarm at once with no reassurance attached.
- Don't imply it reads everything. Your "off by default, groups only" design is the opposite of that and you must say so before anyone asks.
- Don't promise it works on phones. It doesn't — it's WhatsApp Web. Be upfront; a surprise here is a bad review.
- Don't call the Gemini key step "easy". It isn't. Acknowledge it, shorten it, and remove it as soon as you can.
