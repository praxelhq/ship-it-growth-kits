# booked — Video scripts

Use a synthetic brand job, no confidential agreement or client record. Film only actual supported controls. A workflow that isn't yet verified should be stated as a pilot, not staged as working software.

## A · What is due next? · 60 seconds · creator pilot call

0–2s hook: **“The deal is signed. What is due next?”** Show one synthetic deliverable. 2–15s: enter the actual supported job/date fields: “I want one record of the work, not a fictional full dashboard.” 15–30s: record a revision with the actual interface: “The creator needs to know which round and what changed.” 30–45s: update recorded payment status: “This is a status I confirm, not proof booked collected money.” 45–55s: return to the next-action view: “Can I maintain the job myself next week?” 55–60s: show current real terms and URL; if price is unpublished, say it needs confirmation before a pilot.

If a required control doesn't exist, end at that gap and fix it before promoting the video.

## B · Which version is due? · 20 seconds · creator demonstration

Hook 0–2s: **“The brand asked for a revision. Did the deadline change?”** Show a synthetic deliverable and its actual supported revision record. 2–8s: “Keep the requested change beside the round you're working on.” 8–15s: show the next due date/status if the current product supports it: “Check what you owe next before replying.” 15–20s: “One clear next action beats searching three message threads.” Don't stage a control that isn't released; use the limitation as a repair task.

## C · Owed isn't collected · 25 seconds · onboarding

Hook 0–2s: **“This payment status isn't a bank transfer.”** 2–12s: show a synthetic manual status field. 12–20s: “Record what you've actually confirmed. Don't mistake a draft or an unchecked field for money received.” 20–25s: “Keep the next action clear without inventing an integration.”

Use captions and readable zooms; don't hide a failed signup behind a landing-page animation. No paid video production is needed.
