# ORBIT — Positioning

**Live:** https://withinorbit.space/ · **Category:** Other / building-based professional introductions · **Profile:** https://shipped.money/p/orbit-2

**One building with real reciprocal matches beats scattered signups.** The [live page](https://withinorbit.space/), checked 29 October, promises three relevant people each week and 15-minute coffees downstairs. That requires a participating building, willing members and working scheduling—not merely a work-email form.

Current headline: **“Meet the three people in your building worth knowing this week.”** CTA: **“Join your building”**, reaching `/login?mode=signup`. Members are listed as **free**; operators **₹15,000/building/month**. The observed operator purchase link used Dodo's **test checkout** domain. It is not proof live billing is ready or revenue exists. The kit did not create an account, upload a resume or send an intro/email.

## First member and enabling partner

An adult in one coworking/office building with something specific to give and a small professional question to ask. The building manager must first agree to the program. Start with one complementary pair, not a citywide network or a guaranteed weekly quota when membership is too small.

**Pitch:** Meet one useful colleague already in your building, with a fair give-and-get reason to talk.

**Bio:** Less feed. One good conversation downstairs.

The wedge is reciprocal context: both people understand why the introduction helps them, then both choose a short slot. Don't describe a match suggestion as an accepted or attended coffee. “No DMs” shouldn't become unsolicited calendar/email pressure.

[LinkedIn Basic](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a545596) is a real free professional-network alternative. Existing building introductions and a calendar are another no-additional-software-cost substitute. ORBIT's difference is a bounded physical network and scheduling with consent, not a guarantee that a neighbour becomes a collaborator.

The [privacy page](https://withinorbit.space/privacy) says operators see aggregates and resumes are parsed without retaining the file. Those are product promises to verify before collecting real inputs, not independent audit results. Manual profile entry should remain an option.

The roster's **1,000 consumer signups** target stays unchanged: unique member accounts, beside **a reciprocal intro accepted and a coffee actually attended**. Operator subscriptions are a separate B2B metric, not extra consumer users.
