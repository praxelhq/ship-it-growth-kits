# CartPing — Positioning

**Live:** https://cartping.online/ · **Category:** SaaS · **Profile:** https://shipped.money/p/cartping

---

## Who your first customers actually are

Your page already says it: *"Made for small Shopify brands."* Make it narrower still.

Your first 1,000 are **the owner-operator of an Indian D2C Shopify store doing 30–300 orders a month, who personally answers the store's WhatsApp.**

Concretely:
1. **Solo founders and husband-and-wife brands** — apparel, jewellery, skincare, home goods, pet, snacks. They run Shopify, Instagram and a WhatsApp Business number, and the same person packs the orders.
2. **Stores too small for Recart / Wati / BiteSpeed.** Those start around ₹2,000–₹8,000/month and are built for teams with a marketing person. A store doing ₹1.5L/month cannot justify that and knows it.
3. **Brands whose customers already expect WhatsApp.** In India, a WhatsApp message from a store owner is normal. A recovery *email* is ignored. This is the whole reason the product works here and would not work the same way in the US.

What unites them: **they already know they're losing carts and they already recover some by hand, badly, when they remember.** You are not teaching a new behaviour. You are putting a timer and a list on a behaviour they already have. That is the easiest kind of product to sell.

## The one-line pitch

> **Get a WhatsApp alert the moment a customer abandons checkout, and win the sale back from your own number.**

Shorter, for DMs and bios:

> **Abandoned cart alerts on WhatsApp, for small Shopify stores. No app install.**

## The wedge use case

**The first 30 minutes after a cart is abandoned.**

Not "abandoned cart recovery" as a category — that's what the ₹8,000/month tools say. Your wedge is the window: a customer who left checkout 5 minutes ago still has their phone in their hand. Your alert lands in that window and gives the owner a name, a number and a cart value. Nobody has to open a dashboard.

Lead with "within minutes", always. It is the thing an automated email sequence cannot do and the thing a human owner does better than any bot.

## Why someone picks you over the alternatives

You are competing with Recart, Wati, Interakt, BiteSpeed, Zoko, Limechat and Shopify's own abandoned-checkout emails. Your three real advantages:

1. **Price and size fit.** They are built for stores with a marketing team. You are built for the person who also tapes the boxes. Say this explicitly.
2. **"No Shopify app install."** Five-minute setup, no app review, no permissions anxiety. For a non-technical owner who has heard that apps slow down their store, this removes the single biggest objection.
3. **It's the owner's own number, and a human writes the message.** Not a template blast from a brand-name sender. Recovery rates from a real person asking "hi, saw you were looking at the linen shirt, anything I can help with?" beat automated sequences — and in India, a message from an unknown business number gets ignored or blocked.

## The honest weakness, and what to do about it

**"No Shopify app install" is also your biggest distribution problem.** The Shopify App Store is where Shopify merchants look for Shopify tools. You are not in it. That means:

- Every single user has to be found and told, by you, one at a time, for now.
- **Get into the Shopify App Store anyway.** Keep the no-install setup as the "fast path", but a listing is a permanent, compounding discovery channel and nothing else in this kit replaces it. Start the submission in week 1; approval takes weeks.
- Until then, this is a **direct outreach business**, not a content business. The calendar in `06` reflects that honestly.

## The number that sells this

Use it everywhere: **roughly 70% of e-commerce carts are abandoned.** For a store doing ₹2L/month in completed orders, that is ₹4–5L walking away. Your own landing page already shows the math in the dashboard mock (₹46,200 abandoned, ₹18,450 recovered, 21.4% recovery rate). **That 21.4% is your single most persuasive asset — if it is real, put it everywhere and say whose store it is.**

## What NOT to say

- Don't say "AI-powered". You are not, and this audience is tired of it.
- Don't say "increase revenue by 30%". Unprovable, and D2C founders have heard it from twenty tools.
- Don't position against Shopify's built-in abandoned checkout emails as if they don't exist. Acknowledge them: *"Shopify already emails them. This is for the 90% who don't open the email."*
