# Milostudio — Positioning

**Live:** https://milostudio.co.in/ · **Category:** AI · **Profile:** https://shipped.money/p/milostudio

**Sell to ten businesses, not ten people playing with the meme wall.** The low-confidence roster label is resolved by the actual offer: brand kits, monthly generations and an agency tier make this a business content workflow. Start with independent cafés whose owner writes the Instagram captions between service shifts.

## The actual offer

The current headline is **“your brand, but make it post.”** The hero action is **“feed milo your vibe”**, leading toward the login flow. The [live page](https://milostudio.co.in/) offers a free tier with one brand kit, 20 generations and five AI images per month; the next tier is ₹299/month for three kits, and the agency tier ₹999/month for ten. Those are listed limits, not independently tested generation outcomes.

Your public example café has warm, dry-witty brand rules and a “never says” list. That is the useful wedge. **The café owner who can reject an off-brand caption in one glance** is easier to serve than “creators and brands everywhere.” Choose cafés that already post their real menu and events, rather than convincing silent businesses to become content creators.

## Say this

> Give your café a reusable voice, then draft the next post, caption and reel idea from the same brand rules.

Bio: **Brand-aware content drafts for independent cafés.**

The first job is not a month of automated posting. It is one real announcement—new menu item, changed hours or a workshop—turned into an accurate, approved caption. Your site describes a calendar, but this research did not verify automatic publishing. Don't promise it in outreach.

## The honest comparison

A café can keep its tone rules and content list in [Trello Free](https://trello.com/pricing), $0 for up to ten collaborators per workspace. That gives a planning board, not your brand-conditioned generation. The owner can also continue writing directly in Instagram; there is no new content-tool purchase in that approach, and it may be enough for a café posting twice a week.

You win only if the owner corrects less generic wording while retaining control over prices, menu facts and event details. “Sounds exactly like you” is a promise the owner has to judge; show a bad first draft and the correction instead of asserting perfect imitation.

## Weakness to face first

The meme wall is entertaining but can hide the business result. A visitor regenerating a fictional café's joke is not evidence they will use their own kit next week. Put one real-owner workflow ahead of the wall. Keep examples clearly labelled, don't imply the fictional brands are customers, and do not publish a business's content without its approval.

Your ten count only when real businesses put their own brand details in, use the product independently in the final seven days and have a responsible contact you can call. Three brand kits for one café group do not automatically mean three customers.
