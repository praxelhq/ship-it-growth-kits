## 8. The principle post *(blog + Reddit + HN) — your sleeper hit*

> **Why DeskPet is $2 once and will never be a subscription**
>
> Every small Mac utility I loved has become $4.99 a month.
>
> I understand why. Ongoing development costs money and a one-time purchase doesn't fund it. But I keep arriving at the same place as a user: I have eleven small apps and I will not pay $55 a month for eleven small apps, so I use fewer small apps.
>
> DeskPet is $2, once. Here's the honest accounting of why that works:
>
> - **There is no server.** Voice recognition runs on your Mac. There's no account, no sync, no database. My marginal cost per user is zero.
> - **The optional AI uses your key, not mine.** If you connect it, you pay your own provider. I'm not reselling inference.
> - **I'm not trying to fund a team.** This is one person's app. $2 × enough people pays for an Apple developer account and some coffee, and that's the business.
>
> What that means for you: if I disappear, the app keeps working, because it doesn't need me. That is worth more than a feature roadmap.
>
> What it means for me: I have to keep it small. Features that need a server don't get built. I think that's a good constraint for a cat.

Post this to r/MacApps, HN, and your blog. Principle posts outperform feature posts by a wide margin, and this one is true.
