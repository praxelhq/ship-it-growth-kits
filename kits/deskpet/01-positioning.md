# DeskPet — Positioning

**Live:** https://www.deskpet.app/ · **Category:** Productivity · **Profile:** https://shipped.money/p/deskpet

---

## Who your first 1,000 users actually are

**People who want their Mac to feel like theirs.** Not productivity optimisers — decorators.

1. **Mac aesthetic people.** They have a custom wallpaper, a dock set up just so, Raycast, maybe Bartender. They follow r/MacApps and Mac-setup accounts. **They buy $2 apps on impulse and they tell people.** This is your core and it is a well-defined, findable crowd.
2. **Students and WFH people who are lonely at a desk.** The pet is company. This is the real emotional driver and nobody in this category says it out loud.
3. **Cat people.** Genuinely a segment. A large one. People who will download a thing because it is a cat.
4. **Privacy-minded Mac users** who are tired of every small app being a subscription with a cloud account. Your "on-device voice, works offline, AI off until you connect it, $2 once, no subscription" is a complete anti-SaaS position and there is a real audience hungry for it.

What unites them: **they will decide in under five seconds, from one screenshot or one GIF.** That is unusual and it shapes the whole plan — your growth is a visual-media problem, not a copy problem.

## The one-line pitch

> **A tiny cat that lives on your Mac, tracks your water and focus timers, and talks back. $2 once, no subscription.**

Shorter:

> **A tiny cat that lives on your Mac. $2, once.**

## The wedge use case

**The cat itself.** Not hydration, not focus timers, not tasks — those are what makes someone keep it. The cat is what makes someone install it.

Your current page leads with *"A tiny cat that helps you stay on track. Drink water. Find your focus. Finish your tasks."* The first six words are right and the next nine dilute them. A productivity app that happens to have a cat competes with a hundred productivity apps. A cat that happens to track water competes with nothing.

**Lead with the cat everywhere. Let the features be the pleasant surprise.**

## Why someone picks you over the alternatives

There are desktop-pet apps (and one of them, *Kuchu Puchu*, is in your own cohort — worth being aware of). Your actual differentiators:

1. **$2 once.** In a market where everything is $4.99/month, "two dollars, forever, no subscription" is almost a political statement. It removes the decision entirely — nobody comparison-shops at $2.
2. **It does things.** Most desktop pets are decoration. Yours logs water by voice, runs focus timers, holds tasks and reminders, and gives you an end-of-day wrap. That's the retention story and it's what justifies the $2.
3. **On-device voice, works offline, AI optional and off by default.** Talk to your Mac without anything leaving it. This is a genuinely strong privacy story for a 2026 audience that has learned to assume the worst.
4. **Native macOS, Apple silicon.** Mac people care about this and can tell.

## The honest friction, and what to do about it

Your page says: *"First time opening? Right-click DeskPet.app and choose Open."*

That's the Gatekeeper warning for an unsigned/un-notarised app, and for a non-technical Mac user it reads as **"this might be malware"**. For a $2 impulse purchase, that scare screen is where a meaningful share of your installs die, silently.

- **Get an Apple Developer account ($99/year) and notarise the app.** It is the single highest-return thing you can do for conversion and it removes a trust problem no amount of marketing fixes. Yes, it's a cost against a zero budget — frame it to yourself as the cost of 50 sales.
- **Until then:** put the right-click instruction *with a screenshot* directly on the download button, not in the fine print. Explain in one friendly line *why* it happens. Users forgive a known quirk; they don't forgive a surprise.
- Also: **Apple silicon only, macOS 14+.** Say it before the download, not after. An Intel Mac user who downloads and fails will not come back.

## What NOT to say

- Don't call it a "productivity app". You will be compared to Things, Todoist and Raycast and you'll lose. You're a desktop companion.
- Don't say "AI-powered". Your AI is optional and off by default — which is a *better* story. Tell that one.
- Don't apologise for the price. "$2 once" is the headline, not the footnote.
- Don't over-explain the features list up front. Show the cat. The rest is discovered.
