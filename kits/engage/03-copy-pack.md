# Engage — Copy pack

## 1. Tutor opener

Hi — when you finish a short explanation in your online cohort, how do you check whether to move on or explain it another way?

I build Engage. It adds quick questions, reactions and recall checks beside a meeting. I would like to test one harmless question with a tutor running a real paid adult cohort.

It measures actions inside Engage, not whether someone is listening. Would a two-device sample be useful before deciding whether to use it in a lesson?

## 2. Trial email

**Subject: One recall question before the next slide**

For your next online lesson, choose one point you want to check. We can test a question, the participant response and the host view before using it with the group.

Engage's public demo is here: https://engage.neuronlab.in/try

Please explain the visibility boundary first. The host sees participant names and activity timing; poll results are grouped, and individual answers are not shown beside names. Questions are named to the host and room. Quiet in Engage does not prove someone was not paying attention.

The current public page did not publish a price or allowance. Check the actual offer before committing to a live session. I will not ask for a learner list or private lesson recording. If this does not fit, say no and I will stop.

## 3. LinkedIn post

A silent room can mean many things. A student might be thinking, listening, presenting or struggling with the connection.

Engage cannot read attention. It can show actions people choose to take inside Engage and help a host ask a quick check before continuing.

The workflow I am testing is small: explain one point, ask one recall question, inspect the group result and decide whether another explanation would help.

Participants should know what the host can see before joining. Demo and plain data summary: https://engage.neuronlab.in/

If you run paid adult online cohorts, where does a quick check fit naturally in your lesson?

## 4. Organizer introduction ask

Do you know an independent tutor running an adult paid online skills cohort? Could you ask whether a sample recall-check demonstration would help? This is a teacher-business workflow, not a student-attention test. Please do not share learner names or add me to a class group without agreement.

## 5. Participant disclosure for an agreed pilot

We are trying Engage for one lesson question. The host can see your entered name, whether you respond and activity timing. Your poll answer is not shown beside your name; questions you submit are named to the room. Engage does not collect meeting audio, video or screen content. Not responding is not treated as proof you were not listening.

You can read the data summary before deciding whether to join: https://engage.neuronlab.in/privacy.html

The tutor should offer the ordinary lesson/question route if someone does not want to join the tool. Do not turn participation into a public ranking.

## 6. Consented tutor follow-up

Did the group result change what you explained next? Could participants understand the visibility disclosure? What failed in the actual flow: joining, launching, answering, host display or deletion? A description is enough; please do not send learner names, answer histories or meeting screenshots in public.

No fabricated testimonials, learning gains, Google endorsement or claim that silence reveals attention. Repeated posts should use a different genuine question-design lesson.
