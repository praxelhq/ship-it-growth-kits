## 3. Reddit — r/ProductManagement (comment, not a post)

**Do not make a top-level promotional post here. It will be removed.** Search `India` or `interview` sorted by new, every day, and answer real questions. When someone asks what to practise with:

> For PM interviews specifically, the thing that helped me most was separating the two failure modes. Most people practise *content* — frameworks, metrics definitions, product teardowns. But in the actual round you usually fail on *process*: you don't clarify the question, so you answer the wrong one, or you never state a structure, so the interviewer can't follow you.
>
> So practise under a clock, in four fixed phases: clarify, structure, analyse, recommend. Even badly. The clock is what exposes it.
>
> I built a tool that forces exactly those four phases and scores you on them (caseforge.in — free case per function, I'm the founder so take that as you like). But honestly even doing it on paper with a timer is 80% of the benefit. The structure is the thing, not the tool.

**Why this survives:** it is genuinely useful without the link, it discloses the conflict, and it tells the reader they don't need your product. That last part is why it doesn't get removed — and, counterintuitively, why people click.

## 10. Reply templates you'll need all month

**"Is this just ChatGPT?"**

> Partly — the scoring uses a model, same as anything in this space would. What it adds: a fixed clock and four fixed phases you can't skip, the same rubric every attempt so your score last week and this week mean the same thing, and model answers written for Indian placement rounds specifically. If you're disciplined enough to time yourself and hold a consistent rubric in your head, you genuinely don't need it.

**"Why ₹499?"**

> Free tier is one case per function and it's not a trial — it stays free. ₹499/month or ₹3,999/year if you want the full library and progress tracking. I priced it at roughly what a batch-mate spends on coffee during shortlist week, because this audience is students.

**"Are the cases real interview questions?"**

> They're modelled on interview reports, not transcribed from real rounds — I'd rather say that plainly than claim otherwise. The round *structure* is accurate; the specific prompts are written.

**"Does it cover consulting?"**

> No, deliberately. Ten other tools do that well. I built the four functions nobody built for.
