# CaseForge — Positioning

**Live:** https://caseforge.in/ · **Category:** Education · **Audience:** B2C → **1,000 signups in 30 days** ([`TARGET-POLICY.md`](../../TARGET-POLICY.md)) · **Profile:** https://shipped.money/p/caseforge

## You already have the best positioning in your cohort. Don't dilute it.

Your own headline is the strategy:

> **"Case practice for the interviews nobody built a tool for."**
> *"Consulting candidates get timed simulators and scored feedback. Everyone else gets a blog post."*

That second line is the sharpest sentence written by any project in this cluster. **Ten other Ship It projects are selling case practice to consulting candidates.** You are the only one selling it to the product manager, the HR candidate, the sales and marketing applicant — the 70% of an MBA batch that is *not* going into consulting and has been handed nothing but blog posts and a Google Doc of "frameworks".

Everything below is about not losing that.

## Who your first 1,000 users actually are

**The MBA or PGP first-year with a product, marketing, HR or sales shortlist in the next three weeks, who has realised the consulting prep material everyone shared doesn't fit her interview.**

Specifically, in priority order:

1. **Product-management aspirants at Indian B-schools.** The biggest and loudest of your four functions. They have a guesstimate round, a product-design round and a metrics round, and the consulting case library doesn't cover any of it. They are also the most online of the four — there are real PM communities you can reach.
2. **Marketing and sales track candidates** heading for FMCG, D2C and tech sales roles. Large cohorts at every campus, almost zero dedicated prep material, and a culture of sharing resources inside the batch.
3. **HR track candidates** (XLRI, TISS, MDI, SCMHRD and the HR specialisations everywhere else). The most underserved audience in Indian placement prep by a wide margin. **Nobody is building for them.** Smallest in absolute numbers, highest gratitude, most likely to tell their whole batch.
4. **Lateral candidates with 2–6 years of experience** interviewing for PM and marketing roles outside the campus process. Fewer in number, more likely to pay ₹499 without thinking about it, and they find you through search rather than campus.

**Who is not your user:** the consulting aspirant. Ten other products are fighting over her. Every hour you spend chasing her is an hour spent as the eleventh-best option instead of the only option.

## The one-line pitch

> **Timed, scored case practice for product, marketing, sales and HR interviews — the ones consulting prep doesn't cover.**

For a WhatsApp group, where length kills:

> **Case prep for PM/marketing/HR interviews, not consulting. One free case per function: caseforge.in**

For LinkedIn, where the audience is performing ambition:

> **Everyone preparing for consulting has a timed simulator. Everyone preparing for product, marketing, sales or HR has a blog post. I built the simulator for the other 70% of the batch.**

## The wedge use case

**The product-management guesstimate round, four days out.**

One function, one round type, one deadline. A student with a PM shortlist on Friday, who has done two consulting cases and can feel that they are not the same thing, lands on your page on Monday and runs a free PM case in twenty minutes. She gets a score out of 100 across five dimensions and a model answer she can compare herself against.

That is a complete, satisfying, shareable experience inside one sitting — and it is the thing she screenshots into her batch group. **Make the free PM case the best twenty minutes in the product and treat everything else as downstream of it.**

Do not lead with "four functions, timed phases, five scoring dimensions". Lead with *her* round, on Friday.

## Why someone picks you over the alternatives

The honest competitive set, in the order a student actually encounters it:

1. **A senior's Google Drive folder of PDFs.** Free, trusted, and completely passive. You beat it by being timed and scored — it cannot tell her whether her answer was any good, and that is the only question she has.
2. **A consulting case tool** (ten of them in this cohort alone). She beats herself up trying to force a profitability framework onto a product-design question. **You beat it by being the right shape**, and your headline already says so.
3. **ChatGPT.** Free and genuinely capable. This is your real competitor and the kit will not pretend otherwise. You beat it on three things, and only these three: **a timed structure she can't cheat** (Clarify → Structure → Analyse → Recommend), **a consistent rubric across attempts** so her score last week and this week mean the same thing, and **a model answer written for Indian placement interviews** rather than generic advice. Say that plainly. A student who has tried ChatGPT and found it mushy is your easiest convert — and she is most of the market.
4. **Nothing.** The most common option. Most non-consulting candidates walk into the round having done no mock at all.

## The proof problem — your biggest weakness

**Your page has no testimonial, no user count, no named user, no screenshot of a real score.** You are a product whose entire value claim is "our scoring tells you something true about your thinking", and there is nothing on the page that suggests anyone has ever found that to be the case.

For this audience the first objection is never price. It is: *"is this AI score actually any good, or is it going to tell me I'm great and then I'll bomb the round?"*

You cannot answer that with copy. You answer it with **three named students who used it before a real round, and what happened.** `04` makes that a week-1 task and `06` puts it on days 2–6. Nothing else on your page is worth as much.

## What NOT to say

- **Don't say "AI-powered case interview platform".** Every one of the ten others says a version of this. Your differentiation is the *function*, not the AI.
- **Don't broaden to "all interviews".** The moment you become a general interview tool you lose the only sentence that makes you obviously different. Four functions, named, is the asset.
- **Don't quietly start chasing consulting candidates** because the communities are bigger. That's the single most likely way this month fails.
- **Don't lead with ₹499.** Lead with the free case per function. The price is a detail after she has seen one score.
- **Don't claim placement outcomes you can't evidence.** "Cracked BCG with CaseForge" without a real named person is the fastest way to lose this audience, who are sharper about this than most.
