## The first institution

The currently supported hospital's authorized administrator, with a designated document-preparation/review team. The first product task is a synthetic administrative workflow evaluation, not recruiting patients, marketing clinical outcomes or inviting strangers into the hospital's workspace.

**Pitch: Let the authorized team inspect a hospital-document drafting workflow using a clearly fictional example.**

Short version: **Draft, review and approve only what the current release actually supports.**

Those verbs come from metadata, not a verified feature inventory. Before using them as a promise, the administrator must establish which document types, languages, voice steps, review roles and approval actions work today. Do not invent prescriptions, discharge summaries, diagnosis support, digital signatures, integrations or automatic clinical approval.

## The target conflict to face

[Policy](../../TARGET-POLICY.md) requires **10 real business customers**, not ten staff accounts. The public product is branded for one hospital and approval-gated. Ten approved staff at the same hospital are one business at most. No evidence establishes independent hospital tenancy, isolation, procurement terms or permission to onboard outside institutions.

Keep the target at ten and report the actual limitation. A single-institution pilot may be useful and still fall short by nine. Do not invite external hospitals to this existing workspace or rename departments as separate customers. First obtain a real supported external-institution offer and isolated onboarding; then evaluate expansion.

## The manual alternative

The hospital's currently approved document preparation and human review process is the practical alternative. Its exact tools and cost were not accessed, so no comparison price or claim of inefficiency is invented. A synthetic checklist can compare whether a draft preserves the supplied facts and whether the right reviewer controls the next step. The product must earn trust through a verified workflow, not a medical accuracy claim.

Prove access approval and role boundaries with authorized test accounts, a wholly fictional record and a named internal reviewer. Establish what is stored, processed, exported and deleted before actual patient data. A metadata tagline and sign-in screen are insufficient evidence for clinical deployment or an external sales campaign.
