## Your first person

A college student who keeps hearing a company name in class or before an interview and cannot explain what it actually sells. Start with one familiar company and a simple teach-back, not an investor looking for stock tips.

Do not broaden this to everyone who might someday enjoy the category. Find twenty people with this exact situation, watch five use the product, then repeat the invitation that produced an unprompted return.

## The pitch

> Get a first explanation of what a company does, then check what you understood.

For a bio or a short reply:

> Five company cards, a short note and a quiz. Education only.

## Your opening use case

Ask the student to explain the company in one sentence before the sample and again after it. The change you care about is a clearer explanation, not a stock opinion. Keep the same source date and correct a factual error before turning a card into a public clip.

## The real alternative

[Finshots](https://finshots.in/) offers publicly readable business explainers; no comparable paid plan was verified here. [Notion Free](https://www.notion.com/pricing) is $0 for a personal company-notes page. Anant’s narrower proposition is a structured first pass plus an active recall step. Do not say public company information is unavailable elsewhere or claim better investment decisions.

## What you need to prove

Link each current company claim to the relevant official company source inside the product and show its checked date. A five-person teach-back can reveal confusing language, but it is not proof every company can be understood in exactly sixty seconds. Keep the education-only boundary in the sample and invitation.

**Your board target stays 1,000 signups in 30 days.** Your product-specific counting rule is in `07`. The channel forecast in `02` is a planning scenario, not evidence that you will reach it.
