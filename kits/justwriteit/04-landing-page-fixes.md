# JustWriteIt — Landing-page fixes

Public homepage/privacy/terms read 29 October 2026. Welcome/signup/login destinations are exposed; no account, LinkedIn connection, extension, live post or payment was tested. These fixes concern actual public contradictions, not a claim the app is down.

## 1. Resolve the draft legal pages and credential contradiction

**Current:** privacy and terms begin “Draft text.” Privacy describes optional saved cloud-login credentials, then says “We never see or store your LinkedIn password.”

Publish owner-approved accurate pages and functioning support commitments before account recruitment. **Replacement after verifying modes:** **“Read exactly what each mode accesses and stores before connecting it. Manual drafting does not require enabling the cloud assistant.”** Use that last sentence only if the actual flow supports it. Clearly explain each mode's data, controls and deletion; do not hide the contradiction behind a general encryption badge. Don't invite passwords or authenticator secrets through a growth test.

## 2. Separate drafting from automated relationship activity

**Current headline:** “You do what you do. We post and engage on your behalf.” The FAQ admits LinkedIn forbids automated comments/requests and may restrict accounts.

**Write for the launch wedge:** **“Turn your rough idea into a post you review.”** Subtext: **“Supply the facts. Answer what is missing. Edit before you choose to publish.”** Show the current manual mode first. If automated features remain offered, keep the actual account-risk disclosure at their enablement, without implying small limits or daytime use make them authorized.

## 3. Make approval and autopilot different choices

**Current:** “You approve every post” conflicts with full autopilot sending after a selected 6/12/24-hour stop window.

**Write mode labels:** **“Manual: publish only after my explicit approval”** and **“Autopilot: may publish after my selected stop window.”** Explain actual defaults and test them. For this launch, manual mode stays selected and no draft should publish from silence. Don't promote the explicit-approval promise while quietly turning on a timer.

## 4. Put credit consumption before starting

**Current:** two free posts/comments/requests, then $6 for six of each; post credits are spent on start.

**Write beside the action:** **“Start this draft: uses 1 post credit now, before publication.”** Show actual remaining balance and make failure handling clear from implementation. Preserve **“$6 once. No subscription. Credits don't expire.”** Confirm final tax/checkout total and real refund terms; don't invent a per-published-post price.

## 5. Replace absolute factuality with user review

**Current:** “It never invents a story or a number.”

**Write:** **“We ask for missing facts. Review every claim before publishing; AI can still make mistakes.”** Show a labelled input → clarifying question → edited draft example using actual supported behaviour. A synthetic example is not a customer result. Never present stock writing-style checks as proof the facts are true.

## 6. Explain mobile and data scope before setup

**Current:** mobile supports idea capture/dictation; assistant/side panel need desktop Chrome. Privacy describes profile/post text sent to providers, while analytics exports are parsed in-browser with numbers saved.

**Write:** **“Capture an idea on your phone. Check which drafting and publishing controls are available before connecting LinkedIn. Desktop assistant is a separate optional mode.”** Make profile/content processing distinct from analytics-file parsing; not all data stays in-browser. Test fresh signup, declined analytics, token disconnection and export/delete using authorized test data. Proposed preview: **“JustWriteIt — your rough note into a reviewed post.”** No mobile defect or load score was measured.
