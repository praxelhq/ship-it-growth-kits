## 3. LinkedIn post

A score can make a guess look like a fact.

For account prioritization, I want to see what produced the number: an observed need, a source, a missing field, a mismatch and the next question to ask.

That is what I am testing in FiguredIt. The first output is an ICP hypothesis. Real outreach, deal outcomes and retention still have to challenge it.

The landing-page accounts are fictional examples, not a list of verified buyers. Start with the public demo before uploading a permitted company list: https://figuredit.in/demo

What is the most useful reason you have ever removed an apparently good-fit account from your pipeline?

## 4. Introduction ask

Do you know a small B2B consultancy reviewing a company list for one specific service? Please ask whether they want to inspect FiguredIt's evidence breakdown. Do not send me their contacts, CRM or client documents without permission. The first demonstration can use fictional companies.

## 5. Private ranking-review invitation

Would you choose five accounts from your own permitted list and review the order with me? We can separate observed facts, model inferences and unanswered questions, then record any disqualifier that changes the order. You decide what, if anything, to contact. A high score does not require an outreach message.

## 6. Consented follow-up

Which ranking did you change after checking the evidence? Did a real conversation support or contradict the initial hypothesis? Please use aggregate outcomes, not personal contact details or private transcripts. Did you return independently to review your list again?

No invented customer wins, conversion lifts, company revenues or verified buyer roles. Use actual source dates in any public demonstration and fictional contact fields only.
