# GOAT Cards by streak — Landing-page fixes

## 1. Keep the playable promise clear

Actual Chrome headline: **“CALL THE STAT. FIRST TO FIVE.”** Actual CTA: **“DEAL ME IN”**. One real click dealt a hand; selecting a stat returned a comparison and next-card prompt. Retain that low-friction entry. Test the rest of a match and phone layout before expanding traffic; the first flip alone is not a full-game audit.

Supporting line: **“A solo football trump-card duel. Pick a stat on your top card; first to five rounds wins.”** Keep the existing ten-card/35-card explanation available without making it the whole first screen.

## 2. Put the honest labels above the fold

Current rules/credits contain **“Ratings are game numbers for fun, not official stats.”** and the no-endorsement notice. Repeat a concise version beside the main action: **“Unofficial fan game. Game ratings, not official stats.”** It should stay visible in mobile screenshots, not only after expanding credits.

## 3. Explain the turn and tie

Use **“Tap a stat on your card”** before a call, **“Higher number wins both cards”** after comparison, and **“Tie: cards go into the pot. The next winner collects it.”** where applicable. The next-card prompt should be clearly tappable after its transition, not appear permanently disabled to a new player. Test actual timing rather than guessing from one accessibility snapshot.

## 4. Make sound optional and credits durable

Music, crowd and effects toggles are visible. Test mute persistence and offer quiet entry; do not require sound to understand a win. Preserve the game's image/music/font credits and licence links. Those licences are not player endorsements, and public images are not automatically unrestricted advertising assets.

## 5. Mobile and preview

The delivered title is GOAT Cards by streak and its description explains the duel. Keep a clear browser title and unofficial label in the preview. Test small-screen card text, stat hit areas, next-turn feedback, restart and completed-match state. Existing Clarity is in delivered HTML; keep any identifying input out of replay if accounts are later introduced. No fake win-rate proof or user quote is needed.
