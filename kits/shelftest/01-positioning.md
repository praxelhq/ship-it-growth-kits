## The first business

A first-time D2C operator with an actual developing consumer-product business, owner-controlled product assumptions and a near-term decision about a first small launch. Start before large inventory commitments, but distinguish genuine business work from a student trying a fictional idea. The owner needs a clearer next research question, not a model's permission to manufacture.

**Pitch: Pressure-test your own product assumptions and choose the next question to ask real buyers.**

Short version: **A structured first critique, followed by real evidence.**

The public promise is a short quiz, verdict, one first fix and a stronger plan. The actual quiz dimensions/output were not observed, so do not invent a profitability calculator, buyer panel, live market scan or expert review. Treat any verdict as a hypothesis until the owner checks it against actual costs, sources and buyer conversations.

## The headline overpromises the proof

A founder answering a quiz does not establish that buyers will purchase. Even an attractive score cannot replace a real offer and fulfilled order. Shelf Test should help the founder expose assumptions and prioritize a test, not claim demand validation or guaranteed savings before spending.

No public price, trial, paid feature or account limit was found. A Google signup CTA does not mean unlimited free reports or production access for every unfamiliar account. Verify the development-mode access boundary before a broad event. The page says tests/reports stay private to the account; authentication, persistence, isolation and deletion remain untested.

## The alternative

A free [Google Sheets](https://www.google.com/sheets/about/) assumptions log can hold buyer, problem, offer, price/cost assumption, supporting evidence and next question. A founder can ask actual intended buyers about recent purchases and later test a small honest offer it can fulfil. Shelf Test must earn adoption by making the next learning step clearer, not by pretending a quiz is direct customer research.

Prove a permitted fictional quiz, real output, exact account save/return and limitations. Then let an authorized business owner choose actual non-confidential product data. If access is still a restricted development pilot, label it and record its capacity instead of claiming a public launch is ready.

**Target: 10 real business customers in 30 days**, under [policy](../../TARGET-POLICY.md). Each must be a genuine business, onboard its actual permitted product data, use independently within seven days and have a privately known owner name/phone. Student ideas, sample report readers and workshop attendance are not business customers. Pre-product discovery can be useful without qualifying; report it separately.
