# DancePass — Positioning

**Live:** https://dancepass.in/ · **Category:** Marketplace · **Profile:** https://shipped.money/p/dancepass

**Your first ten are active class suppliers, not dancers and not directory entries.** The page has a large studio directory, but it explicitly says those entries are public, unclaimed and not evidence of a partnership or current class availability. Treat that honesty as the starting point.

The actual headline is **“Your next move starts here.”** The main action is **“Find classes”**, while **“Teach on DancePass”** goes to `/auth`. The public offer is finding a workshop, booking a spot and revisiting instructor material. No platform fee or supplier subscription was established on the pages read. Say **“confirm the current fee and settlement terms before listing”**, not “free” or “zero commission.”

## The first supplier

Pick one independent workshop instructor in one city and one style, with a real class date within the next fortnight and permission to sell those places. Start in the city where you can attend a class yourself. Don't open Delhi, Mumbai and Bengaluru simultaneously just because the homepage filter lists them.

> Give dancers one accurate page to find and book your next workshop, with the class details you approve.

Bio: **Your next real workshop, clearly listed and bookable.**

The first job is a single genuine class: style, beginner suitability, address, date, available places, price, cancellation policy and instructor confirmation. A beautiful searchable directory with no confirmed inventory cannot deliver that job.

## Why the instructor would switch

Their current Instagram announcement plus direct message may already fill the class. [Google Business Profile](https://business.google.com/) is a free public-presence alternative for eligible studios, but isn't this workshop-specific booking flow. Your advantage must be current class information and a working booking handoff—not a claim that nobody can discover studios elsewhere.

The homepage's rendered “Loading classes…” is a research limitation, not proof the live database is empty. Test a current class on a fresh phone and confirm the owner receives the booking. Do not call the product broken from a partial fetch. Conversely, never convert the directory's 150 listed studios into “150 partners.”

## Keep the business target honest

An onboarded supplier enters their own class information, independently manages a real workshop within the final seven days, and has an authorized responsible contact you can call. Ten studios listed without their participation count as zero customers under that rule. If the present teacher flow cannot yet support independent management, say the ten-business target is gated on that release; don't relabel dancer accounts as supply businesses.
