# DancePass — Measurement

**Target: 10 active supply-side businesses.** Report **instructors/studios independently managing their own confirmed workshop** beside the headline. Student registrations and bookings matter, but are different metrics.

## Day 0

Count real approved suppliers, current bookable classes, controlled versus real bookings, cancellations and final-week supplier activity from your own records. The [directory](https://dancepass.in/studios) displayed 150 public listings when read, explicitly unclaimed and not partnerships. That number does not establish any onboarded supplier count. Record actual [Clarity](https://clarity.microsoft.com/) sessions and current [course profile](https://shipped.money/p/dancepass) rank/reading/timestamp; if unknown, collect it rather than entering zero.

Use `teacher_onboarding_started`, `supplier_verified`, `class_approved`, `booking_completed`, `booking_received_by_supplier`, `class_cancelled` and `supplier_returned`. Distinguish a button click from a completed booking and test data from real classes. Don't store student identities or class recordings in public analytics.

## Four customer tests

Under the [policy](../../TARGET-POLICY.md), the supplier is a real teaching business, its actual class data is onboarded, it used the product in the final seven days without your prompt, and you know an authorized responsible person with a callable contact privately. A public directory entry does not pass. Two instructors working for the same studio are not automatically two businesses. Paid is not required; actual platform fee terms must still be clear before any transaction.

If the present teacher flow cannot support independent class management, the active-business target is **gated on that capability**. Don't rename dancer sessions or manually copied directory records to meet it.

## Forecast and checkpoints

40 approaches × assumed 25% response × 60% real-class trial × 50% independent continued use ≈ 3 suppliers. Five warm contacts may add 0–1. **The base 3–4 is below ten.** These aren't measured dance-marketplace rates. After the first 20 approaches, use actual owner objections and confirmed-date supply to decide whether more relevant introductions can close the gap.

Watch five sessions that see a class but don't book: missing level, unclear address and surprise fees are different failures. A healthy month has a small set of real classes dancers can actually attend, not a large unclaimed-directory screenshot.
