# Journaleaf — Positioning

**Live:** https://journal-leaf.com/ · **Category:** Health and fitness · **Profile:** https://shipped.money/p/journaleaf

Your first user wants a one-minute kind pause, not a full diary habit or a promise that positive words will fix a hard day. **The flower is a record of showing up, not proof of better mental health.**

Current headline: “Type a kind sentence. Grow a flower.” The page offers six feelings, a sentence typed letter by letter, and one flower for a day of showing up. Missing a day does not wilt the garden. This is the distinctive loop: choose, type, finish, see something grow. No personal entry or completed session was submitted during research.

**Pitch:** Type one kind sentence at your own pace and grow a small garden of days you showed up.

**Bio:** One sentence. One minute. A flower that doesn't punish a missed day.

Start with an adult student who abandons long journal prompts but is willing to try one sentence before closing the laptop. Your wedge is a **first complete typing session with no pressure to reveal its content**. The journal and archive are supporting features, not the first thing to explain.

The [pricing page](https://journal-leaf.com/pricing), checked 29 October 2026, displays a 3-day free trial, then $4.99/month, $29/year or $39 lifetime at the founding price until 1 January 2027, then $59. It says Indian buyers pay local rupee prices, which were not exposed in this view; do not convert or invent an INR quote. No automatic charge at trial end; existing entries stay readable, but adding requires a plan.

[Day One](https://dayoneapp.com/pricing/) has a $0 Basic plan and paid Silver at $49.99/year and Gold at $74.99/year in the checked official view. You cannot claim every journal is expensive. Your distinction is the small tactile sentence-to-flower ritual and forgiving garden, not having more features or stronger privacy than established alternatives.

The [privacy explanation](https://journal-leaf.com/privacy) makes an important distinction: before sign-in, content stays in the local browser; after sign-in, it syncs through account storage and is **not end-to-end encrypted**. Do not replace that honest explanation with “nobody can ever access it.” Shared-device visibility and clearing browser data matter to the user.

Keep B2C **1,000 signups**, with a transparent no-account session interpretation if that is the first live experience. Report completed flowers and later-day returns beside it. A trial is not a paying subscriber.
