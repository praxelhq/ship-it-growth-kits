# Afterthoughts — Measurement

**B2C target: 1,000 new verified consumer accounts in 30 days.** Report **first recorded and retrievable entries** alongside, then **voluntary use on another night**. A public browser-demo click isn't an account, and an invented sample isn't a customer diary.

Activation is one real user making an entry and finding its source again. Measure that with content-free events or voluntary usability responses, not by listening to private recordings. Source-grounded recall is a separate quality test; fluent invented memories aren't successful answers.

## Arithmetic

At an assumed 20% visitor-to-account rate, 1,000 accounts needs 5,000 qualified visits. If 60% make/retrieve a first entry, that's 600 activations. These are hypotheses, not benchmarks. Twenty warm invitations,six posts,one conditional session and directory discovery form a 15–35 first-entry experiment, not evidence of thousands of visitors. Keep repeat entries separate from new users.

## Weekly decisions

|Day|Decision|
|---|---|
|7|Three fresh users must understand demo/account data differences and retrieve a synthetic entry. Privacy confusion or recording-control failure: fix before wider invitations.|
|14|Fewer than five of ten willing testers retrieve an entry: observe capture/transcript/search friction. Don't replace that loop with a prettier orb.|
|21|Who returns without your nudge? What source helped them find a memory? No repeat value: narrow the retrieval job instead of adding clinical-sounding patterns.|
|30|Report accounts, first retrievable entries, repeat use, grounded recall errors, hours and target gap. No therapeutic or relationship outcome claimed.|

These are experimental decisions, not mental-health or journaling standards. Correct unsupported recall and keep descriptive patterns from becoming judgments. An unshared private entry can be a successful use; public sharing isn't the activation requirement.
