# Afterthoughts — Copy pack

Public examples are invented and labelled. Don't ask for diary audio, publish a user's private memory or make a therapeutic promise. Use actual access/pricing terms after checking them.

## 1. Warm test invitation

> Do you ever record a voice note for yourself and then lose it? I build Afterthoughts, a voice diary meant to make an entry findable later. Would you inspect the demo and try a non-sensitive example only after reading how recordings are handled? I want to know whether you can find the entry again, not hear what you recorded. No pressure to share a personal memory: https://afterthoughts.app/.

Once after seven days: “Closing the loop on the diary test. If it isn't useful now, no problem; I won't keep nudging. If you tried it, was the hard part recording, transcription or finding the entry again?”

## 2. Owned-feed post

> The part of a voice diary I care about happens tomorrow.
>
> Can I find the little thing I mentioned tonight without playing every recording again?
>
> I'm building Afterthoughts around that loop: say an ordinary moment, keep it privately and find it by a word later. The demo uses invented nights, not customer stories. The public browser demo and the account service have different data boundaries; read those before recording.
>
> Inspect it at https://afterthoughts.app/. What word would help you find a note you made last week?

## 3. Useful reply

> I find “what word will I remember later?” a useful note label. It can be a place, object or ordinary detail rather than a polished journal title. You don't have to make a private memory into a public post for it to count as keeping it.

Use only if this reflects your actual habit and the discussion; no link unless invited.

## 4. Organizer request

> Could I offer a free small demo on keeping and finding one ordinary memory? We'd use fictional examples and never ask anyone to play or share a diary entry. I build Afterthoughts and would disclose that. People can inspect the privacy/access explanation before choosing whether to try. May I send you the outline?

## 5. Approved broadcast

> A small free demo on keeping a moment in your own voice and finding it later. Fictional/non-sensitive examples only; nobody shares diary content. Afterthoughts is optional: https://afterthoughts.app/. Read actual data/access terms before recording, and reply to the organizer for confirmed details.

## 6. Peerlist description

**Tagline:** Keep a night in your voice, then find it by a word.

> Afterthoughts is a voice-diary retrieval workflow. The sample nights are invented, and the public browser demo is distinct from private account recordings. I'd like feedback on first capture, readable transcription and finding the source entry again. Patterns are descriptive, not diagnoses or advice about your relationships.

## 7. Product Hunt maker note

> I wanted a diary that makes a small memory retrievable without making it performative. Please start with the invented demo, inspect how account recordings are processed and try a non-sensitive example if it suits you. The useful test is whether you find the entry again and trust the source, not how emotional the AI can sound. No personal story or vote is expected.

## 8. Editor pitch

**Subject:** A free exercise for keeping an ordinary memory

> I wrote a short memory-keeping exercise: describe one ordinary moment, choose the word you'll remember and find the note later. Could it fit a creative/community edition? I can send the full piece with an invented example and disclose that I build Afterthoughts. No paid feature or reader diary content requested.

Repeat with a new retrieval lesson, not invented transformations in someone's mood. A user recommendation should talk about the tool voluntarily; their recording remains private.
