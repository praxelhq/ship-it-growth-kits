# Lynkr — Copy pack

Publish the price/free-access line only after `04` Fix 1 matches the tested account flow. All examples below are illustrations, not claims about actual student outcomes. Replace an example with your own verified work when using it personally.

## 1. Organizer invitation

> Could I run a free 30-minute session for 20 students on turning a real project into one clear LinkedIn message? We would separate personal contribution from team claims, check every fact, and write a message with one genuine question. I built Lynkr to help with that process. Nobody has to share a private résumé or send a message during the session. If this would help your members, may I send you the outline first?

Send individually. Ask for permission before sharing a signup link. Your offer is the session, not access to their contact list.

## 2. Approved campus broadcast

> Built something but struggling to explain it on LinkedIn? I'm running a small working session on turning one project into a factual headline and a message to one relevant professional. Bring a project you actually worked on. We will check the facts before drafting; no invented impact numbers and no messages sent for you. Lynkr is at https://www.lynkr.cloud/. Read the current free-access and paid-plan details before creating an account. Reply to me if you'd like a place; I'll confirm the session details directly.

Use only after the organizer accepts. Send actual time and location in your confirmation, not a fabricated public event. Do not collect résumés in a public group.

## 3. LinkedIn teaching post

> “Worked on the college fest dashboard” leaves the reader guessing.
>
> Three better questions:
>
> What did you personally build?
> Who used it?
> Which result can you actually support?
>
> If you wrote the SQL, say that. If you don't know the time saved, leave the number out. “Built a dashboard used by the sponsorship team” is more credible than an invented efficiency percentage.
>
> I'm building Lynkr around that sequence: tell one real story, confirm the facts, then draft. You still decide what's true and send the message yourself.
>
> https://www.lynkr.cloud/
>
> Which project is hardest for you to describe in two sentences?

Attach a clearly labelled synthetic example. Never imply the example belongs to an actual user.

## 4. Useful comment without a product pitch

> One thing I'd add to a project write-up: separate “our team shipped” from “I personally did.” A reader can then ask about your part without guessing. If the result isn't measured, describing who used the work is still useful evidence.

Use only under a relevant project/portfolio discussion. Change the first sentence to address the actual post. No link unless invited; do not repeat the identical comment.

The important part is before generation: confirm what you personally did and decide whether you have a real reason to contact the recipient. ## Repeat without becoming noise

The second post should use a different real problem: a vague About section, an overly broad question, or a team achievement presented as individual work. Change the example and the lesson, not just the adjectives. Publish actual user feedback only with explicit permission and without private messages or identifying details.
