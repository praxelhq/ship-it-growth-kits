# Lynkr — Video scripts

Record a screen and your own voice with a phone microphone. Use a synthetic project labelled “example”; never show someone else's résumé, messages or contact list. Captions should carry the whole idea without sound. No rendered video or paid editor is needed.

## A. One project to one message — 70 seconds

**Where:** the landing page, clinic invitation and LinkedIn. **First two seconds:** “Don't let AI invent your experience.”

| Time | Shot / on-screen text | Spoken line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–2s | A made-up impact percentage crossed out; “Unsupported” | Don't let AI invent your experience. |
| 2–12s | Type a short example: built a fest dashboard; wrote SQL; team used it | Start with what you personally did. I'm using an example project here. |
| 12–27s | Show interview and actual fact-confirmation step, normal speed | Lynkr asks about the work, then I check the facts before using them. |
| 27–40s | Disable an unverified result and leave an unknown recipient detail blank | I don't know the impact number, so it stays out. I don't know this person's work yet, so I have to fill that in. |
| 40–56s | Generate the actual headline/message; highlight one clear question | Now the draft has evidence and a reason to write. It still needs my judgment. |
| 56–65s | Review, copy; stop before sending | Nothing is sent for me. I decide whether to use it. |
| 65–70s | URL and the tested current access terms | Try a non-confidential project at lynkr.cloud. Read what's free and what's paid first. |

Do not simulate a successful generation in the edit. If it fails, fix or reshoot after it genuinely works. Show enough of the fact-check step to make the advantage visible.

## B. The number you should delete — 25 seconds

**Where:** LinkedIn native video and WhatsApp clinic preview. **Hook:** “Did your project really save 40%?”

0–2s: zoom on “improved efficiency 40%.” Say the hook. 2–10s: ask “How did you measure it?” and pause. 10–18s: replace with “Built the dashboard used by the sponsorship team,” marked “example.” Say, “If you didn't measure the percentage, name the actual work and user instead.” 18–25s: show a Lynkr fact card being switched off. Say, “That's why I ask you to confirm facts before drafting.” End with the URL, no claim that the product verifies reality independently.

## C. The stranger doesn't owe you a referral — 30 seconds

**Where:** personal LinkedIn feed, then the clinic. **Hook:** “Your first message asks for too much.”

0–5s: show a synthetic “Please refer me” message. 5–12s: cross out the referral request and show a relevant public-post reference. 12–24s: show the example “I built a dashboard for our fest. Your point about metric definitions came up there too. How do you settle a disputed metric before building?” Read it aloud. 24–30s: say, “A genuine question starts a conversation. A better draft cannot guarantee a reply.” Show the reason-to-contact step and URL.

## D. Same facts, different tool — 35 seconds

**Where:** Peerlist gallery and LinkedIn. **Hook:** “The interesting part happens before the paragraph.”

Record a normal free drafting workflow using the same synthetic facts, then Lynkr's confirmed vault and unknowns. Give both a fair prompt. Show any weak result honestly. Say, “Generic writers can draft well. I'm testing whether confirming facts once makes the next draft easier to trust.” Finish with a question: “Which fact would you want to check before sending?” Do not claim a competitor always hallucinates or Lynkr never makes mistakes.

Bank five reusable clips: the interview, confirming a fact, disabling a fact, filling an unknown and reviewing before copy. Keep the cursor and text large enough to read on a phone. No recruitment screenshots or fake reply notifications.
