# SATHI — Measurement

## Day 0: write the baseline before changing the page

**Target: 1,000 signups in 30 days**, under the [consumer target policy](../../TARGET-POLICY.md). For this product, count **an adult in India starting their first genuine non-test AI conversation, using a stable anonymous/account identity only where the product already supports it**. Exclude your own tests, bots and repeat identities. Do not count APK downloads, the public demo, repeated conversations or support-route opens as new signups. If anonymous identities cannot be deduplicated, report starts as an estimate separately.

**Activation:** the adult completes a voluntary ordinary-use exchange and can find the leave/delete controls; this is product use, not a wellbeing outcome. Report the signup and activation totals beside one another every week. A view, a CTA click or a download attempt is not automatically either.

[Microsoft Clarity](https://clarity.microsoft.com/) can help on public landing pages if configured consistently with your privacy promise and consent choices. Check whether it is already present before installing a second tag. The current site says no third-party analytics or trackers. Do not add Clarity to the app or chat. Use only the already-disclosed anonymous feature counts with no message text; respect consent and the exact privacy promise.

## Product events, with a reason

- First eligible conversation start, without content.
- Reply delivered versus failed, without transcript or emotional labels.
- Session voluntarily ended; no target for long chat duration.
- Later-date voluntary return, not a new signup.
- Control/error counts only if already disclosed; never send health text to analytics.

Keep the source on the first visit and the date of the first eligible completion. Show counts by source and the observed conversion fraction. Record returning people separately; do not add overlapping 24-hour cohort readings to manufacture a monthly total. The leaderboard is context, not your definition of a successful user.

## Weekly review

| Checkpoint | What you write down | What you change if behind |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 7 | The five private invitations, one maker post and one clip scheduled through Day 7: record actual deliveries, visits, first starts and completions; watch five attempts. | If nobody qualified starts, change the invitation. If people start but fail, fix `04` before more distribution. |
| Day 14 | Cumulative signups + activations, best source and repeat-use cohort. | Compare actual rates with `02`; stop the weaker route after two real attempts, not after one unanswered message. |
| Day 21 | Repeat use, partner attendance and hours spent per activated person. | Repeat the channel that produced finished use. Drop publicity that creates support but no useful completions. |
| Day 28 | New versus returning people, activation rate, complaints or failed expectations. | Close the largest gap you can demonstrate. Do not loosen the counting rule. |
| Day 30 | Verified signups, activated people, returners, revenue if real, hours and unresolved problems. | Explain the shortfall from 1,000 and the observed next-month route. |

These are decision checkpoints, not invented weekly growth promises. If a tester thinks a person is monitoring, the app is therapy or the privacy promise excludes all provider processing, fix that misunderstanding before more invitations. Do not use crisis events, sensitive message content or time spent chatting as a marketing success metric.

## Your one-page results note

Write the actual dates; eligible signups; activated people; people who returned; the source that produced them; the largest point of abandonment; one real change and what happened afterwards. Separate paid transactions from free use, and revenue from profit. Quote users only with permission. If you are below target but have a small cohort returning for the intended job, show that evidence plainly.
