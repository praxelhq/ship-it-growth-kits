## Who your first customers actually are

**The owner of one PG or hostel with 20–80 beds, in a student or IT corridor of an Indian city, currently running it from a notebook and a WhatsApp group.**

Your own page nails the caricature and it's accurate: *"Still running 40 beds from a WhatsApp group and a notebook?"* That sentence should be doing more work than it currently is.

Specifically:
1. **Single-property PG owners, 20–80 beds.** Usually one person, often with a warden. Collects rent by UPI and cash, chases on the 5th, loses track of deposits.
2. **Small chains, 2–5 properties.** Your best commercial customer: the pain scales, they have a manager to delegate to, and they'll pay per bed without flinching.
3. **Hostel and co-living operators** near colleges and IT parks.
4. **Accidental landlords** with one building of 10–15 rooms who've realised it's a job.

**The crucial fact for distribution: this buyer is geographically clustered.** PGs cluster around colleges, IT parks and hospitals — Kota, Koramangala, Hinjewadi, Gachibowli, Noida sectors, Kalyan Nagar, Mukherjee Nagar. **You can walk down a single road and find forty of them.** Almost no other SaaS buyer in India is this physically concentrated, and your plan should exploit that ruthlessly.

## The one-line pitch

> **Run your PG from one dashboard instead of a notebook and a WhatsApp group — rent reminders, UPI collection, agreements and complaints, all on WhatsApp.**

For a doorstep conversation:

> **Rent ki yaad dilane se le kar receipt tak, sab WhatsApp pe apne aap. Aapko sirf dashboard dekhna hai.**

## The wedge use case

**The 5th of the month.**

Not "property management" — the specific day the owner starts calling tenants one by one to ask where the rent is. Your page already has the right image: *"Calling every tenant on the 5th to ask about rent."*

Everything leads with that. Bed maps, KYC, staff tracking and P&L are what make them stay; **automatic WhatsApp rent reminders with a one-tap UPI link is what makes them start.** Sell one thing.

## Why someone picks you over the alternatives

Real competition: a notebook, a WhatsApp group, and Excel. Then a handful of PG-management apps, and generic property software that doesn't understand beds.

Your genuine advantages:

1. **WhatsApp-native, no tenant app.** *"Your tenants don't need another app."* This is the single most important thing on your site. Every competitor that requires a tenant download fails, because you cannot make 40 students install anything. **Lead with this.**
2. **Beds, not units.** You model floors, rooms and beds, with a live bed map. Generic property software models apartments and is useless for a PG. This is the "they actually understand my business" signal.
3. **Free up to 15 beds, ₹29/bed after.** Transparent, per-bed, and small enough that a 40-bed owner does the maths in his head: ₹1,160/month against the rent he loses to one late payment. **That comparison is your whole pricing pitch.**
4. **It covers the unglamorous things** — agreements with e-sign, Aadhaar KYC, deposits, late fees, staff shifts, announcements. PG owners' real pain is administrative sprawl, not any single feature.

## The objections, and the honest answers

1. **"My tenants won't use an app."** — They don't have to. This is your best answer and you should get the objection *so that* you can give it.
2. **"Is the e-signed agreement legally valid?"** — Your FAQ handles this well (leave-and-licence templates, Aadhaar e-sign, e-stamping where applicable). Have the short version memorised; it will come up every single time.
3. **"Who sees my tenants' Aadhaar?"** — Be ready with a clear, specific answer about storage and access. KYC data for 40 people is a real responsibility and owners feel it.
4. **"I manage fine with my notebook."** — Don't argue. Ask: *"How much rent is pending right now, and from which beds?"* The pause is the pitch.

## What NOT to say

- Don't say "property management SaaS platform". Say *"PG chalane ka ek dashboard"*.
- Don't lead with multi-property, P&L or GST invoices. Your single-property buyer hears "this is for someone bigger than me".
- Don't promise legal certainty you can't back. Agreements and Aadhaar are regulated areas; be precise and conservative.
- Don't bury "free up to 15 beds". A hesitant owner with 14 beds should be able to say yes instantly.
