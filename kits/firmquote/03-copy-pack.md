## 3. LinkedIn post

“Can you add a booking page?” is not automatically scope creep. The question is whether both sides know what was agreed.

For a fixed-price website, I want the quote to name the pages, exclusions, revision rounds and timeline. If the client asks for something new, it deserves a clear change and price before work begins.

That is the workflow I am testing in FirmQuote: a dated yes to an exact version, then separately accepted changes.

## 6. Consented follow-up

Could the client identify the version they reviewed? Did a requested addition stay separate until accepted? What was confusing about the link or approval? A general description is enough; please do not send private editor links, client names or contract screenshots publicly.

Never turn the example $2,500 quote into a price for FirmQuote, promise enforceability, or invent recovered revenue. Repeated posts need a different actual scope ambiguity, not a fabricated client dispute.
