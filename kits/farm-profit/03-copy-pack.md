# Farm profit — Copy pack

No guaranteed profit, crop prescription or fabricated farmer testimonial. Always identify demo numbers and planned scenarios as such. Use the farmer's preferred language in person; don't pretend an unverified translation is part of the app.

## #1 — Warm introduction request

Do you know a farm operator who already compares crop costs before the season and would critique a planning tool? I'm testing FarmProfit as a way to put assumptions side by side, not to promise a crop will make money. Could you ask whether they'd like an introduction? I don't need their loan details, land documents or private member list.

## #2 — Grower / FPO business approach

“I'm building FarmProfit to make a crop-cost comparison easier to check. Could we use a non-sensitive example to see whether the units, input costs and selling-price assumptions are clear? You and a qualified local adviser should review it before any planting or spending decision. The homepage's profit figure is only a demo. I won't claim it predicts your farm's return.”

For email, subject: **A checkable crop-cost comparison, not a profit promise**.

One follow-up after seven days: “Would reviewing the assumptions be useful, or should I close the loop? No problem if your current ledger/adviser process works better.”

## #3 — Adviser / organizer request

I'd like a qualified review of FarmProfit's comparison method before promoting it to growers. The questions are simple: are area and yield units explicit, are all relevant costs included, is the price source dated and local, and are uncertain inputs kept uncertain? We can use a synthetic example and won't collect member records in a group. Would you be willing to review the workflow or host a small permitted discussion? This isn't an endorsement request. https://farmprofit.online/

## #4 — Public founder post

A profit number can look precise while the assumptions are weak.

FarmProfit's homepage shows a demo crop comparison. It is not a forecast for someone's land. Before a real planning decision, the farmer needs the units, local costs, yield assumptions and price source in view—and qualified local judgment where the model cannot know.

I'm testing whether the tool makes that conversation clearer, not whether an AI can confidently name a “best crop.” The what-if scenarios on the page are marked coming soon.

If you work with growers, which missing assumption makes a cost comparison misleading most often?
