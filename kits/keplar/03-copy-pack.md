## 3. r/SAT — answering a question *(do this 2–3 times a day, every day, from day 1)*

This is the real work. Example of the tone:

> The trick here is that you don't need to solve for x at all.
>
> You've got 3x + 2y = 14 and they want 6x + 4y. That's just 2(3x + 2y), so it's 28. Done.
>
> The Digital SAT does this constantly — it gives you a system and asks for a multiple of one of the expressions rather than a variable. Whenever you see a question asking for a combination like 6x + 4y instead of x or y, check whether it's a scalar multiple of something you already have before you start solving. It's usually 15 seconds instead of 90.
>
> Skill tag for this one is "Systems of two linear equations" if you're tracking your misses.

**No link. No signature. No mention of your product.** Over four weeks of this, people will click your profile. That's the mechanism.

> Honest ranking, and the first one isn't mine:
>
> **1. Bluebook (College Board's own app).** Non-negotiable. The full-length adaptive practice tests are free and they're the real thing. Everything else is practice for this.

## 10. Reply to a "what should I study" post *(the highest-conversion thing you can write)*

Search r/SAT daily for people posting their score breakdown asking what to do.

> From your breakdown, the thing I'd do first: your math is losing most of its points in Algebra, specifically linear systems, and your R&W is losing them in Words in Context. That's two skills out of 29, which is good news — it means you don't need a general study plan, you need about ten focused hours.
>
> Concretely for the next two weeks:
>
> - 20 linear-systems questions, but **write down the reason** for each one you miss. "Careless" is not a reason — find the actual one.
> - Retest the ones you missed **three days later**, not the same day. Same-day retests only prove you remember the answer.
> - Words in Context: do these in context, never with flashcards. The SAT tests the word's meaning in that sentence, not the dictionary definition.
> - Leave the full-length Bluebook test until the end of the two weeks, then take one to measure.
>
> Don't do general practice sets until those two skills are solid. You'll feel busier and your score won't move.
