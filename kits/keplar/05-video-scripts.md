# Keplar — Video scripts + shot lists

**Video is your biggest channel and the format is unusual: you need volume, not polish.** 60 short vertical videos over 30 days beats one beautiful product film. Teenagers scroll; the only thing that matters is whether the first 1.5 seconds stops the thumb.

---

## The workhorse format — "one question, one shortcut" (0:20–0:30, vertical)

**Make 60 of these.** They are the channel.

### Template

| Time | On screen | Voice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:02 | **The question, already on screen, large.** No intro, no face, no logo. | "Don't solve this." / "Everyone gets this wrong." / "This is on every test." |
| 0:02–0:10 | You annotate the question with a finger or a pen | The setup — what they gave you, what they want |
| 0:10–0:18 | The shortcut, performed | The actual trick, said once, clearly |
| 0:18–0:24 | The answer | "Twenty-eight. Fifteen seconds instead of ninety." |
| 0:24–0:28 | One generalisation + skill tag | "Any time they ask for a combination instead of a variable, check for a multiple first. Skill tag: systems of linear equations." |
| 0:28–0:30 | Tiny end card | "More in bio. Free." |

### Shot list / production rules

1. **Shoot 10 at a time.** One hour of filming = a week of posts. Set the phone up once, print or display the questions, run through them.
2. **Question visible in frame one.** The hook is the question itself, not you.
3. **No intro.** Not "hey guys", not a logo sting. You lose 40% of viewers in the first second to anything that isn't content.
4. **Captions burned in**, large, high contrast. Most viewing is muted.
5. **Hard cut on every sentence.** No pauses, no breathing room.
6. **Vertical, 1080×1920.** Film vertical natively.
7. **Post 2/day** — one morning, one evening US time. Post the same file to TikTok, YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels.
8. **Comment-reply videos:** when a comment asks a question, make a 20-second video answering it. The algorithm rewards these disproportionately and they take five minutes.

### 12 question types to cover first

Pick the ones your data says are most-missed. Suggested starting set:
linear systems shortcuts · "which expression is equivalent" · percent change traps · function notation f(g(x)) · circle equation completing the square · right-triangle trig ratios · two-variable data / line of best fit · probability from a two-way table · Words in Context strategy · Transitions (the four-category method) · Command of Evidence (Quantitative) · Boundaries / semicolon rules

## Video B — "Stop doing practice tests" (0:45, vertical) — the opinion piece

**This is your highest-ceiling video.** Strong claims about study method travel much further than tips.

| Time | On screen | Voice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:04 | Text: **"Stop taking practice tests."** | "If you're stuck at the same score, stop taking practice tests." |
| 0:04–0:12 | A score graph flatlining | "A practice test measures. It doesn't teach. Taking five of them tells you five times that you're a 1290." |
| 0:12–0:24 | A list of wrong answers being tagged by skill | "Here's what to do instead. Take every question you've gotten wrong in the last month and write down the *skill*, not the topic. Not 'math'. 'Systems of linear equations.'" |
| 0:24–0:34 | The short list emerging | "You'll find it's three to five skills. Out of twenty-nine. That's the whole problem." |
| 0:34–0:42 | Calendar, +3 days | "Then here's the part nobody does: retest those questions three days later. Not the same day — same-day you just remember the answer. Three days later you find out if you actually learned it." |
| 0:42–0:45 | End card | "That's it. That's the whole method." |

**No product mention at all** except the bio. This is the one that gets shared, stitched and argued with in the comments — all of which is reach.

## Video C — The product demo (1:30, horizontal and a 45s vertical cut)

**Use:** the landing page (you already have "A real study session, start to finish" — this replaces or sharpens it), YouTube, Product Hunt, the counselor email.

**0:00–0:12 — The real problem**
> "It's nine at night, you have a test in six weeks, and you open Khan Academy. Thirty units. Which one? You spend twenty minutes deciding and then do a random set. That's why your score isn't moving."

*Shot: a real phone, a real library-style interface, a cursor hovering indecisively. Make the indecision visible.*

**0:12–0:30 — Today**
> "Keplar opens on one thing. 'Up next.' One task, picked from your test date, the days you said you can study, and what you got wrong recently. Your streak, the days left, and today's job."

*Shot: the Today screen. Hold on "Up next" for three full seconds.*

**0:30–0:55 — The miss (your differentiator — give it the most time)**
> "Here's the part that actually matters. When you get one wrong, it doesn't just show you the answer. It shows you why your choice doesn't work, and why the right one does. Then it tags the cause — and it brings that question back in a few days to check it stuck."

*Shot: get a question wrong on purpose. Show the explanation. Show the tag. Then cut to three days later and show the same question returning. **That cut is the single most persuasive five seconds you can film** — do it properly.*

**0:55–1:10 — The plan around a real week**
> "You pick the days you can actually study. It schedules the lessons, the practice and the free official Bluebook tests — and when you miss a day, it moves things instead of just going red."

*Shot: the plan view. Deliberately skip a day. Show it re-plan rather than guilt-trip.*

**1:10–1:30 — Honesty + free + close**
> "These are original questions written to the official Digital SAT spec — they're not College Board's, and we don't pretend otherwise. For full-length tests, Keplar schedules the free official Bluebook ones. The plan, the daily practice, the mistake tracking and the college tracker are free. No card. You don't even need an account to start. keplar.app."

*Shot: end on the guest-start screen, so the last thing they see is how low the barrier is.*

### Shot list
- Screen-record on a **phone**, not a desktop. Your viewer studies on a phone.
- Use a real demo student account with real history — an empty app is unpersuasive.
- Your own voice, calm, unhurried, no sales cadence. You're explaining, not pitching.
- Cut a **45-second vertical version** (problem → up next → the miss/retest → free) for Reels and the top of r/SAT posts.

## Video D — "Things I'd tell a junior studying for December" (0:40, vertical, face to camera)

Seasonal, timely, high shareability right now.

> "Six weeks out. Five things.
>
> One. Take one Bluebook test this weekend so you know where you actually are. Not a third-party test — the official one.
>
> Two. After it, write down the *skill* behind every question you got wrong. Not the subject. The skill. You'll have a list of four or five, not twenty.
>
> Three. Spend the next four weeks on only those. It'll feel like you're neglecting everything else. You're not — the rest is already fine.
>
> Four. Retest your misses three days later. Same day doesn't count; you just remember the answer.
>
> Five. Last week before the test, do nothing new. Review what you already fixed and sleep.
>
> That's it. Most people study harder than this and move less."

*Shot: one take, face to camera, no cuts. The unbroken take reads as sincere, which is the point of this one.*
