# Keplar — Landing page conversion fixes

Based on https://www.keplar.app/ as it was on 28 Oct 2026.

**Your current above-the-fold:** `Know exactly what to study next.` / *"Keplar builds your SAT plan around your test date, picks practice for your weak spots and explains every question you miss."* / `Get started — it's free` · `Watch the tour` / *"No account needed. Free plan, no card."* / then the stat strip: `484 original practice questions · 29 skills across all 8 SAT domains · 1,847 colleges with sourced score data · $0 to start, no card needed`

**This is the strongest landing page in wave 1.** The headline is the right one, the proof strip is specific, the honesty about original-vs-official questions is unusual and valuable, and the FAQ pre-empts real objections. The fixes below are optimisations, not rescues.

---

## Fix 1 — Put the test date on the homepage 🔴 highest impact

Your entire product is "a plan built around your test date". Right now, getting a plan requires clicking `Get started`, which is an unknown-length commitment to a teenager at 11pm.

**Put the first step of onboarding directly in the hero:**

> ### Know exactly what to study next.
> **When's your test?** `[ Dec 6 ] [ Mar 14 ] [ May 2 ] [ Not sure yet ]`

One tap, and they're in. The page should then show, immediately, *"38 days. Here's what tomorrow looks like."*

This converts far better than a button because it's a question a student can answer instantly, it's about them, and it demonstrates the product rather than describing it. It also captures intent before any friction.

---

## Fix 2 — Your best feature is invisible above the fold 🔴

The thing that separates you from Khan Academy is: **misses are tagged by cause and come back for a retest a few days later.** No competitor does this. It is the exact thing a plateaued student needs.

It currently appears below the fold, under "When you get one wrong".

**Add it to the hero sub-headline:**

> Keplar builds your plan around your test date, picks practice from the questions you actually miss, and brings every mistake back a few days later to check it stuck.

That last clause is your moat. Put it in the first 25 words.

---

## Fix 3 — "No account needed" is buried and it's a superpower 🟠

> *"No account needed. Free plan, no card."*

This is small grey text under the CTA. For a 16-year-old, "I don't have to ask my parents for anything or give anyone my email" removes the single biggest barrier to trying.

Make it part of the button, not a disclaimer:

> `Start studying — no account needed →`

And in the hero proof strip, promote `$0 to start, no card needed` to first position. It's the most persuasive of the four stats for a cold visitor.

---

## Fix 4 — Social proof is entirely absent 🔴

Scanning your page, there is not one student, score, testimonial, or usage number. For an education product a teenager is deciding to trust with their December test, that's a real gap — and r/SAT visitors in particular will look for it specifically.

Add whatever you honestly have, in order of preference:

1. **A real student quote with a real situation.** *"I was stuck at 1290 for two months. The misses-retest thing found that it was all linear systems." — junior, Ohio.*
2. **Usage numbers**, if they're respectable. *"X students studying for the December test."* Don't fake this.
3. **If you have neither yet:** use a transparency statement instead, which converts better than nothing and matches your existing tone. *"We launched in 2026 and we're small. We don't have thousands of testimonials yet, and we'd rather say that than make them up. What we do have: 484 questions, every one written to the official Digital SAT spec, and no score promises anywhere on this site."*

That third option is on-brand for you and r/SAT will respect it.

---

## Fix 5 — The honesty about original questions should be above the fold, not in the FAQ 🟠

> *"They're original questions written to the official Digital SAT domains and skills, not College Board questions."*

This is in FAQ #2. It is one of the best things about your page, and on r/SAT it is the difference between credibility and being dismissed as another app.

Promote it to a visible one-liner near the hero:

> `Original questions written to the official spec — and we schedule the free Bluebook tests rather than replacing them.`

Your skeptical visitor is specifically looking for whether you're pretending to have real College Board questions. Answer it before they have to go looking.

---

## Fix 6 — $19.99/month is a hard price for your buyer 🟠

$19.99/month or $79/year for a free-tier user who is 16 is a big ask, and the free tier is generous enough (30 questions/day, mistake tracking, plan, games, college tracker) that few will need Plus.

That's a reasonable strategy for acquisition — but if revenue matters:

- **Make the annual the default visual.** $79/year reads much better than $19.99/month for a product used for 3–6 months. Show `$79 for the year` first, monthly as the alternative.
- **Consider a "test-date plan"** — one price through your test date (e.g. $29 through March 14). It matches how students actually think about this, which is in terms of one test, not a subscription.
- **Make the Plus wall concrete:** the moment someone hits the 30-question daily limit is your real pricing page. Make that screen good.

---

## Fix 7 — The seasonal urgency is missing 🟡

Right now, through early December, your visitor has a deadline and your page doesn't acknowledge it.

Add a dynamic line near the hero: **`38 days until the December 6 SAT.`** Update it automatically per test date. Deadlines are the most powerful motivator in this entire category and you're not using the one that's already in your visitor's head.

---

## Fix 8 — 1,847 college pages are an unused SEO asset 🟠

You have sourced score data for 1,847 colleges. That is 1,847 pages targeting `sat score for [college]` — one of the highest-volume long-tail query families in US education search, and it's data you already own and already cite.

Build them. Each page: the sourced ranges, published minimums vs reported ranges (your existing honest distinction), deadlines, and a CTA into the college tracker. This is probably the single highest-return engineering week available to you, and it is entirely separate from the 30-day push.

---

## Fix 9 — Mobile 🟡

Your FAQ says *"Keplar runs in your phone's browser with a tab bar… add it to your home screen"*. Good. Verify:

- Every TikTok and Reddit visitor arrives on a phone. The hero, the test-date picker (Fix 1) and the first question must all work one-handed.
- **Add an explicit "Add to Home Screen" prompt** after the first completed session. A student with your icon on their home screen is worth ten who bookmarked you.
- Test on an older Android, not just an iPhone.

## What to watch in Clarity

- **Segment by referrer = reddit.com vs tiktok.com.** These are completely different visitors: Reddit is skeptical and reads; TikTok is impulsive and scrolls. If TikTok bounces at 80%+, your hero is too text-heavy for them and needs the Fix 1 picker to be the first thing they see.
- **Recordings of guest sessions that answered one question and left.** This is your biggest leak. What was the question? Was it too hard as a first impression? Consider making the first question deliberately easy.
- **Scroll depth to the pricing section.** If free-tier users never see Plus, that's fine for signups and bad for revenue. Decide which you're optimising.
- **Rage clicks in the diagnostic.** 16 questions / 15 minutes is a big ask as step 2 — check whether people abandon mid-way, and if so make it skippable more visibly.
- **Dead clicks on the stat strip.** If people try to click "484 original practice questions", they want to see them. Link it.
