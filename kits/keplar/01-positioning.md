# Keplar — Positioning

**Live:** https://www.keplar.app/ · **Category:** Education · **Profile:** https://shipped.money/p/keplar

---

## Who your first 1,000 users actually are

**US high-school juniors, 4–12 weeks out from a Digital SAT date, who have already started studying and are not sure it's working.**

Three segments, in order of how easy they are to reach:

1. **The self-studier.** Can't afford a $1,200 tutor or a $400 course. Uses Khan Academy and Bluebook. Is on r/SAT every day. Knows their target score to the point. **This is your buyer and they are extremely concentrated online.**
2. **The plateau student.** Took a practice test, got a 1280, took another, got a 1290. Has been doing random problem sets and getting nowhere. Your "misses tagged by cause, retested a few days later" feature is built exactly for them and they don't know such a thing exists.
3. **The parent.** Searches "best SAT prep app", pays if asked, but is not the one who will use it daily. Secondary for acquisition, primary for the $19.99 conversion.

**Do not chase:** international students broadly, PSAT sophomores, ACT, or graduate tests. In month one, every hour spent outside "US junior with a December or March test date" is wasted.

### Timing matters more than anything else in this kit

SAT demand is violently seasonal. As of late October 2026 you are in the run-up to the **December test date**, followed by a quiet January and then the **March** surge. Practically:

- **Now through early December:** high-intent panic traffic. People studying *right now*. Convert them.
- **Late December–January:** the dead zone. Use it to build SEO and content for March.
- **February–March:** the biggest wave of the year.

A 30-day campaign starting now catches the December run-up. That's good timing — lean into deadline-shaped messaging.

## The one-line pitch

> **An SAT plan that tells you exactly what to study next, built around your test date and the questions you actually get wrong.**

Shorter:

> **Stop guessing what to study. Keplar picks your next SAT task every day. Free.**

## The wedge use case

**"I don't know what to study today."**

Not "SAT prep" — Khan Academy owns that and it's free. Your wedge is the **decision**, which is the thing that actually paralyses a self-studying teenager. Your own homepage already nails this: *"Most students lose time deciding what to study."* That sentence should be your headline.

The second wedge, and your strongest feature: **misses are tagged by cause and come back for a retest.** Nobody else does spaced repetition on *your own wrong answers* in SAT prep. A plateaued student understands immediately why that matters.

## Why someone picks you over the alternatives

You're competing with Khan Academy (free, official, enormous), Bluebook (official, mandatory), UWorld ($), Princeton Review ($$), and a pile of apps.

Your three honest advantages:

1. **It makes the decision.** Khan Academy gives a student a library and a teenager four hours from now still hasn't started. "Up next" is one task. That difference is the whole product.
2. **It tracks what you got wrong and brings it back.** Misses tagged by cause, retested days later. This is a real pedagogical edge and it is *completely buried* on your current page.
3. **Genuinely free and genuinely no account.** "Start as a guest, no card, no email" is a bigger deal for a 16-year-old than you think — the friction of asking a parent for an email is real.

**And be honest about what you're not:** you don't have College Board's questions, and you say so. Good — keep that. Your line *"Keplar schedules the free official Bluebook tests and lets you log your scores"* is excellent positioning: you're the plan around the official material, not a replacement for it. **Say that louder.** It converts skeptics on r/SAT, who will otherwise crucify you for "original questions".

## What NOT to say

- **Never promise a score increase.** "Raise your score 200 points" gets you destroyed on r/SAT and is unprovable. Your site is already clean on this — keep it.
- **Don't say "AI-powered".** Teenagers in 2026 read that as "low effort". Describe the mechanism instead: "it picks tomorrow's practice from what you got wrong today".
- **Don't compete with Khan Academy on price.** You're both free. Compete on the decision.
- **Don't lead with the 1,847-college database.** It's a nice retention feature and a terrible acquisition hook — a student panicking about December doesn't care yet.

## The number to lead with

**484 original practice questions across 29 skills and all 8 Digital SAT domains**, plus the scheduling of the free official Bluebook tests. Specific, checkable, not overclaimed. Use it everywhere.
