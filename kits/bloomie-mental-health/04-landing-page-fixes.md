# Bloomie Mental Health — Landing-page fixes

Checked 29 October 2026: homepage, signup, privacy and help. No account, personal entry or deletion test was submitted. Template status and missing contact/operational facts are observed; legal adequacy and technical access controls were not audited.

1. **Complete the factual privacy/support explanation before traffic.** The privacy/help pages explicitly identify themselves as templates awaiting review. Hosting region, backup period and real responsible contact details are unresolved. Replace those unfinished sections with the actual operating facts and working support route, then verify they match implementation. Do not invent a contact or simply remove the template label while facts remain missing. Pause acquisition until this is done.

2. **Keep the actual gentle headline; narrow the promise.** Current H1 “Small moments of care, every day” is appropriate. Supporting replacement: **“A short check-in and optional reflection for adults who want to notice their own patterns.”** Keep the primary **“Create a free account”** → `/signup`. Do not bring back the roster's claim that journaling will solve someone's issues.

3. **Replace absolute access claims with precise, verified language.** Current feature text says “Only you can read it.” The policy describes managed storage and account-level security; this is not evidence of end-to-end encryption or zero operator access. Use **“Your entries are private to your account. Read how storage, access and deletion work before writing.”** Only publish after checking the actual controls. Do not claim an independent security audit.

4. **Explain optional AI before consent.** The policy says AI is off by default and processes selected material only after opt-in. Make that setting and the exact content/provider explanation visible before it is enabled. Replacement: **“AI is optional. We'll explain what is sent before you choose to use it.”** Test the default rather than assuming the policy proves it.

5. **Let people test without a personal disclosure.** Add **“You can explore with a made-up entry. You never need to share journal content for feedback.”** During usability sessions use synthetic data. Do not place session recording on private journal screens or health-input fields merely to improve a conversion chart.

6. **Make boundaries and real support findable.** The help page describes self-care rather than diagnosis, therapy or crisis monitoring, but its account support contact is unfinished. Provide a real working account/data support path and verify any external help information through an appropriate current source. No unverified phone numbers are copied into this kit. Keep clinical claims out of signup copy.

7. **Test phone, access and deletion before a launch.** On your own authorized test accounts, verify account separation, export, deletion and optional-AI defaults; use no real health data. Check large text and visible errors on a phone. Preview title **“Bloomie — a small daily self-care check-in”** and description **“Reflect at your pace. Understand your data choices before creating an entry.”** No privacy compliance or performance certification is claimed here.
