# Bloomie Mental Health — Copy pack

**Do not use these invitations until the privacy/support gate in `04` is complete.** Write no cure, diagnosis or crisis-service promise. Never use a real journal entry or mood as marketing proof.

## 1. Private adult tester invitation

> I'm testing Bloomie, a small self-care app for a daily check-in and reflection. Would you try the navigation for five minutes? You can use a made-up entry; I don't need to see anything personal. It's a free account, and I'll send the actual privacy/support explanation before you decide. I want to know what is confusing, not ask you to report a health outcome. Can I send the link?

After consent send the live privacy page and https://www.bloomie.world/signup . No follow-up after silence.

## 2. LinkedIn maker post

> A private journal app needs more than a calm colour palette.
>
> Before asking people to use Bloomie, I'm checking the concrete things: what is stored, who can access it, what optional AI sends, how deletion works, and how somebody reaches support.
>
> The app's job is modest: a small daily check-in and reflection. It isn't therapy or a promise to solve someone's problems. A person should understand the data choices before sharing anything personal.

After those changes ship, link the actual explanation and describe exactly what was tested. Don't claim the checks are complete while they aren't.

## 3. Organizer request

> Could I offer an optional navigation demo for adults interested in journaling? Bloomie is a self-care check-in app, not a clinical service. We'll use synthetic entries; nobody needs to disclose a mood or personal story. I want feedback on clarity and control, including privacy and deletion. No attendee list, testimonial or public filming is required. If it fits, we can agree a fourth-week slot.

## 4. Peerlist note

**Tagline:** A quiet daily check-in and reflection, at your pace.

> Bloomie combines a short check-in, optional journal and guided self-care exercises. I'm keeping the claims small and testing whether people understand the data choices before entering anything sensitive. Please use synthetic content when testing and tell me where onboarding or control is unclear. No medical outcome or independent privacy audit is claimed.

## 5. Moderator-permitted journaling question

**Title:** What should a journaling app explain before asking for your first entry?

> I build Bloomie, so this is a disclosed product-design question. I'm interested in the practical information a person needs: storage, access, optional AI, export and deletion. Please don't share journal content or health history. If discussing an app isn't appropriate here, I'll keep it out of the group.

Only post if current rules/moderators allow it; no app link or private outreach by default.

## 6. Organizer email

**Subject:** An optional, synthetic-data journaling-app demonstration

> I'd like to offer a short Bloomie usability session for interested adults. It will cover the first check-in flow and clear data controls, using made-up entries. It is a product demonstration, not mental-health advice or a support session. Participation, signup and any follow-up are optional. No private attendee information or journal material is requested. If this does not fit your group, no problem.

## 7. Return check-in

> Did you choose to open Bloomie again later, without a reminder from me? You don't need to say what you wrote or how you felt. Was the first action easy to understand, and could you find the data/export/delete controls? Please report only navigation issues you're comfortable sharing.

Send only to testers who agreed to follow-up. Never turn a positive response into a testimonial without separate permission.
