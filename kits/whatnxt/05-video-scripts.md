# WhatNxt — Video scripts

Show the information flow with fictional choices; no physical exercise required. No claimed safe technique, personal prescription, injury improvement, gym bystander footage or unverified native-app recording.

## A. Filter-to-inspection demo — 50 seconds

0–2s: **“Would this option actually fit the equipment I chose?”**

2–12s: open actual Start and choose a fictional equipment setup. “This is an interface test, not my workout prescription.”

12–25s: choose an area and deliberate level, show real result labels. “Filters should explain what they include.” Don't call beginner medically low-risk.

25–38s: inspect actual cues/video entry after verified; no performing. “Read requirements and decide whether to reject the option.” If cue content hasn't been reviewed, don't endorse its quality.

38–50s: Back/reject and URL. “No signup in the current flow. Your plan and judgment stay yours.” No ten-second speed claim without timing the real path.

## B. Equipment hook — 18 seconds

0–2s: **“Bodyweight can still need a support.”**

2–12s: show an actual result's required setup after verified, without recommending the movement. “The filter should make that visible.”

12–18s: back/select controls and URL. No instruction to improvise unsafe equipment.

## C. Level hook — 20 seconds

0–2s: **“Beginner isn't personal clearance.”**

2–12s: show actual label/criteria and the need to inspect. “A library category doesn't know your individual constraints.”

12–20s: public flow/URL. “You can inspect, reject and keep your existing plan.” No medical suitability claim.

## D. Speed hook — 20 seconds

0–2s: **“Did I understand it, or just tap quickly?”**

2–12s: actual own timed test if measured, with cuts/timer honest. “Choosing is only useful when the labels make sense.” Otherwise use an untimed walkthrough, not a fake ten-second result.

12–20s: result/cue entry and URL. “No physical challenge required to test the interface.” No fabricated user praise or body outcome.
