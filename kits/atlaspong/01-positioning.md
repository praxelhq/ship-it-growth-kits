## Understand the two clocks before pitching

The 29 October 2026 public render opens with **“Before we start”** and a birth-year selector. The actual post-gate heading supplied by the game script is **“A small yes. A sidequest.”**, with a **“Play”** button. Metadata calls it **“Atlaspong: survive the storm”**: name a place beginning with the previous place's last letter. No birth year was submitted, no game completed and no purchase tested in this research. Do not call the gate a broken homepage or claim a successful room from source inspection.

The pricing page advertises free play: **three hearts and a 2:30 thinking clock, no account needed**. Pro is **₹99/month or ₹999/year**, with five hearts and a 3:00 thinking clock; **payments are being switched on**, not verified available. Delivered game code resolves a possible misunderstanding: the five-minute survival timer and personal thinking-time bank are different clocks. Five minutes versus 2:30 is not necessarily contradictory. Verify the current game, then explain both rather than “fixing” the survival promise away.

Start with an adult friend group that already plays verbal Atlas/place-name chains. Their first use is one free solo round against Ziggy; optional friends rooms come after a successful test. Avoid schools/children as your acquisition wedge while age/parent flows require further owner checks. Do not collect birth years in your growth spreadsheet.

**Pitch:** Keep a place-name chain alive against Ziggy, with a fact card after each answer.

**Bio:** Place-name chains. Fact cards. One browser round.

Your wedge is last-letter chaining, game pressure and facts, not a claim to teach geography better or a comparison with an unverified subscription price. Keep **1,000 consumer signups**, using meaningful first started game sessions under the accountless-product policy, with first valid chain/finished-round activation alongside.

The weakness is the age gate before the product is visible. Adults need to understand why it exists and what happens to their data before continuing. A fact card is also not infallible: link its real source and correct reported errors rather than marketing every card as verified educational truth.
