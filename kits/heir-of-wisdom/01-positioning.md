# Heir of Wisdom — Positioning

**Live:** https://www.hierofwisdom.me/ · **Category:** Education · **Profile:** https://shipped.money/p/heir-of-wisdom

**The buyer is a parent, not a child creating an account.** Your live product is a visual-reasoning studio for ages 8–10. Do not turn its original puzzles into a promise of gifted-test scores or school admission.

Actual headline: “Teach your child to see what others miss.” Actions are “Create a free parent account” and “Try a lesson, no sign-up.” The public shape demo was tested by an adult: turning the flag 90° clockwise produced “It matches!” with an explanation. Nothing about a child was entered. Parent signup, PIN controls, private progress and a completed Discovery Quest were not tested.

Your first parent has an 8–10-year-old who enjoys manipulating shapes but guesses at multiple-choice patterns. The wedge is **turn a shape, explain the rule, then try an unfamiliar puzzle using it**, with the parent choosing whether to save progress. The interactive board is stronger proof than a fear-based test-prep headline.

**Pitch:** Let your child discover a visual rule with shapes, then try using that rule on a new original puzzle.

**Bio:** Turn, flip, discover—and explain the rule.

The [curriculum](https://www.hierofwisdom.me/curriculum) lists four skill families, 13 guided lessons, a 10-puzzle Discovery Quest and 31 original practice puzzles. It explicitly excludes full verbal/quantitative test coverage, official scoring and admission predictions. Keep those limits; a small honest scope is more credible than “complete CogAT prep.”

[Pricing](https://www.hierofwisdom.me/pricing) is $0 during early access. A $14/month Family plan is coming later, not a current required purchase; parents must choose before being charged. [Testing Mom](https://www.testingmom.com/buy-now/) offers broader test-prep packages: checked USD prices are $29.99 monthly or $132 billed yearly; the $11 figure is the annual monthly equivalent, not an $11 cancel-any-month plan. Do not copy its fear-based promises or imply affiliation with any test publisher.

Your weakness is interpreting little evidence too confidently. A correct answer after hints isn't independent transfer, and a parent dashboard isn't a validated psychometric score. Show what happened, hints used and uncertainty, without public comparison between children.

Keep B2C **1,000 signups**, counting real parent accounts once per household/account, not each child profile as another customer. Report families completing a first real guided lesson and later voluntary practice beside it. Demo solves are separate.
