# StockIQ — Copy pack

Use only after the report-coverage correction. These drafts teach a process and don't recommend a security, target price or portfolio action. Public company names in the sample are not permission to make a stronger allegation than the source supports.

## 1. Warm learner invitation

> Would you inspect one public company-research sample and its cited source? I build StockIQ and want feedback on whether assessed checks, assumptions and missing data are clearly separated. No need to tell me your holdings or make any investment. The public samples are at https://finlite.app/#samples. I'd like to know which conclusion the evidence doesn't yet support.

One follow-up after seven days: “Closing the loop on the sample-reading test. If it isn't useful now, no problem; I won't keep nudging. If you looked, which missing-data label needed to be more visible?”

## 2. LinkedIn teaching post

> “Not assessed” is not the same as “passed.”
>
> A research checklist should tell you what it checked, which source it used, what date it applies to and what is still unknown. A green summary shouldn't hide missing evidence.
>
> I'm building StockIQ around a source-reading workflow and improving how the reports show coverage. Two reports come with a free account; additional new reports are₹99. You can inspect public samples first: https://finlite.app/#samples.

## 3. Useful comment

> I'd separate the company's reported fact, management's forward-looking statement and the analyst's assumption. Then check the date and what's missing. A scenario calculation can be internally consistent without proving its growth assumption will happen.

Use only where relevant; don't answer a stranger's “should I buy?” question with a product link or personal advice.
