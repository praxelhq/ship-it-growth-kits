# StockIQ — Positioning

**Live:** https://finlite.app/ · **Category:** Consumer financial education · **Profile:** https://shipped.money/p/stockiq

**Your report must not call missing evidence a clean bill of health.** The public sample labels a financial section “FINANCIALLY SOUND” while saying two checks pass and six are not assessed. Its forensic section says “FORENSIC CLEAN” with twelve data gaps. Those are actual report labels, not an allegation about the underlying company. Fix the coverage-to-verdict mismatch before promoting the report to beginners.

The homepage headline is “Check a stock with a checklist, not tips.” “Try StockIQ free” moves to the analysis section; “Sign in to analyse” opens email-link login. A free account gets two reports; each new report after that is₹99. Public sample reports need no sign-in. The eleven-lecture Udemy course is separate; its checkout price was not verified and isn't invented here. No new report generation, account or purchase was submitted.

## Your first user

An adult learning to read one listed company's disclosures, who wants to understand a checklist criterion and inspect its source. They aren't asking for a personalized stock pick, a guaranteed return or a shortcut around missing information.

Start with a source-reading exercise on the existing public sample, not “the next multibagger.” Ask the user to find one source, identify what the report actually assessed and name one unresolved gap. That is a teachable first action even if they never buy a report.

## The pitch

> Learn a company-research checklist, inspect the cited evidence and keep unanswered checks visible. Use the report as a starting point for your own source reading, not a buy/sell instruction.

Bio version: **Read the evidence behind the checklist.**

Your wedge is an explainable research process with dated sources and coverage. A long report is not automatically a better report; a shorter “not assessed” outcome can be more honest than an attractive verdict.

## The honest weakness

The existing sample's data gaps and decisive labels can teach a beginner the wrong confidence level. Make the source date, assessment coverage, assumptions and unknowns more prominent than the final color. Do not present a model-generated judgment as professional investment advice, a forensic certification or evidence of a company's fraud/quality beyond what the sources support.

Keep the **1,000-consumer-account target**, with a completed source-review exercise/report review alongside. Course sales, report generations and brokerage outcomes are different metrics. A user choosing not to act on incomplete evidence is not a failed conversion.
