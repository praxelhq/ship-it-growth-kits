# Signal-Post-AI — Landing-page fixes

Checked 29 October 2026. The real homepage and `/try` render content. A public try form is present, but generation, form success and email delivery were not submitted. Test those before posting; an available form is not proof of a completed result.

## 1. Make the first action the working demo

**Current:** the page mixes “Try it free,” “Add me to the wishlist” and “Join the wishlist.” `/try` asks for the topic/audience and says a post plan will appear after generation.

Use the primary action **“Create my first post plan”** pointing to `/try`. Put **“Get updates about the full product”** below as the wishlist's distinct purpose. Suggested helper text: **“Try the current generator first. The wishlist is for release updates, not required to inspect the try page.”** Verify this matches actual access limits. If generating fails, stop acquisition and fix it before treating the flow as available.

## 2. Replace an unproven outcome with a visible deliverable

**Current:** “Stop staring at a blank box. Post what actually gets noticed.”

Replacement:

> **Turn one topic into a post with a clear point.**
>
> Choose who you're talking to and what you want them to take away. Get a hook, draft, tone, length and visual idea to review before you publish.

“Gets noticed” is not a product feature or a verified result. Show the choices the user controls.

## 3. Show why two plans differ

**Current:** a budget-announcement example suggests an angle but does not establish where its facts came from.

Add a synthetic example labelled **“Illustration, not a news report.”** Show the same work observation aimed at a new colleague versus a customer. Under each output write **“Chosen reader”**, **“Main point”**, **“Claim to check”** and **“Why this format.”** Replacement caption: **“The reader changes the plan. Check every factual claim against your source before posting.”** Do not use a fabricated news statistic to make the demo look current.

## 4. State the actual price status

**Current:** free joining and founding-member pricing are mentioned, but no paid amount appears in the checked page.

Use **“The public try page is available now. Paid pricing for the full product has not been announced here.”** Add precise generation limits if they exist, after checking the product. Do not invent a countdown, discount percentage or release day. In the wishlist acknowledgement, say exactly what email the user should expect and test that it arrives.

## 5. Give generation an honest progress and failure state

**Current:** `/try` says generation usually takes 10–20 seconds and repeats the output-placeholder instruction.

Use a progress label **“Preparing your post plan…”** and a failure label **“That plan couldn't be generated. Your topic is still here; try again.”** Keep the entered topic and audience after a failure. Show **“Review facts and wording before publishing”** above the result. These are proposed replacements, not a claim that a current failure was observed.

## 6. Reduce the phone-to-demo gap

Put the first action and one readable output example before long feature explanations. Keep body text large enough for phone reading and input labels visible above the keyboard. Verify the actual mobile view, keyboard behaviour and slow-network form submission; this text audit did not measure speed or observe clipping. Don't spend week one on decorative motion while the product/wishlist distinction remains unclear.

## 7. Make the shared preview specific

Proposed title: **“SignalPost AI — choose a point, then write the post.”** Description: **“A topic-to-post plan with hook, draft, tone, length and visual idea. Try it, inspect it and check the facts before publishing.”** Test the actual preview in your own messaging apps. Never label a preview as broken without observing it.

Success means three new users can tell you what is available now, what the wishlist does, and can generate a first plan without coaching. Record rejected or unhelpful suggestions alongside successful generations; polish alone does not establish usefulness.
