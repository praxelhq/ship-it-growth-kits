# Signal-Post-AI — Video scripts

Use a screen recording and your own phone microphone. Every example must be non-confidential and labelled synthetic if it isn't your actual work. Show real generation; cut waiting time only with a visible “waiting shortened” label.

## A. One topic, one reader, one plan — 60 seconds

**Use:** homepage, LinkedIn and approved clinic invitation. **Hook in first two seconds:** “A topic isn't a point.”

| Time | Shot and on-screen text | Spoken line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–2s | Blank editor, “Team onboarding” | A topic isn't a point. |
| 2–12s | Open `/try`; enter a synthetic onboarding observation | I want to explain one change to a new team lead, not to everyone on LinkedIn. |
| 12–22s | Show audience and objective fields | The reader and takeaway come before the paragraph. |
| 22–38s | Generate normally; inspect actual hook, key points and draft | Here's the suggested plan. I need to check that the example is true and the point fits my reader. |
| 38–50s | Highlight the actual visual idea and reject or keep it | This visual helps only if it explains the change. A post doesn't always need an image. |
| 50–60s | Review/edit output and hold URL | SignalPost helps make the choices. I still check the facts and decide what to publish. Try signalpostai.com/try. |

If the product cannot produce the shown output, don't fake it; fix or narrow the demo.

## B. Three audiences — 25 seconds

**Use:** LinkedIn native video. **Hook:** “Same event. Wrong reader.” 0–5s: show a synthetic onboarding topic. 5–15s: three cards read “new joiner: what changed,” “team lead: what decision worked,” “customer: why it matters.” Say those lines. 15–22s: choose one reader and show the resulting plan. 22–25s: “Choose the reader before you ask for five captions.” End on URL. No engagement claim.

## C. Does it need a carousel? — 30 seconds

**Use:** campus marketing session and LinkedIn. **Hook:** “This probably doesn't need seven slides.” 0–8s: show a simple one-sentence lesson and an overlong mock carousel, clearly labelled example. 8–18s: show the plan's format suggestion; explain whether it adds understanding. 18–26s: reduce it to a short post if appropriate. Say, “The format should serve the point, not hide that there isn't one.” 26–30s: show `/try`, “Review, then publish.”

## D. Check the claim — 35 seconds

**Use:** launch gallery and a follow-up teaching post. **Hook:** “A good hook can still be wrong.” Show an invented statistic marked “example, unsupported,” remove it, and open the real source the user would need to verify before publishing. Say, “The tool can help structure an opinion. It does not make an unchecked number true.” End with the revised plan and a question about which claim the viewer would check first. Do not imply the product performs live fact checking unless it actually does.

Capture reusable shots of entering a topic, choosing the reader, inspecting a hook, changing the format and rejecting a weak suggestion. Use hard cuts and readable captions. A recorded founder explaining one rejected output is more useful here than stock footage of a busy feed.
