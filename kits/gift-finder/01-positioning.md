# Gift Finder — Positioning

**Live:** https://gifts.ayushiuniyal.me/ · **Category:** Consumer shopping tool · **Profile:** https://shipped.money/p/gift-finder

Your first user has a friend's birthday this week, a real budget and two useful facts about the recipient. They don't want fifty products or a corporate gifting consultation. They want three ideas they can explain as thoughtful.

The live headline is “Find a gift they'll actually like.” The actual primary button is “Find my gift,” with a gift emoji; it is a JavaScript control rather than a link. The page promises five questions, three ideas, no signup and less than a minute. A “Get corporate gift ideas” link goes to `/corporate`; that is a separate buyer/job. No paid consumer-tool price is shown. The writer read the public flow but did not submit answers or verify generated results, live retailer prices or availability.

## The pitch

> Answer five questions about the person and your budget. Get three gift ideas with a reason each fits, then check the seller before buying.

Bio version: **Three considered ideas. Less gift panic.**

The wedge is an ordinary birthday for someone you know, not “perfect gifts for everyone.” Have the user name an interest and a specific shared memory if they want, without asking for a recipient's full name, address or private details. The result should explain the match; a generic mug at every budget doesn't earn the promise.

## Alternatives

[ChatGPT Free](https://chatgpt.com/pricing/) can suggest gift ideas without paying for another tool. Asking a mutual friend and browsing a retailer manually cost no extra recommendation-software fee. Gift Finder must earn the click with a short guided decision and three reasons worth considering, not claim an exclusive ability to recommend.

You are not a retailer. An idea isn't evidence that a specific listing is in stock, legitimate, correctly priced or deliverable by the occasion. Keep item-price estimates and live seller checks distinct. If any links are affiliate links, disclose that where the user chooses, not only in a footer.

## The first trust test

Watch five people try the quiz on their phones with fictional/non-sensitive recipient details. Time the whole path to three usable ideas. If it takes longer than a minute, change the promise rather than cutting the questions that make the recommendations relevant. Test save/revisit behaviour before marketing the “Saved” button as persistent across devices.

Keep the **1,000-consumer-signup target** under the no-account session definition: unique first quiz sessions, reported beside completed three-idea results and meaningful saves/outbound checks. Corporate lead forms, repeat quiz refreshes and retailer purchases must not be blended into that count.
