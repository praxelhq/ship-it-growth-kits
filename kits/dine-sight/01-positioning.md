## Start here: you are pre-launch, and that changes everything

Your page says `Launching Soon`, *"Early access is open"*, and *"Request access and we switch on AI drafts and Google review sync as we onboard you."* Zomato and Swiggy are "coming next".

So the goal for this month is **not a signup count** — and the board agrees: you are B2B, and your target is **10 active restaurants** ([`TARGET-POLICY.md`](../../TARGET-POLICY.md)). What this kit aims you at:

> **40–80 real restaurants in one city, onboarded by hand, with the Google integration actually working for them — the first ten of them inside two weeks.**

That is worth more than 1,000 email addresses from people who will have forgotten you by the time you launch. A waitlist that goes cold is the most common way a pre-launch B2B product dies.

Build the free, ungated tool in `04` Fix 5 **because it opens conversations and ranks in search**, not to manufacture a signup number. Its uses do not count toward your ten.

## Who your first customers actually are

**The owner or manager of an independent restaurant in one Indian city, with 50–800 Google reviews and a rating between 3.6 and 4.3.**

That band is the whole business:

- **Above 4.4:** they're doing fine, they don't feel the pain.
- **Below 3.5:** they have a food problem, not a review problem, and you can't fix it.
- **3.6–4.3 is where the anxiety lives** — close enough to care, far enough to hurt. Your page says it exactly right: *"Your rating moves your bookings. Right now, nobody's watching it."*

Who specifically:
1. **Owner-operators of 1–3 outlets.** Cafés, casual dining, cloud kitchens. The owner reads the reviews personally and feels every one-star.
2. **The manager who's been told to "handle the online stuff"** and has no idea how.
3. **Small local chains (3–10 outlets)** — your best commercial customers, because the pain scales and so does the price.

**Critically: these people cannot be reached online.** They are not on Reddit, Twitter, Product Hunt or LinkedIn in any volume. They are in their restaurants, between 3pm and 6pm, when the lunch rush is over. That single fact determines this entire kit.

## The one-line pitch

> **Every Google review your restaurant gets, answered in your own voice, within minutes.**

For a doorstep conversation:

> **Aapke Google reviews ka reply apne aap ban jaata hai — aapki hi bhasha mein. Aap approve karke post kar dijiye.**

## The wedge use case

**The unanswered one-star.**

Not "review management" — the specific, visible wound: a bad review sitting unanswered for eleven days while every potential diner reads it. Your own page says it: *"One bad review sits unanswered for days — and every diner sees it."* That line should be your headline.

The demo on your site is already perfect for this: *"Waited 40 minutes, food was cold"* → a warm, specific, apologetic reply drafted in the restaurant's voice. **That before/after is your entire sales pitch and it works in 15 seconds on a phone, standing in someone's restaurant.** Build everything around showing it.

## Why someone picks you over the alternatives

The real competition is **nothing** — most independent Indian restaurants reply to no reviews at all. Secondarily, an agency charging ₹10–25k/month to "manage online presence".

1. **It's in their voice.** Not a template. Not corporate. The difference between "We regret the inconvenience caused" and the reply on your own demo page is the entire product.
2. **Minutes, not days.** Speed is visible to every future diner reading the thread.
3. **They stay in control.** Approve, edit, or auto-post once they trust it. For an owner who's terrified of an AI saying something stupid under their restaurant's name, "you approve it" is the whole sale. Your FAQ already asks this question — put the answer in the hero.
4. **One inbox, eventually.** Google now, Zomato and Swiggy next. Say honestly what's live today.

## The two objections that will kill the sale if unprepared

1. **"Will the AI embarrass me?"** — Your FAQ names this. The answer must be demonstrated, not stated: show a draft, let them edit it, point out that nothing posts without their tap.
2. **"What will it cost?"** — Your FAQ has this collapsed with no answer. **An owner who can't find a price assumes it's expensive and stops.** Commit to a number this week, even if it's "free for the first 50 restaurants, then ₹X".

## What NOT to say

- Don't say "AI-powered review management platform". Say "your reviews get replied to".
- Don't lead with Zomato and Swiggy — they're not live. Overpromising to a restaurant owner you'll see again next week is a bad trade.
- Don't sell "reputation management". It sounds like an agency and agencies have burned this audience.
- Don't ask for a password to anything. Your page handles this well already — keep saying "real Google sign-in, no password shared" loudly.
