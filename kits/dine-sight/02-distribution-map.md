# Dine Sight — Distribution map

**This is the most offline kit in wave 1 and that is not a limitation — it's the correct strategy.** Your buyer stands in a building you can walk into, with a phone in their pocket, between 3pm and 6pm. Almost nobody else in the cohort has an addressable market that physical.

Pick **one city and two neighbourhoods**. Own them completely. A dense cluster of 40 restaurants that all know each other is worth more than 400 scattered signups, because restaurant owners talk to each other constantly and referral is the real channel.

---

## Tier 1 — Walking in 🔴 your primary channel, by a distance

### The list (build it in half a day, free)

**Google Maps is a complete, free, perfectly-qualified prospect database for you.**

1. Open Google Maps, search `restaurants in [your neighbourhood]`.
2. For each: note the name, the rating, the review count, and **how many recent reviews have an owner response**.
3. Qualify: **3.6–4.3 rating, 50+ reviews, and visibly unanswered recent reviews.** That last one is the buying signal and it's visible from your sofa.
4. 200 qualified restaurants in two neighbourhoods is one afternoon's work.

**You will know more about their review situation than they do before you walk in.** That is your opening line.

### The visit

- **Go between 3pm and 6pm.** Never during a meal rush. You will be thrown out, correctly.
- **Ask for the owner or manager by name** if you can find it on Google.
- **Lead with their own reviews on your phone**, not with your product. See copy pack #1.
- **Show the demo in 30 seconds**: their real unanswered review → a drafted reply in their voice. Do it live on their restaurant if the product can; if not, use the demo on your site with their review pasted in.
- **Onboard them on the spot.** Google sign-in takes a minute. Do not leave with "I'll email you a link" — you will never hear from them again.

**Realistic: 20 visits/day → 8–10 real conversations → 3–5 sign-ups.** Three days a week for four weeks = **36–60 restaurants.** That's your month.

### The follow-up that makes it stick

Two days later, send a WhatsApp with **one real reply their account drafted** and the words *"this one's ready, want me to post it?"* The second touch converts the undecided.

## Tier 2 — The people who already visit restaurants daily 🔴

Every one of these has a relationship with 50–300 restaurant owners and visits them weekly. One partnership beats a hundred cold walk-ins.

| Partner | Why they'll talk to you | The offer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Restaurant supply vendors** — vegetables, meat, packaging, disposables, beverages | They see every owner weekly and have nothing new to say to them | Free for any restaurant they refer; a small referral fee if you can afford it |
| **POS / billing software resellers** (Petpooja, Posist, Dotpe local partners) | Already selling software to exactly your buyer | Co-sell or bundle |
| **Food photographers and menu designers** | Work with new and rebranding restaurants | Referral |
| **Zomato / Swiggy field account managers** | Visit restaurants constantly; always looking to add value | Informal referral |
| **Local food bloggers and Instagram reviewers** | Know every owner in the area | Give them something to talk about |
| **Restaurant associations** (NRAI local chapters, city hotel/restaurant associations) | Directly represent your buyer; hold meetings | Offer a free session at a members' meeting |
| **Commercial kitchen equipment dealers, CA firms serving restaurants** | Same relationship | Referral |

**Pitch 20 potential partners in week 2.** Realistic: 3–5 conversations, 1–2 that produce anything, **10–40 restaurants.**

## Tier 3 — Where restaurant owners actually are online 🟠

Not where you'd guess.

| Where | Notes | Realistic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **WhatsApp groups for restaurant owners** | These exist in every city, usually organised by an association or a supplier. **Getting into three of them is worth more than every other online channel combined.** Ask every owner you meet: "are you in any restaurant owners' groups?" | 100–400 members each, very high trust |
| **Facebook groups** — "Restaurant Owners India", "Cloud Kitchen Owners", city-level F&B groups | Real and active | 300–3,000 views/post |
| **Instagram** — most restaurants are run from their Instagram account, and the owner reads the DMs | **DM them.** 20/day, personalised, referencing their actual reviews. Better reply rate than email by a wide margin. | 20–30% reply rate |
| **LinkedIn** | Only useful for chains (3+ outlets) and for partner outreach | 500–3,000/post |
| **r/india, r/indianrestaurants, r/bangalore / r/mumbai etc.** | Owners are thin on the ground, but F&B-adjacent people are there | 300–3,000 |

## Tier 4 — The free tool that gets you volume 🟠

**This is how you reconcile "40 onboarded restaurants" with "1,000 signups".**

Build a free, ungated **Review Reply Generator**: paste any review, pick a tone, get a reply. No account. Works today, no onboarding, no waitlist.

Then:
- It's a page that ranks for `how to reply to a bad google review restaurant`, `restaurant review reply examples`, `negative review reply template` — all real, searched queries with perfect intent.
- It's shareable in every Facebook group and WhatsApp group without being a pitch.
- **Every user of it is a qualified lead** who has just demonstrated they care about review replies.
- It produces email addresses at a volume walking the street never will.

Realistic: **300–1,500 uses in month 1**, growing with search.

## Tier 5 — Builder audiences 🟡 *(credibility, not customers)*

Peerlist, Product Hunt, r/SideProject, Indian startup communities. **A few hours total, no more.** They will not give you restaurants. They might give you a partner introduction or a hire.

---

## Channels to skip

- **Hacker News, dev communities, Twitter/X.** Zero restaurant owners.
- **Broad digital advertising.** Zero budget, and this buyer needs to meet a human.
- **Cold email.** Restaurant `info@` addresses are unmonitored. Instagram DM and walking in are 10x better.
