# Crewup — Copy pack

Use real confirmed plan details when inviting people privately. Don't invent a location/date in this public kit or imply a free trial lasts forever. No attendee identities, private addresses or contact lists in public screenshots.

## 1. Personal plan invitation

> I'm trying to turn our “we should do a board-game/study evening” into one actual plan. I've put the host, time, place and headcount together in Crewup. Would you like the details? The activity uses what we already have; I'll state any platform/event cost and trial limit before you decide. No pressure to create an account if the plan doesn't suit you.

Send the real plan only after interest. A repeated invite must be relevant to that week's plan, not another nudge after refusal.

## 2. Approved group message

> One concrete plan instead of another “sometime”: I've organized a small activity for our existing circle, with the actual details and headcount in Crewup. Reply if you want the private plan link. I'll confirm cost, trial terms and how to update/cancel before you join. Please don't forward private participant or location details outside the group.

Use only with organizer permission and a real event. The public product is https://crewup4you.me/; don't imply the public feed is your private invitation.

## 3. Co-host invitation

> Would you host one small activity you already know how to run for our circle? I build Crewup and want to learn whether the plan/headcount workflow helps someone other than me. No paid venue or purchase needed. You choose the real details and participants; I'll explain current platform terms first. An honest “not useful” is fine.

## 4. Weekly owned-feed post

> “We should meet” isn't a plan yet.
>
> Who is hosting? When? Where or online? How many people? What happens if the details change?
>
> I'm building Crewup around that last mile, starting with a small circle that already knows one another. I want to measure actual plans and attendance, not a feed full of hypothetical events.
>
> Current public terms include a ten-day trial with fees waived; check the actual post-trial price before joining: https://crewup4you.me/.

After a real event, publish an anonymized lesson, not a fictional attendance result.

## 5. Useful reply

> I'd choose one host and a small concrete plan before opening it to a whole city. An accurate headcount and a clear update/cancellation route solve more than another list of possible activities.

No product link unless asked. Don't advertise under someone else's private gathering announcement.

## 6. Peerlist description

**Tagline:** Turn a familiar group's idea into an actual plan and headcount.

> Crewup is an early activity-planning/discovery app. We're testing the narrow loop: one real host, a specific plan, participants who know what they're joining and an actual outcome. The site advertises a ten-day full-feature trial with fees waived; current post-trial terms must be clear before signup. I'd like feedback on the plan/RSVP flow, not a promise of citywide supply.

## 7. Product Hunt maker note

> The first product problem here is density: a useful plan for a real circle, not a map of empty possibilities. I built Crewup to help people get from a chat suggestion to a plan they actually attend. Please inspect current fees, privacy and participant controls before trying. No vote or event purchase is expected.

## 8. Editor pitch

**Subject:** A free checklist for turning a group idea into a plan

> I wrote a short organizer checklist: host, time, place/link, headcount, cost, expectations and update/cancellation route. Would it fit a community edition? I can send the full worksheet with a fictional example and disclose that I build Crewup. No paid feature, venue partnership or member data requested.

One appropriate follow-up after seven days is enough for an unanswered test request. Respect declines. Don't make a “women-only” label into an unsupported safety claim or publish photos of people who didn't agree.
