# Case Room — Copy pack

Ready to post. **Change the details only you know** — your own competition, your own team's argument, the real number of teams using it.

**Do not post any of this until `caseroom.me` has a real landing page** (`04` Fix 1). A post that sends people to a loading screen burns the channel.

Two rules throughout:

1. **Write for the one organised person on the team**, not for the team. She's the only one who will push four others into a new tool mid-crisis.
2. **Never fight WhatsApp.** Every piece of copy says *keep the group, put the decisions here.* Teams will not leave WhatsApp and you don't need them to.

## 6. Reddit — r/MBA or a campus sub (week 2, once)

Lead with the useful thing. The tool is a footnote.

**Title:** `How to actually run a case competition team (from losing three before we placed)`

> We lost three case competitions before we placed in one, and almost all of the difference was process rather than analysis. Writing down what changed, because nobody told us any of this.
>
> **1. Decide the angle by day two and write it down somewhere findable.** Not in chat. Our first three submissions each contradicted themselves somewhere, because we'd re-litigated the angle on day five and only half the team registered it.
>
> **2. One person owns the story, one owns the numbers.** Not "we'll all do everything". The deck that wins has one voice, and four people editing one narrative produces four narratives.
>
> **3. Write the recommendation slide first.** Then build backwards. If you can't write it on day two, you don't have an angle yet — you have a topic.
>
> **4. Assign someone to argue against you on day six.** Every judge question we couldn't answer was one a teammate had raised and we'd waved away.
>
> **5. Freeze the deck 12 hours before.** Everything after that point made our submissions worse, every single time.
>
> I built a workspace around points 1 and 3 because that's where we kept failing (link in comments, I'm the founder). But the five points are the actual content here and a shared doc with a decision log at the top does most of it.
>
> What's the process thing that changed it for your team?

**First comment:** `Tool I mentioned: caseroom.me, free.`

## 10. Replies you'll need all month

**"We already have a WhatsApp group."**

> Keep it — honestly, don't move your chat. This is only for the things you need to find later: what you decided, why, and what the recommendation is. The group is for talking; this is for remembering.

**"We don't have time to learn a tool."**

> Fair, and you shouldn't. It's one screen — name the case, write the decision. Under a minute, and your teammates don't need accounts to read it. If it takes longer than that, it's my bug and I want to hear about it.

**"Is it free?"**

> Free, yes. No card, no trial countdown.

**"Isn't this just Notion?"**

> You could absolutely build this in Notion. The difference is that it already knows what a case competition needs, so you're not designing a template at 1am on day six — which is exactly when we tried to and gave up.

**"How many teams use it?"**

Answer honestly, always. *"Eleven, including mine"* is more persuasive to this audience than a vague claim, and a number you inflate will be checked by somebody who knows somebody.
