# Case Room — Distribution map

**Read this first, twice:**

1. **Nothing in this document works until `caseroom.me` has a real landing page** (`04` Fix 1). Driving traffic to a 2KB loading screen wastes the channel permanently — you only get one good first post in most of these places.
2. **You need teams, not users.** Every channel below is ranked by whether it delivers *teams with a shared deadline*. One competition cohort beats a hundred scattered signups, and a signup who brings nobody is worth almost nothing.

Reach estimates state their basis. **Verify before you plan around them.**

---

## Tier 1 — Where teams are, at the moment they form 🔴

### 1. Unstop — your single best channel, and it's not close

[Unstop lists 21,000+ competitions](https://unstop.com/competitions) and is the central registry for essentially every Indian campus and corporate case competition. Individual competition pages carry **100–400 registered participants** — for example [Case Twist at NSUT Delhi with 319 registrants](https://unstop.com/competitions/case-twist-netaji-subhas-university-of-technology-nsut-delhi-1651181/amp), [Startup Expo at IIT Delhi with 196](https://unstop.com/competitions/startup-expo-case-competition-iit-delhi-1740065/amp), [Case Crusade 6.0 at Hindu College DU with 120](https://unstop.com/competitions/case-crusade-60-zeitgeist-2026-hindu-college-du-new-delhi-1650329/amp).

**Those registrants are in teams, with one deadline, right now.** That is the exact shape of audience you need and it is sitting in public.

| Play | Reach | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Competition comment sections** | 50–300 views per competition, high intent | Every competition page has a discussion area full of *"anyone need a 4th member"* and *"when is the deck due"*. Be useful there. Answer a real question about the format, then mention the workspace once. |
| **The WhatsApp/Discord groups that form around each competition** | 50–400 per group | The organiser usually creates one. Join as a participant, be helpful, share the tool when someone complains about coordination — **and someone always does.** |
| **Register for competitions yourself** | — | **The highest-leverage thing in this kit.** Enter 4–6 competitions with your own team, use your own product, and talk about it from inside. You get credibility nobody can question, direct access to every other team, and real product feedback under real pressure. |
| **Filter for competitions closing in 7–20 days** | — | A team that registered yesterday is in your 48-hour adoption window. A team on day 8 of 9 will never switch tools. |

### 2. Competition organisers (the channel that scales)

Every one of those thousands of competitions is run by a campus club or committee that wants its entrants to submit better work and has no budget.

**The offer: a free workspace for every team in their competition.** It makes their competition better at no cost and no effort.

| Target | Realistic | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Undergraduate commerce/management college clubs** — SRCC, Hindu, Hansraj, Christ, NMIMS, SIMSREE, St. Xavier's, Symbiosis | 30 DMs → 10–15 replies → **4–8 who tell their entrants** | Each is 100–400 participants in 25–100 teams. **Undergrads are the real volume in Indian case competitions**, and they're ignored by everyone building for MBAs. |
| **MBA case clubs** | 20 DMs → 6–10 replies → 2–5 | Smaller, more intense, higher-quality users |
| **Corporate competition organisers** (the IIT Delhi / Markhub-type national events) | 10 outreach → 2–4 replies | Biggest single cohorts; slowest to say yes |

**An organiser's yes is worth 25–100 teams in one message.** Four of those is your entire month. `03` #1 is written for this person.

### 3. Your own campus and your own team

| Where | Reach | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Your own case competition team** | 4 people | **Start here, today.** Use it for a real competition this week. Nothing else you do is credible until you have. |
| **Your campus case club** | 30–150 | They know every team on campus |
| **Batch and section WhatsApp groups** | 60–400, 30–60% open | Target the organised person in each team, not the group |
| **Juniors** | often more active in competitions than your own year | Underrated — undergrads and first-years compete far more |

### 4. LinkedIn — where case competition results are performed

This audience posts *"thrilled to share that our team secured 1st runner-up"* constantly, with a photo of four people holding a cheque. That makes LinkedIn unusually good for you.

| What | Reach | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Your own posts** | 1,000–15,000 impressions | The "400 messages and nobody remembers the decision" post is highly relatable to anyone who has ever done a case comp. `03` #2. |
| **Comments on competition-result posts** | 200–2,000 profile views each | Congratulate, then ask a real question about how they divided the work. 10 a day, 15 minutes. These people compete repeatedly — they're your next month's users. |
| **DMs to organisers** | — | Club secretaries are all on LinkedIn and they do reply |

## Tier 2 — Worth real hours 🟠

| Channel | Reach | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Instagram** — campus club pages and competition hashtags | 300–5,000 | **Undergraduate case competition culture lives on Instagram, not LinkedIn.** Club pages announce every competition. DM the page; they're run by students who reply. |
| **r/MBA** | 2,000–15,000 views | Self-promo removed on sight. Days 7–11 is your window in the [cluster schedule](../../shared/case-prep-cluster.md). Your legitimate angle is a genuinely useful post on *how to run a case competition team*, link in the comments. Once. |

## Tier 3 — Don't 🔵

- **Product Hunt, Hacker News.** Wrong audience entirely.
- **X/Twitter.** Not where Indian campus competition culture happens.
- **Consulting interview communities.** Nine cohort-mates there and it isn't your product.
- **Coaching-owned CAT Telegram groups.** Wrong audience and you'll be removed.
- **Any channel delivering scattered individuals.** For you this is a strategic rule: a lone signup can't use a team workspace, and a bad first experience with an empty workspace is worse than no signup at all.

## Cohort cross-referrals

- **CaseForge** (`caseforge.in`) — functional interview cases, solo. Zero overlap, same campuses. Straight trade.
- **Cassify** (`cassify.in`) — **careful.** It also targets case competitions, but solo and gamified rather than team-based. Adjacent enough to be a competitor; the honest move is a conversation about who owns which half rather than a cross-promotion.
- **Role.via** (`rolevia.xyz`) — GD practice. **Their audience is already group-shaped**, which makes it the best-fitting cross-referral in the cohort.
- **Skill Prep** (`skillprepp.space`) — drills. Complementary, no overlap.

## Reach summary

| Channel | Low | High |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unstop (comments + competition groups + entering yourself) | 400 | 3,000 |
| Competition organisers (60 outreach) | 500 | 4,500 |
| Own campus + own team + juniors | 150 | 600 |
| LinkedIn (posts + comments + DMs) | 300 | 3,000 |
| Instagram (club pages, hashtags) | 200 | 2,000 |
| Reddit (r/MBA + campus subs) | 250 | 2,500 |
| Peerlist + Quora + YouTube | 150 | 1,200 |
| **Total addressable visitors** | **~1,950** | **~16,800** |

**The honest read.** 1,000 signups needs roughly 8,000–10,000 visitors, so the low end misses and the high end clears. But the headline number is **not the right way to read this plan**, and you should tell the board so.

Your real arithmetic:

| | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Active teams with 3+ members | **120** |
| Average members per team | 3.5 |
| **Signups implied** | **~420 from teams** |
| Plus individuals who signed up and never formed a team | ~580 |
| **Total signups** | **1,000** |

**120 real teams is a genuinely strong month** and it's what the product is for. A thousand signups with fifteen active teams would be a failure wearing a success's clothes. `07` measures teams first and says so to the board.

**Three or four organiser yeses gets you most of the way to 120 teams.** That's the plan. Everything else is support.
