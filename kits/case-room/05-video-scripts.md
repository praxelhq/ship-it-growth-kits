# Case Room — Video scripts and shot lists

Scripts and shot lists only — **you record these on your phone.**

Your product has no visual drama: it's a page with decisions written on it. **So your videos are about the problem, not the product** — and the problem is extremely filmable, because everyone in your audience has lived it.

The single best piece of footage you can get is **a screen recording of a real case-competition WhatsApp group, scrolling.** That shot is your entire argument. Blur the names.

All vertical 9:16, captions burned in.

---

## Video 1 — The hook (your most important asset)

**Length:** 20–26 seconds · **Where:** LinkedIn, Instagram, WhatsApp status, the landing page

### Script

| Time | On screen | You say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–4s | Your face, close | "We won the argument on Tuesday and forgot it by Friday." |
| 4–12s | **Screen recording: a real case-comp WhatsApp group, scrolling fast, names blurred. Hundreds of messages flying past.** | "Nine-day case. We agreed the angle on day four. By day six, two of us were building slides on different assumptions." |
| 12–17s | Still scrolling, then stop | "The decision was in here. Four hundred messages back. Nobody could find it." |
| 17–22s | Cut to the decision log, three clean lines | "So I built the page we needed. What we decided. Why. That's it." |
| 22–26s | Your face, URL captioned | "Free: caseroom.me" |

### Shot list

1. **Talking head, 0–4s.** Phone propped at eye level, window light, quiet room.
2. **The WhatsApp scroll, 4–17s — this is the video.** Screen-record a real group from a competition you were in, scrolling at speed. **Blur every name and photo.** The density of messages is the whole point; a staged thin group chat won't work.
3. **The decision log, 17–22s.** Clean, three real decisions, readable on a phone. The contrast with the previous shot is the argument.
4. **Talking head close**, URL captioned for the last four seconds.

### Rules

- **"We won the argument on Tuesday and forgot it by Friday"** is the best line you have. Open on it. Don't open with "Hi guys, so I built...".
- **The cut from chaos to three clean lines at 17s is the whole product.** Make it abrupt.
- Blur names. One screenshot of a real person's messages posted without consent is a genuine problem, and this audience is small and interconnected.
- No logo intro, no music under speech.

## Video 2 — "Five things that changed how our team competes" (your shareable asset)

**Length:** 60–80 seconds · **Where:** LinkedIn, YouTube, Instagram carousel-style, Reddit comments

This is the video people share who aren't your users yet, because it's useful on its own. It's `03` #6 in video form.

### Script

> **(0–6s, your face)** "We lost three case competitions before we placed. Almost all of the difference was process, not analysis."
>
> **(6–18s, text on screen, your voice over a deck being built)** "One. Decide the angle by day two and write it down somewhere you can find it. Not in chat. Our first three submissions each contradicted themselves, because we re-decided on day five and only half the team registered it."
>
> **(18–30s)** "Two. One person owns the story, one owns the numbers. Not 'we'll all do everything'. Four people editing one narrative produces four narratives."
>
> **(30–42s)** "Three. Write the recommendation slide first, then build backwards. If you can't write it on day two, you don't have an angle — you have a topic."
>
> **(42–54s)** "Four. Assign someone to argue against you on day six. Every judge question we couldn't answer was one a teammate had raised and we'd waved away."
>
> **(54–66s)** "Five. Freeze the deck twelve hours before. Everything we did after that point made it worse, every single time."
>
> **(66–80s, your face, then end card)** "I built a workspace around one and three, because that's where we kept failing. caseroom.me, free. But the five points are the actual advice."

### Shot list

1. **Talking-head bookends**, 6s and 14s.
2. **On-screen text for each numbered point** — this gets screenshotted and the text is the share.
3. **B-roll between points:** a deck being edited, a whiteboard, four people at a table, the WhatsApp scroll again. Short cuts, 2–3 seconds each.
4. Keep the product shot to the last five seconds.

**Post this on YouTube with the title `How to actually run a case competition team`.** It's an evergreen query with no good video answer and it will still be sending you teams in March.

## Video 3 — The 60-second product demo

**Length:** 45–60 seconds · **Where:** landing page ("See what it looks like"), Peerlist, reply to anyone asking how it works

Your product's promise is "useful in under a minute". **Prove it by filming it in under a minute, in one take, with a visible timer.**

> **(0–5s)** "Sixty seconds, one take, real timer. This is the whole thing."
> **(5–15s)** Screen recording: open the link, name the case. "No account, no setup."
> **(15–30s)** Log the first decision — a real one, with reasoning. "What we decided. Why we decided it. Who disagreed."
> **(30–42s)** Paste the link into a WhatsApp group. Teammate opens it and reads it — **no account.** "They don't need to sign up to read it. That's the bit that matters, because they won't."
> **(42–55s)** The story outline and the recommendation field. "Story structure's already there. Recommendation goes first, then you build backwards."
> **(55–60s)** The timer. "Fifty-eight seconds."

**The timer on screen is the whole video.** If your real run takes three minutes, don't fake it — go fix the product (`04` Fix 4) and film it again. This video is a promise you have to keep.

## Video 4 — The organiser video (different audience, different register)

**Length:** 40–50 seconds · **Where:** attached to the DM in `03` #1, sent to case clubs and competition committees

A club secretary doesn't care about your founder story. He cares whether this makes his competition better without costing him anything.

> **(0–8s, you, seated, shirt)** "Hi — I'm [name] from [campus]. This is a free workspace for the teams in your competition. Sixty seconds and you'll know if it's useful."
>
> **(8–20s, the example workspace)** "Teams log what they decided and why, shape their story, and build the recommendation they'll defend. It sits next to their WhatsApp group — it doesn't replace it."
>
> **(20–32s, the decision log)** "The problem it fixes: teams re-decide things on day five and submit decks that contradict themselves. Judges spot it immediately."
>
> **(32–42s, you)** "It's free, nothing to install, works in a browser. If it's useful to your teams, I'll send you a one-line message you can forward."
>
> **(42–50s)** "caseroom.me — or just reply to my message. Good luck with the event."

**Register notes:** shirt, quiet room, no jump cuts, no music. Say **"free"** twice and **"nothing to install"** once. Under a minute — he won't watch two.

## Video 5 — Competition diaries (weekly, 30 seconds, while you compete)

`02` tells you to enter four to six competitions yourself. **Film it.** This is the most credible content available to you and it costs nothing but the phone in your pocket.

One per competition, posted during it:

> **(0–6s)** "Day 6 of [competition]. Here's where our team is."
> **(6–18s)** The actual decision log, as it really is — messy, with a reversal in it. "We changed the angle yesterday. That's logged, so nobody's building on the old one."
> **(18–28s)** "Submission Friday. I'll post whether it worked."
> **(28–30s)** End card.

**Then post the result, win or lose.** A founder who posts "we didn't place, here's what our process missed" earns more trust in this community than any winner's post. And it's honest content you'll have anyway.

## Production rules

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **The WhatsApp scroll is your signature shot** | It's the problem, instantly, with no explanation needed |
| **Blur every name and photo in it** | Non-negotiable — small, interconnected community |
| **Vertical 9:16, captions burned in** | Muted autoplay everywhere you post |
| **Show the product for 5 seconds, the problem for 15** | Your product is text on a page; the problem is the story |
| **Video 3's timer must be real** | It's a promise about your onboarding. Fake it and the first user finds out. |
| **Video 4 is formal; the rest are not** | Organisers are a different audience |
| **Show real decisions, including reversals** | A log with a changed mind in it is more convincing than a tidy one |

**Make Video 1 and Video 4 first.** Video 1 carries LinkedIn; Video 4 carries the organiser outreach that `06` builds the month around. Video 2 is the evergreen asset — make it in week 2. Videos 3 and 5 come after the page and the product are fixed.
