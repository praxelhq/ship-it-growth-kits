# Case Room — Positioning

**Live:** https://caseroom.me/ · **Category:** Education · **Audience:** B2C → **1,000 signups in 30 days** ([`TARGET-POLICY.md`](../../TARGET-POLICY.md)) · **Profile:** https://shipped.money/p/case-room

## Read this before anything else: you have no landing page

`caseroom.me` returns **2,087 bytes of HTML.** There is a title, a meta description, a skip link, and the words *"Opening your workspace…"*. Then the app loads.

There is no headline, no explanation, no screenshot, no pricing, no sign-up path for someone who has never heard of you. A visitor who arrives from a link in a WhatsApp group lands inside a loading state for a product she doesn't understand.

**Consequences you are living with right now:**

- **No search engine can index anything about you** beyond one sentence.
- **Every link anyone pastes renders as a bare URL** — no `og:image`, no preview card. In WhatsApp, where this audience lives, your link looks broken next to everyone else's.
- **Your bounce rate is near-total for cold traffic**, because there is nothing on the page that answers *what is this*.

**You cannot run any of `02` until this is fixed.** `04` Fix 1 is the page, and it is the whole of your days 1–3. Everything else in this kit assumes it exists.

The good news: **your one existing sentence is excellent positioning** and the page can be written around it.

> *"Case Room: a workspace for case competition teams to decide, shape a story and defend a recommendation."*

That is specific, differentiated and clear. Nine of the eleven case-prep projects in this cohort couldn't write a sentence that good about themselves.

## Who your first 1,000 users actually are

**The team of three to five students who have just registered for a case competition, have a deck due in nine days, and are currently losing the argument in a WhatsApp group.**

In priority order:

1. **Undergraduate commerce and management students at Delhi University, Mumbai and tier-1 city colleges.** This is the real engine of Indian case competitions — SRCC, Hindu, Hansraj, NMIMS, SIMSREE, Christ, and dozens more. [Unstop lists thousands of competitions](https://unstop.com/competitions) and the entrants are overwhelmingly undergraduates, not MBAs. **They compete constantly, they compete in teams, and nobody has built them a tool.**
2. **MBA students on case-competition circuits.** Fewer teams, higher intensity, more likely to pay. Their campus case clubs are a channel in themselves.
3. **The one organised person on each team.** This is the individual who actually signs up. Every team has one person who makes the deck at 2am and is quietly furious about the WhatsApp chaos. **Write every piece of copy for her**, not for the team — she is the one who adopts tools and drags everyone else in.
4. **Campus case clubs and competition organisers.** They have lists of exactly these teams and a reason to help them.

**Who is not your user:** the solo candidate preparing for a consulting interview. Nine other projects in this cohort serve her. You are the only one that works on *teams*, which is a different product and a much less crowded one.

## The one-line pitch

Build on your own sentence, with the pain in front:

> **Your case competition team is arguing in a WhatsApp group. Case Room is where the decision actually gets made, written down, and turned into a defendable recommendation.**

Short, for a WhatsApp group:

> **Workspace for case comp teams — decisions, the story, and the recommendation in one place instead of 400 messages. caseroom.me**

For an organiser or club:

> **A free workspace for the teams in your competition. Fewer lost decisions, better submissions.**

## The wedge use case

**The decision nobody can remember making.**

Day four of a nine-day case. The team agreed on a market-entry angle on Tuesday. By Friday, two people are building slides on different assumptions, the WhatsApp group has 400 messages, and nobody can find the moment the decision was made or what the reasoning was.

**That is your wedge, and it is painfully specific.** It is also the thing a judge punishes hardest — a submission that contradicts itself because the team never actually converged.

So lead with: **"what did we decide, and why?"** One screen, one answer, findable in four seconds. Not "collaboration". Not "workspace". The decision log.

Everything else you build — the story structure, the recommendation — hangs off that first moment of relief.

## Why someone picks you over the alternatives

The honest competitive set, and it is tough:

1. **WhatsApp plus Google Docs plus Canva.** Free, universal, already open. **This is your real competitor** and you must beat it on one narrow thing rather than all things. You beat it on *decisions being findable*. A Google Doc has everything in it, which means it has nothing in it — nobody scrolls a 4,000-word doc at 1am to find what was agreed on Tuesday.
2. **Notion.** Genuinely good and genuinely free for students. **You beat it on being pre-shaped for a case competition** — a blank Notion page is a second problem at the moment the team already has one. Your structure (decide → shape the story → defend the recommendation) *is* the product. Say that.
3. **Nothing.** Most teams. They muddle through, submit something internally contradictory, and don't place.

**The sentence that wins this argument:** *"It already knows what a case competition needs. You don't have to build the template at 1am."*

## Your structural challenge: you need teams, not users

Like a GD product, **your unit of adoption is a group, not a person.** One student signing up does nothing — the workspace is empty and pointless until three teammates join it.

This changes your growth model completely:

- **A "signup" that doesn't bring a team is nearly worthless.** `07` measures **active teams with 3+ members**, not accounts.
- **Your target is the one organised person per team**, because she's the only one who will push four others into a new tool mid-crisis.
- **Target teams at the moment of registration**, not during the competition. Once the WhatsApp group has 400 messages, moving is too expensive. **The 48 hours after a team registers on Unstop is your entire window.**
- **One competition cohort is worth a hundred scattered signups.** Forty teams in one competition, all with the same deadline, is a dense, warm, perfectly-timed audience — and that's why `02` is built on competition organisers.

## The two objections that kill the sale

1. **"We already have a WhatsApp group."** Of course they do, and you are not replacing it. Say so immediately: *"Keep the group for chat. Put the decisions here."* Fighting WhatsApp loses; sitting beside it wins.
2. **"We don't have time to learn a tool."** Fatal if unanswered, because it's true — they're in a crisis. Your answer has to be **under 60 seconds to usefulness**: open a link, name the case, log the first decision. No setup, no invites required to start, no account for teammates to view.

## What NOT to say

- **Don't say "collaboration platform" or "team workspace for students".** Both are category words that describe twenty products. Say *"what did we decide, and why"*.
- **Don't try to be a consulting-interview prep tool.** That's nine competitors and your worst position. Teams are your moat.
- **Don't launch anything until there's a page** (`04` Fix 1). Driving traffic to a 2KB loading screen converts at approximately zero and spends channels you only get to use once.
- **Don't promise AI anything.** Your differentiation is structure and memory, which is rarer and more credible than another AI claim in a market drowning in them.
