# Cassify — Copy pack

Ready to post — **from day 8, not day 1.** Nothing here goes out until one case works end to end with saved responses (`04` Fix 1). A post that sends your own campus to a demo that forgets them is the most expensive mistake available to you.

Three rules throughout:

1. **Lead with the coffee shop case, not the platform.** *"Sales are up. Profits are down. Where should this campus café look first?"* is a better hook than any sentence you could write about Cassify.
2. **Write for a beginner.** Your whole positioning is *"less 'where do I even start?'"* — so never assume she knows what a framework is.
3. **Undergraduates, not MBAs.** Different vocabulary, different platforms (Instagram and WhatsApp, not LinkedIn), different fears.

## 1. The beginner answer — Unstop competition discussions (your best channel)

On the discussion section of any competition with registration open. **These are full of "first time, any tips?" and almost nobody answers properly.**

> First competition? The thing nobody tells you is that judges aren't looking for the right answer — most of these cases don't have one. They're looking at whether your thinking is organised.
>
> **What that means practically:**
>
> **1. Break the problem into parts before you solve anything.** If sales are up and profits are down, the answer is in costs — you know that before you look at a single number, because profit is revenue minus cost. That's the whole skill: knowing where to look.
>
> **2. Say your structure out loud at the start.** "I'll look at three things: X, Y, Z." Judges can follow you, and it stops you wandering.
>
> **3. Pick one recommendation and commit to it.** The most common way teams lose is hedging — presenting three options and not choosing. A clear wrong answer scores better than a vague right one.
>
> **4. Write the recommendation slide first.** Then build backwards. If you can't write it on day two, you don't have an angle yet, you have a topic.
>
> **5. Use the numbers you're given.** Teams routinely build a beautiful argument and never touch the data in the brief. Judges notice immediately.
>
> If it helps, there's a free 15-minute case you can try to get the feel of it — a campus café where sales are up and profits are down (cassify.in, I built it, so weigh that accordingly). But the five points above are the actual content here.
>
> Good luck — genuinely, the first one is the hardest and everyone's first submission is bad.

**Why this works:** it's complete and useful with the link deleted, it answers the real question (*am I going to embarrass myself?*), and that last line is why a nervous first-timer will click.
