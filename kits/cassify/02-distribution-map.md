# Cassify — Distribution map

**Read this first: do not spend any of these channels until the build in `04` ships.**

Your site currently has no account and saves nothing. A visitor who arrives, does the coffee shop case, and finds it forgotten is a user you have spent and lost — and she is on your campus, in your club, talking to the exact people you need next. **You get one first impression per campus and per club, and right now you'd be spending it on a demo.**

So this map is written for **days 8–30**, after one case works end to end.

The good news: **your audience is the undergraduate case-competition circuit, which nine of your ten cohort-mates are ignoring** in favour of MBA consulting candidates. You are not competing for channel space with them.

**Your window in the [cluster schedule](../../shared/case-prep-cluster.md) is days 9–13**, which lines up well with finishing the build first.

Reach estimates state their basis. **Verify before planning around them.**

## Tier 1 — The undergraduate competition circuit 🔴

### 1. Unstop — where your users are, at the moment they need you

[Unstop lists 21,000+ competitions](https://unstop.com/competitions) and is the central registry for Indian campus case competitions. Individual pages carry **100–400 registrants** — [Case Twist at NSUT Delhi had 319](https://unstop.com/competitions/case-twist-netaji-subhas-university-of-technology-nsut-delhi-1651181/amp), [Case Crusade 6.0 at Hindu College DU had 120](https://unstop.com/competitions/case-crusade-60-zeitgeist-2026-hindu-college-du-new-delhi-1650329/amp), [Startup Expo at IIT Delhi had 196](https://unstop.com/competitions/startup-expo-case-competition-iit-delhi-1740065/amp).

**Those registrants are overwhelmingly undergraduates who have just committed to a case competition and don't know where to start. That is your user, identified by name, in public, at the exact right moment.**

| Play | Reach | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Competition discussion sections** | 50–300 per competition, very high intent | Full of *"first time doing this, any tips?"* **Answer that question properly** — the structure of a case competition, what judges look for, what to do in week one. Then mention Level 01 is free. |
| **The WhatsApp/Discord groups around each competition** | 50–400 each | The organiser usually makes one. Join as a participant, be useful, share the free case when someone's stuck. |
| **Filter for competitions with registration still open** | — | Someone who registered yesterday needs you. Someone on day 8 of 9 doesn't. |
| **Enter a few yourself** | — | Credibility you can't buy, plus direct access to every other team. |

### 2. Campus case societies and competition organisers

Your own copy names them: *"Case competitions ✳ Consulting clubs ✳ Business classrooms."* These clubs run beginner sessions for new members every term and have nothing structured to hand them.

**Your level ladder is the structured beginner path they don't have.**

| Target | Realistic | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Undergraduate commerce/management college societies** — SRCC, Hindu, Hansraj, Christ, NMIMS, SIMSREE, St. Xavier's, Symbiosis, Jai Hind, Narsee Monjee | 30 DMs → 10–15 replies → **4–8 who share it** | **The real volume in Indian case competitions**, and almost nobody is building for them. Each is 100–400 members. |
| **Competition organisers** | 20 DMs → 6–10 replies → 3–5 | Offer Level 01 free to every registered participant — it makes their competition better at no cost |
| **Business-school clubs' junior cohorts** | 15 DMs → 5–8 replies | First-years are exactly your Rookie level |
| **Instagram pages of campus societies** | 300–5,000 per post | **Undergraduate case culture lives on Instagram, not LinkedIn.** DM the page — they're students and they reply. Offer a ready-made story graphic. |

**The campus leaderboard is your lever here, once it's real.** A society that turns it on gets friendly competition inside its own cohort, which is a thing a society genuinely wants — and it makes your product spread inside the club without you.

## Tier 3 — Don't 🔵

- **Institutional/university sales, this month.** Real motion, wrong month. **No university buys a concept prototype**, and a premature pitch closes a door you want open in March.
- **MBA consulting-interview communities.** Nine cohort-mates there and it isn't your market.
- **Product Hunt, Hacker News, X/Twitter.** Wrong audience entirely.
- **Any public launch before the build ships.** The one genuinely costly mistake available to you.
- **Paid anything.** Budget is zero.

**The honest read, and it's the bluntest in this cluster.**

**With three weeks of channel time instead of four, and one working level instead of four, 1,000 signups is unlikely.** A fair expectation is **300–700**, and that would be a genuinely good month given where you're starting.

**Say that to the board in week 1.** The alternative — launching everything on day 2 against a demo that saves nothing — would produce a worse number *and* burn your campus and your clubs, which are the channels that matter for the next two years.
