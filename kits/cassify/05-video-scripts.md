# Cassify — Video scripts and shot lists

Scripts and shot lists only — **you record these on your phone.**

**Your audience is undergraduates and they are on Instagram**, which makes Reels your single most natural channel — more natural than for any other project in this cluster. It also means your video job is easy, because **you already have the perfect script: your own case prompts.**

> *"Sales are up. Profits are down. Where should this campus café look first?"*

That's a question a nineteen-year-old can answer, wants to answer, and will argue about in the comments. **That's a Reel.**

## Format A — The case prompt reel (your workhorse; make 12)

**Length:** 15–22 seconds · **No face needed. No voice needed.** · **Where:** Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, WhatsApp status

### Template

| Time | On screen |
| --- | --- |
| 0–3s | **"Sales are up."** |
| 3–5s | **"Profits are down."** |
| 5–8s | **"Where do you look first?"** |
| 8–11s | *(beat — nothing moves, let them think)* |
| 11–17s | **"Costs. Profit = revenue − cost. If revenue's up and profit's down, the answer is on the cost side."** |
| 17–20s | **"You just did the most useful thing in business analysis."** |
| 20–22s | **"Free 15-min case → link in bio"** |

### Rules that make this work

- **The beat at 8 seconds is the most important part of the video.** Three seconds of nothing, so she answers in her head. A viewer who answers is a viewer who taps.
- **Give away the whole answer.** The lesson is the product sample. A teaser teaches nothing and gets shared by nobody.
- **"You just did the most useful thing in business analysis" is the payoff** — it's a compliment, and compliments get screenshotted. This audience is nervous about whether they're any good; telling her she just did something real is the whole emotional job.
- **Under 22 seconds.** Completion rate is the algorithm.
- **No face, no voice** — which means you can batch twelve in one afternoon.

### The twelve prompts

Start with the two you already have, then build out. Each needs one specific insight, not a framework.

| # | Prompt | The insight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Sales up, profits down — campus café | Profit = revenue − cost. Look at costs. |
| 2 | A delivery startup, one budget, three cities | Don't pick the biggest market. Pick where you can win. |
| 3 | A competitor just stole your customers | Ask *why they switched* before you cut prices. |
| 4 | A café is full every evening and still losing money | Full ≠ profitable. Check what they're buying, not how many. |
| 5 | Revenue doubled, cash ran out | Growth consumes cash. Revenue isn't cash. |
| 6 | Two outlets, same menu, one loses money | Compare the inputs, not the outputs. |
| 7 | 10,000 app downloads, 200 users | An install isn't a user. |
| 8 | Everyone loves the product, nobody buys twice | Great first experience, broken second one. |
| 9 | How many cups of coffee does your campus drink daily? | Start from people, not from cups. |
| 10 | A sale doubled volume and killed profit | Volume up is easy. Margin is the question. |
| 11 | Your best salesperson leaves and revenue doesn't drop | It wasn't them — it was the territory. |
| 12 | A product sells out every week and the company is unhappy | Selling out means you priced too low. |

**Post daily from day 8.** With an Instagram audience this specific, one breakout reel does 20,000 views on its own — and you only get breakouts from volume.

### Shot list

1. **Text-only cards.** Your design is the best in this cohort — use it. Clean type on your brand colour, nothing else.
2. **One idea per card**, held long enough to read on a phone.
3. **Hold the final insight for two full seconds.** That's the screenshot.
4. **No music with lyrics.** A simple beat or silence.

## Format B — "Your first case competition" (make 1, the shareable one)

**Length:** 50–65 seconds · **Where:** Instagram, YouTube, Unstop comments, campus society pages

This is `03` #1 as video. It's useful on its own, which is why societies will share it.

> **(0–6s, your face)** "If you've signed up for your first case competition and you're quietly panicking — five things nobody tells you."
>
> **(6–18s, text: JUDGES AREN'T LOOKING FOR THE RIGHT ANSWER)** "One. Most of these cases don't have a right answer. Judges are looking at whether your thinking is organised. That's genuinely easier than knowing the answer, and nobody tells you."
>
> **(18–30s, text: SAY YOUR STRUCTURE OUT LOUD)** "Two. Start by saying what you're going to look at. 'Three things: X, Y, Z.' It helps them follow you and it stops you wandering."
>
> **(30–42s, text: COMMIT TO ONE RECOMMENDATION)** "Three. Pick one answer. The most common way teams lose is hedging — three options and no choice. A clear wrong answer scores better than a vague right one."
>
> **(42–52s, text: USE THE NUMBERS)** "Four. Use the data in the brief. Teams build a beautiful argument and never touch the numbers they were given. Judges notice in about thirty seconds."
>
> **(52–62s, your face)** "Five. Your first submission will be bad. Everyone's is. Do it anyway."
>
> **(62–65s, end card)** "Free first case → cassify.in"

**Why this is your best video:** it answers the real fear (*am I going to embarrass myself?*), it's generous, and the last line is the reason a nervous first-year sends it to a friend. **Pin it.**

## Format C — The level-up video (make 1, for the landing page)

**Length:** 35–45 seconds · **Where:** landing page, Peerlist, "how does it work" replies

Shows the path, which is your differentiator and hard to convey in text.

> **(0–6s, the ladder graphic — Rookie highlighted)** "Four levels. You start at Rookie, and the first one takes fifteen minutes."
>
> **(6–18s, Level 01 running, the café case)** "Rookie: learn to ask the right questions. Sales up, profits down, where do you look? No frameworks, nothing you're assumed to know."
>
> **(18–30s, Level 02)** "Analyst: find the story behind the numbers. A delivery startup with one budget and three cities."
>
> **(30–40s, the ladder advancing)** "Then Strategist and Contender — full competition simulations. You move up as you get better, not when you decide you're ready."
>
> **(40–45s)** "First two levels free. cassify.in"

**Only make this once the levels actually work** (`04` Fix 1). A video of an illustrated product is the thing you're trying to get away from.

## Format D — Society story graphics (not video, but make them first)

**Your Instagram DM offer in `03` #3 promises a ready-made story graphic.** Make it before you send the DMs — it's the line that converts those messages.

**Three 1080×1920 stories, designed in your brand:**

1. **"Sales are up. Profits are down. Where do you look?"** → swipe-up to Level 01
2. **"[Society name] — free first case, 15 minutes"** → template with space for their name
3. **"Your first case competition? Start here."** → the five tips from Format B as a single readable card

**Why these matter more than they look:** a society page needs content constantly, and you're handing them three posts. It costs you an hour and it's the difference between a yes and a "sure, send it over" that never happens.

## Format E — Comment-bait reel (weekly, from week 3)

Once you have an audience, use it. **Post a prompt with no answer.**

> **(0–4s)** **"A café is full every single evening and still losing money."**
> **(4–8s)** **"Where do you look?"**
> **(8–12s)** **"Comment your answer — I'll reply to every one."**
> **(12–15s)** **"Answer tomorrow."**

**Then actually reply to every comment**, and post the answer the next day.

Why this works harder than it looks: comments are the strongest ranking signal on Reels, replying to all of them multiplies it, and **answering a nineteen-year-old's business reasoning thoughtfully is exactly the product you sell.** You're demonstrating it in public for free.

## Production rules

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Nothing public before day 8** | A reel that sends people to a demo that forgets them is the one costly mistake |
| **Use your own design** | It's the best in this cohort — your text cards will out-perform anyone's talking head |
| **The pause before the answer** | Three seconds of nothing is what makes her participate |
| **Give away the full insight** | It's a sample of the product, not a teaser for it |
| **Under 22 seconds for Format A** | |
| **Compliment the viewer** | "You just did the most useful thing in business analysis" — this audience is unsure whether she's any good |
| **Instagram first, LinkedIn second** | Undergraduates aren't on LinkedIn in the volume MBAs are |
| **Post daily from day 8** | One breakout reel is worth twenty good ones, and volume is how you get one |

**Make Format D first** (the society graphics — they unlock your best channel), **then batch twelve Format A reels in one afternoon**, then Format B to pin. Format C waits until the product is real. Format E starts in week 3 once there's an audience to answer you.
