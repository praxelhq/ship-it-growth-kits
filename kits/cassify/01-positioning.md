## Read this first: you don't have a product yet, and your own site says so

Your footer reads **"Concept prototype · 2026"**. Elsewhere on the live page:

- *"Showcase demo — no account required. **Responses are not saved.**"*
- *"Original illustrative challenges. Times and XP show the **planned** learning experience."*
- *"**ILLUSTRATIVE PREVIEW**"* on the campus arena, with a "DEMO COHORT" leaderboard
- *"Cassify's institutional **vision**"*, *"A **planned** cohort view"*
- A footer link labelled **"Premium test checkout"** pointing at `billing-test.html`
- A link to `casify-two.vercel.app` — a preview deployment, linked from production
- Several `href="#"` dead links

**So: there is no account, nothing is saved, the XP and levels are illustrations, and the checkout is a test page.**

**You cannot run a signup campaign against this.** 1,000 signups in 30 days is not a hard target here — it is an impossible one, because there is nothing to sign up to. Driving traffic now would spend your channels, and your reputation on your own campus, on a demo that forgets the user the moment she leaves.

**This is the honest version of your kit, and it says: week 1 is not marketing. Week 1 is making one thing real.** `04` is that build list. Everything after it assumes you shipped it.

**Tell the board this directly rather than reporting against a number you can't hit.** It is a much better conversation than the one that happens in thirty days.

### And this is not a criticism of the work

**Your page is the best-designed one in this cohort of eleven**, by a distance. The positioning is sharper than most shipped products:

> *"Less 'where do I even start?' More 'what's my next challenge?' A clear path from curious to competition-ready."*

The level ladder (**The Rookie → The Analyst → The Strategist → The Contender**) is well-judged, the sample cases are genuinely good — *"Sales are up. Profits are down. Where should this campus café look first?"* is a better case prompt than most paid products ship — and the three named challenges are specific and appealing.

**You have done the hard thinking. What's missing is comparatively mechanical**, and it's a week of work, not a month.

## Who your first 1,000 users actually are

Worth knowing now so that week 1 builds the right thing.

**The undergraduate who has signed up for her first case competition and has no idea what she's supposed to do.**

In priority order:

1. **Undergraduate commerce and management students at Indian city colleges** — SRCC, Hindu, Hansraj, Christ, NMIMS, SIMSREE, St. Xavier's and dozens more. **This is the real volume in Indian case competitions**, not MBAs, and your "where do I even start" framing is written exactly for her. [Unstop lists thousands of competitions](https://unstop.com/competitions) and the entrants are overwhelmingly undergraduates.
2. **Members of campus consulting and case societies.** Your own copy names them: *"Case competitions ✳ Consulting clubs ✳ Business classrooms."* Clubs are the best channel in this cluster, and your levels + campus leaderboard are built for a cohort.
3. **First- and second-years with months ahead of them.** Your level ladder suits someone with a horizon, not someone with a submission on Friday.
4. **Institutions** — your "For institutions" motion. **Real, and a month 3 conversation, not a month 1 one.** No university buys a concept prototype.

**Who is not your user:** the MBA consulting-interview candidate. Nine cohort-mates serve her and several are further along. **Your audience is undergraduate case *competitions*, which is a genuinely different and less contested market.** Stay there.

## The one-line pitch

Yours is already good. Keep it and add the audience:

> **A clear path from curious to competition-ready. Start at Rookie, learn to ask the right questions, and work up to full case simulations.**

For an undergraduate who just registered for her first competition:

> **Signed up for a case competition and don't know where to start? Level 01 is free and takes 15 minutes.**

## The wedge use case

**"The coffee shop conundrum."**

Your own Level 01: *"Sales are up. Profits are down. Where should this campus café look first?"* — 15 minutes, free, a campus café, no jargon.

**That is a perfect first case** and it should be the entire homepage. A nineteen-year-old who has never done a case can understand the question, has an opinion about it, and learns the single most useful idea in business analysis (revenue up, profit down → look at costs) in a quarter of an hour.

**So the one thing to build in week 1 is that case, working, end to end, with the response saved and a score at the end.** Not four levels. Not XP. Not the leaderboard. **One case that remembers her.**

## Why someone picks you over the alternatives

Worth being honest about, because it shapes what to build:

1. **Case Room** (`caseroom.me`), in your own cohort, also targets case competitions — but **teams and collaboration**, where you're **solo and gamified**. Genuinely different halves of the same market. Worth a conversation with them rather than a race.
2. **The other nine case-prep projects** aim at MBA consulting interviews. **You're not really competing with them**, which is a better position than most of them have.
3. **A senior's PDF and a YouTube video.** Your real competitor for the undergraduate. You beat it on structure and progression — *"where do I even start"* is her actual question and nobody answers it.
4. **Nothing.** Most undergraduates in their first competition. Enormous share.

**Your differentiation is real and it's the ladder.** Nobody else tells a complete beginner what to do first. Protect that and don't drift toward MBA interview prep, where you'd be the eleventh option.

## What NOT to say

- **Don't launch publicly before the build.** You get one first impression on your own campus and in each club. **A demo that forgets the user burns both.**
- **Don't pitch institutions this month.** No university buys a concept prototype, and a premature pitch closes a door you'll want in March.
- **Don't say "gamified learning platform".** Say *"start at Rookie"* and name the coffee shop case.
- **Don't chase MBA consulting candidates.** Nine cohort-mates are there and your real market is less contested.
- **Don't build all four levels this month.** Build one, completely. Then the second.
