# CaseCrack — Video scripts and shot lists

Scripts and shot lists only — **you record these on your phone.**

Your video strategy is different from everyone else's in this cluster, because you're selling a **habit** rather than a tool. That means your main format isn't one polished launch video — it's **thirty short ones**, posted daily, which are also your distribution (`02`, Tier 1.2).

All vertical 9:16, captions burned in.

---

## Format A — The daily streak clip (your main asset; make 30)

**Length:** 20–30 seconds · **Where:** LinkedIn, Instagram, WhatsApp status · **Cadence:** every single day

This is the written streak post from `03` #2, on camera. **Do whichever you'll actually sustain** — but video outperforms text on both platforms, and ninety seconds of filming is less work than writing a good post.

### Template

| Time | On screen | You say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3s | Your face. A number card: **"DAY 14"** | "Day 14. One case, fifteen minutes." |
| 3–9s | Screen recording: the brief | "Regional airline, load factor down 6%, competitors flat." |
| 9–16s | Your structure being typed or spoken | "I went demand side first — route mix, pricing, seasonality." |
| 16–24s | **The follow-up appearing on screen. Hold it.** | "And it asked whether they'd added capacity before I blamed demand. They had. I hadn't checked." |
| 24–30s | The score. URL captioned. | "71. Day 15 tomorrow. casecrack.live" |

### Rules that make this work

- **The follow-up is the shot.** Hold it long enough to read. That four seconds is your entire product and nothing else you film will outperform it.
- **Post the bad scores.** A 64 is worth more than an 88. You are selling a tool that judges people's thinking — the only way to be trusted is to be judged badly in public.
- **Keep the number card.** "DAY 14" is what makes people follow rather than watch. By day 22 the streak itself is the reason they're there.
- **Film it right after you do the case**, in whatever state you're in. A tired founder at 11pm is more convincing than a staged one.
- **Ninety seconds of work, every day, no exceptions.** Thirty mediocre clips beat four good ones for a habit product.

### Shot list

1. **Talking head, propped phone, window light** (or a lamp — it's 11pm, and that's fine and on-message).
2. **Screen recording** of the brief, your answer, the follow-up, the score. Real session, real score.
3. **No editing beyond trimming and captions.** Polish actively hurts here.

## Format B — The "question after the question" reel (make 8)

**Length:** 15–20 seconds · **No face. No voice needed.** Pure text on screen.

Your most shareable format, because it's a tiny lesson rather than a product demo.

### Template

| Time | On screen |
| --- | --- |
| 0–3s | **"A regional airline's load factor is down 6%. Competitors are flat."** |
| 3–7s | **"Most people: 'let's look at the demand side.'"** |
| 7–13s | **"The question after the question: did they add capacity in that period?"** |
| 13–17s | **"If they did, demand isn't your problem. You just spent ten minutes in the wrong place."** |
| 17–20s | **"casecrack.live"** |

### The eight to make

1. **Load factor / capacity** — the one above
2. **Declining profits** — did revenue fall or did costs rise? Most people never ask.
3. **Market entry** — can we win, or just: is the market big? Different question.
4. **Pricing** — what does the customer's *alternative* cost, not what does it cost us
5. **New product cannibalisation** — is this new revenue or moved revenue?
6. **A guesstimate** — sanity-check your answer against one known number before you commit
7. **Falling market share** — did we lose customers or did the market grow faster than us?
8. **Cost reduction** — which costs are actually variable? Most "savings" aren't.

**Why this format works:** each one teaches something real in fifteen seconds, which makes it shareable to people who will never use your product — and that's how a product with no budget gets reach. It also demonstrates your differentiator (the follow-up) without ever mentioning a feature.

## Format C — The 15-minute proof video

**Length:** 60–75 seconds · **Where:** landing page next to the CTA (`04` Fix 3), Peerlist, reply to "how long does it take"

Your biggest credibility problem is that **a seven-step flow does not look like fifteen minutes.** This video exists solely to answer that, and it's the one video on this list you should make carefully.

> **(0–6s, your face)** "Seven steps. Fifteen minutes. People don't believe that, so here's one, with a timer, nothing cut."
> **(6–14s)** Screen: the brief, timer running at 0:00. "Brief — one minute."
> **(14–26s)** Scoping questions. "Scope. Two minutes. These never count against your free items, which is deliberate — asking good questions shouldn't cost you."
> **(26–40s)** Structure typed, then a voice note recorded. "Structure, three minutes. You can type it or just say it — I usually say it, it's closer to the real round."
> **(40–54s)** Investigate and analyse, exhibits. "The numbers. Four minutes."
> **(54–66s)** Recommendation, then the follow-up pushing back. **Hold the follow-up.** "Recommend. And then the bit that actually matters."
> **(66–75s)** The score, and **the timer visible: 13:42.** "Thirteen forty-two. Score 74. That's the whole thing."

**The timer is the video.** If your real run is 26 minutes, don't fake it — go fix the items or change the claim on your page, then film it again. This video is a promise and your first user checks it.

## Format D — The honest comparison (make 1, post in week 3)

**Length:** 40–50 seconds · **Where:** LinkedIn, Reddit comments, replies to "why not just use ChatGPT"

ChatGPT is your real competitor and pretending otherwise insults the audience.

> **(0–6s, your face)** "Why not just ask ChatGPT for a case? Honest answer, including where it wins."
>
> **(6–16s, split screen: ChatGPT on one side, your product on the other)** "It wins on breadth. Infinite cases, any industry, right now, free. I'm not going to pretend otherwise."
>
> **(16–30s, screen: a generic AI asking about a data point that was never given)** "Where it lost me: it asked me about a figure that wasn't in the case. Not once — repeatedly. After that I stopped trusting any score it gave me, which made the whole exercise pointless."
>
> **(30–42s, your product's follow-up)** "So mine can't do that. Follow-ups are anchored to the case's approved facts. Same rubric every attempt, so last week's score and this week's mean the same thing."
>
> **(42–50s)** "If you're disciplined enough to hold a consistent rubric in your head, genuinely use ChatGPT. I wasn't. casecrack.live"

**Why this works:** conceding the competitor's real advantage is what makes the rest believable. The last line gives people permission not to use you, which is the single most persuasive thing you can say to a sceptical audience.

## Format E — Discord thread companion clips (optional, weekly)

Once your daily Discord guesstimate thread is running (`03` #1), film a 25-second feedback summary once a week.

> **(0–5s)** "Week 2 of the guesstimate thread. 40 answers this week."
> **(5–18s)** "The split: most of you went population-first. The ones who got closest went supply-side — café count rather than coffee drinkers. Worth knowing that supply-side is sometimes the shorter path."
> **(18–25s)** "Thread's daily in [server]. Full items at casecrack.live."

**This is the only content in your kit built from other people's work**, which makes it the most interesting and the most likely to be shared by the participants themselves.

## Production rules

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Vertical 9:16, captions burned in** | Muted autoplay on every surface you post to |
| **Daily beats polished** | You're selling a habit. Thirty rough clips prove it; four polished ones don't. |
| **The follow-up moment is the money shot** | Hold it long enough to read, every single time |
| **Post your bad scores** | The only credible way to sell a tool that judges thinking |
| **Real timers only** | Format C is a promise your first user will verify |
| **Full URL `casecrack.live` captioned**, never spoken | Three cohort-mates have near-identical names |
| **No logo intro, no music under speech** | Reads as an ad, gets suppressed as one |

**Make Format C first** — it answers your biggest objection and it goes on the landing page. **Then start Format A on day 1 and never miss a day**; the streak is simultaneously your best content, your best proof, and your main distribution channel. Batch-record four Format B reels in one sitting on day 6 so you always have something to post when a day collapses.
