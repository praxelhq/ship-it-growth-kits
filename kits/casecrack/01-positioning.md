# CaseCrack — Positioning

**Live:** https://casecrack.live/ · **Category:** Education · **Audience:** B2C → **1,000 signups in 30 days** ([`TARGET-POLICY.md`](../../TARGET-POLICY.md)) · **Profile:** https://shipped.money/p/casecrack

---

## You are competing with nine near-identical products. Here is the half-inch of difference, and it's real.

**Ten other projects in this Ship It cohort sell case-interview practice.** Four of them have names within one word of yours. Several have the same AI-interviewer-plus-rubric architecture. Member counts and feature lists will not separate you.

Two things in your product do, and both are already written on your page:

**1. Fifteen minutes, not ninety.** Your own copy: *"Practise realistic consulting cases in 5–15 minutes a day."* Everyone else sells a full case simulation — a 30–45 minute commitment that a student in placement season does roughly twice and then abandons. **You are the only one selling a daily habit**, and a habit is a fundamentally different product with different retention, different copy and different channels.

**2. The interviewer pushes back, and it can't make things up.** Your copy again: *"After each answer you submit, the interviewer responds to the substance of your reasoning"* and — crucially — *"Follow-ups are anchored to the item's approved facts — never invented outside the content."*

**That second clause is the most valuable sentence on your website and I doubt you think of it as marketing.** Every competitor in this market generates confident nonsense when the model runs out of facts. You built a constraint against it and you label scripted flows as scripted. To a student who has been told by an AI tool that she missed a data point that never existed, that's not a footnote — it's the reason to trust your score.

## Who your first 1,000 users actually are

**The consulting aspirant who has decided to practise every day, and keeps failing to, because every tool asks for forty minutes she doesn't have.**

In priority order:

1. **First-year MBA students in the middle of summer placement season** with a shortlist in the next three weeks. She has four hours a day of commitments and a case round coming. **Fifteen minutes is the only offer she can actually accept.**
2. **The pre-MBA or final-year undergraduate building the skill over months**, not weeks. She's your best retention cohort by a distance — she has time, no deadline panic, and a genuine interest in getting good. **She is also the one who survives into February**, which makes her disproportionately valuable.
3. **Working professionals preparing for lateral consulting interviews.** Fewest in number, most likely to pay, and the ones for whom "voice note on a commute" is a feature rather than a curiosity.
4. **The guesstimate-specific practiser.** You have guesstimates *and* drills, not just full cases — a wedge nobody else in the cluster occupies properly, and the thing people search for by name.

**Who you shouldn't chase:** the non-consulting functional candidate (CaseForge owns her), the GD candidate (Role.via owns her), and the CAT aspirant (Skill Prep owns her, and she isn't buying case practice four weeks before her entrance exam). The cluster is genuinely big enough for all of you if you each stay in your lane.

## The one-line pitch

Lead with the time, because it's the differentiator and it's also the objection:

> **One case in fifteen minutes a day, with an interviewer that actually pushes back on your reasoning.**

Shorter, for WhatsApp:

> **Case practice in 15 min a day. The interviewer argues back. Five free: casecrack.live**

For the trust-led version, which works better on anyone who has already tried an AI tool and been annoyed by it:

> **An AI case interviewer that can't invent facts. Follow-ups are anchored to the case's approved data — so when it says you missed something, you actually missed it.**

## The wedge use case

**Fifteen minutes before bed, every day, for three weeks.**

Not "prepare for your case interview". That's what nine other products say and it invites a forty-minute mental price tag.

Your wedge is the smallest unit of real practice that still produces a scored, debriefed result: **one scoped item, typed or spoken, with follow-ups, and a rubric at the end — finished before she loses interest.**

The emotional moment you're selling is **the follow-up that catches her out.** She submits a structure she's pleased with, and the interviewer asks the question after the question — the one a real partner would ask. That's a specific, memorable, shareable four seconds. **Build every piece of copy around it**, because it's the part no static case library can reproduce.

## Why someone picks you over the alternatives

1. **The other nine in this cohort.** You beat them on session length and on the no-invented-facts constraint. You will not beat them on library size — *"growing towards 200 items"* is honest, and honest is the right call, but don't compete on volume.
2. **ChatGPT.** Free, and your real competitor. You beat it on: a consistent rubric so last week's score and this week's mean the same thing, follow-ups that can't drift outside the case's facts, and a scored debrief you don't have to prompt for. **Say exactly that.** A student who has tried and found it mushy is your easiest convert, and she's most of the market.
3. **A case partner.** Better than you when she has one, and she usually doesn't — not at 11pm, not daily, not for fifteen minutes. **Don't claim to replace a good partner.** Claim to be what she does on the twenty-six days a month she can't find one. It's true, and the honesty converts.
4. **A casebook PDF.** Free, trusted, passive. It can't tell her whether her answer was any good, which is her only actual question.

## Your three weaknesses, in order

**1. No pricing, anywhere.** Your page offers *"five free practice items · unlimited retries"* and then says nothing about what happens next. For a student deciding whether to build a daily habit, that silence reads as: *this will ask me for money at the worst possible moment.* Pick a number and publish it (`04` Fix 2). Free is also a legitimate answer — just say which.

**2. No proof at all.** No testimonial, no user count, no named student, no visible scorecard. You're asking someone to trust an AI's judgement of her thinking with nothing to suggest anyone has found that judgement sound. `04` Fix 1, and it's a week-1 task because it depends on other people.

**3. The name.** `casecrack.live` lives alongside `caseforge.in`, `caseforgeprep.in` and `caseflex.app` in your own cohort. A student who hears "CaseCrack" in a WhatsApp group and types it into Google may well land on one of the others. **Always post the full URL `casecrack.live`, never just the brand name** — in every message, every comment, every caption.

## What NOT to say

- **Don't say "AI-powered case interview platform".** All ten of you say that. Say "fifteen minutes" and "it argues back".
- **Don't compete on library size.** You'll lose, and your honest *"growing towards 200"* is better positioning than a fake number anyway.
- **Don't claim to replace a human case partner.** Overclaiming here is instantly recognisable to anyone who has had a good partner.
- **Don't bury the no-invented-facts constraint in a privacy note.** It's your best trust asset. Put it on the homepage (`04` Fix 4).
- **Don't drift into non-consulting functions, GD, or CAT prep.** Three cohort-mates own those, do it better, and you'd be the second-best option in all three.
