# CaseCrack — Landing page conversion fixes

**Do these before you drive traffic.** Your window in the cluster schedule is days 13–17, so you have time to get the page right first — use it.

**Your page is well built.** Strong H1 (*"Don't memorise cases. Train your thinking."*), a specific subheadline, a clear seven-step flow (Brief → Scope → Structure → Investigate → Analyse → Recommend → Feedback), a real explanation of Learn vs Mock modes, no dead `href="#"` links, and consent handled properly before voice recording. The problems are three omissions and one buried asset.

---

## Fix 1 — You have no proof at all. This is the conversion problem. 🔴

No testimonial. No user count. No named student. No visible scorecard.

You are asking someone to trust an AI's judgement of her thinking, and there is nothing on the page suggesting any human has found that judgement sound. Your headline promises to *train her thinking* — her first question is *"says who?"*

**This week, get three named students to run five items and tell you what happened.** Then put them above the fold with real names and campuses:

> **"The follow-up asked whether I'd checked capacity before blaming demand. I hadn't. That's exactly what my BCG interviewer did to me four days later."**
> — [Name], [campus]

Ask your own consulting club first, and your seniors — someone who already has an offer has nothing to lose by lending their name and is the most credible voice you can get.

**Second-best, available today without anyone's permission:** your own streak. *"I've run 18 cases on this in 18 days. Here's my score curve."* A founder who publicly uses the thing daily is real proof, and `02` makes that your main mechanic anyway.

**Do not invent a testimonial.** This cohort is small, the campuses are interconnected, and one fake quote found out ends the month.

---

## Fix 2 — Publish a price, or say "free" 🔴

Your page offers *"five free practice items · unlimited retries"* and then **nothing**. No price, no plan, no "free forever", no "paid coming soon".

To a student deciding whether to invest a daily habit, silence reads as: *this will ask me for money at the worst possible moment, probably the week of my interview.* That doubt is invisible in your analytics and it's costing you signups right now.

**Pick one and publish it:**

> **Free** — 5 items, unlimited retries, scoping questions always free
> **Pro — ₹[number]/month** — full library, all modes, progress tracking

₹299–₹499/month is the right band for this audience — roughly what one casebook costs, well under any coaching programme. **Or genuinely commit to free for now** and say *"free while the library is growing — no card, no trial countdown."* That's a perfectly good answer and it's honest given your library size.

**Either is dramatically better than an unanswered question.**

---

## Fix 3 — Prove the fifteen minutes, don't claim it 🔴

*"5–15 minutes a day"* is your entire differentiation against nine near-identical competitors — and a visitor looking at a seven-step flow (Brief, Scope, Structure, Investigate, Analyse, Recommend, Feedback) does not believe you. Seven steps looks like forty minutes.

**Two things to do:**

1. **Put a timer in the product and show it on the page.** *"Average item: 11 minutes."* Real number, measured from real sessions. If it's actually 23 minutes, **either fix the items or change the claim** — this audience is counting minutes in placement season and will notice.
2. **Annotate the flow with times.** `Brief 1m · Scope 2m · Structure 3m · Investigate 2m · Analyse 3m · Recommend 2m · Feedback 1m`. Suddenly the seven steps support your claim instead of undermining it.

**And add a 30-second screen recording of one complete item**, timer visible, next to the CTA. It answers the only real objection you have.

---

## Fix 4 — Move the no-invented-facts constraint to the homepage 🟠

Right now this is buried in your product copy:

> *"Follow-ups are anchored to the item's approved facts — never invented outside the content."*
> *"Scripted flows are labelled as scripted, and hints stay off in Mock mode."*

**This is the most valuable sentence you've written and it's the most defensible claim in the cluster.** Every competitor's AI eventually asks about a data point that doesn't exist, and once a student has experienced that twice she stops trusting the score — which makes the whole product pointless.

Put it above the fold as a claim, in her language:

> **It can't make things up.** Follow-ups come from the case's approved data, not the model's imagination. If it says you missed something, you missed it.

A student who has been annoyed by a general AI tool will recognise that instantly, and she's most of your market.

---

## Fix 5 — Make the follow-up the hero moment 🟠

Your best emotional moment is *"the question after the question"* — she submits a structure she's pleased with and the interviewer asks what a real partner would ask.

**Show it, verbatim, above the fold.** Not described. Quoted:

> **You:** "I'd look at the demand side first — route mix, pricing, seasonality."
> **Interviewer:** "Before we go there — did they add capacity in that period? If they did, your load factor is down for a reason that has nothing to do with demand."
> **→ That's the question after the question.**

Four lines, and it sells the product better than every feature on your page. Use a real exchange from a real item.

---

## Fix 6 — Add an `og:image` 🟠

You don't have one. Every time your link is pasted into a WhatsApp group, a Discord channel or a LinkedIn post, the preview renders with no picture.

**Your two main channels are Discord posts and a daily LinkedIn streak** — both of which are links in feeds. Twenty minutes of work.

Make the card the thing you sell: 1200×630 with the follow-up exchange from Fix 5 as text, and `casecrack.live`.

---

## Fix 7 — Turn library honesty into a feature 🔵

*"A quality-checked library of cases, guesstimates and drills — growing towards 200 items in reviewed batches"* is honest, and honest is right. But as written it reads as *not finished yet*.

Reframe so the smallness is the point:

> **Every item is reviewed before it ships.** We're growing towards 200 in checked batches rather than generating a thousand — because a case with a wrong number in it teaches you the wrong thing.

Same fact, and now it's a quality claim against competitors who do generate a thousand. Add: *"Ran out of a type you need? Tell me and it moves up the queue."* That turns your weakness into a reason to talk to you.

---

## Fix 8 — Two small things 🔵

- **Lead with Google sign-in and make the phone number clearly optional.** Your signup collects email, offers Google OAuth, and has an optional phone field. **Any phone field on a student signup form reads as "they will call me".** Label it optional or drop it this month.
- **Test a full item on a phone, on mobile data, including a voice note.** Your fifteen-minutes-before-bed use case is a phone in a dark room. If recording a voice note fails on mobile Safari, your most distinctive feature is broken for half your traffic and you won't hear about it.

You have until day 13 before your cluster window opens. **Spend days 1–7 on the page and on starting your public streak** — by the time you post publicly you'll have both proof and a page worth sending people to.

## The test to run before you launch

**Give your phone to someone who has never seen CaseCrack and say nothing.** Three things to watch:

1. Does she understand within ten seconds that this is fifteen minutes rather than ninety?
2. Does she start an item without asking you what it costs?
3. Does she get to a score?

If all three, launch on day 13. If not, that's where the rest of the week goes — and it's worth more than any post you could make instead.
