# Case Forge — Copy pack

Ready to post. **Change the details only you know** — your campus, your own hunt for a partner, the real number of sessions run.

Three rules throughout:

1. **Don't post anything public until the page exists** (`04` Fix 1). A visitor who arrives at 295 characters and a login button leaves, and in most of these places you only get one good first post.
2. **Always ask for a specific time, never a signup.** "Thursday 9pm" produces a match. "Sign up and get matched" produces an empty match screen.
3. **Always post the full URL `caseforgeprep.in`.** `caseforge.in` is a different product in your own cohort, one word from your name, with a stronger search position.

## 7. Reddit — r/MBA (days 11–15, once)

Lead with the useful thing. **And be aware a Reddit spike brings you scattered individuals you can't match** — so write this for awareness and for club secretaries, not for signups.

**Title:** `How to find a case partner and actually keep one (the scheduling thing nobody mentions)`

> Everyone's advice is "do cases with a partner". Nobody tells you how to find one or why it falls apart, so here's what I learned doing it badly.
>
> **Why partnerships die, in order:** scheduling, not commitment. Two people agree to do cases, manage three in a fortnight, then one gets a shortlist and it ends. Second reason: **mismatched levels.** If one of you is three months ahead, the sessions are charity in one direction and demoralising in the other, and both of you quietly stop.
>
> **What actually works:**
>
> 1. **A fixed recurring time, not arranged sessions.** Same two evenings every week, default yes. Arranging each one is the thing that kills it.
> 2. **Match on level, not friendship.** Your closest friend is usually the wrong partner.
> 3. **Swap roles every time.** Running a case teaches you more about what a good answer sounds like than doing one.
> 4. **Have a third person.** Two people is fragile; three means it survives one person's shortlist week.
> 5. **Decide the cases in advance.** "What should we do today" burns ten minutes and sometimes the whole session.
>
> I built a matching tool because of point 2 (link in comments, I'm the founder, judge accordingly). But points 1, 3 and 4 are free and they're most of the benefit — and if you've already got a partner you don't need anything else.
>
> **If you run a consulting club**, point 4 scales: a pool of twelve people who all show up on the same evening solves this permanently for everyone in it.
