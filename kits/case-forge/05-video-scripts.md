# Case Forge — Video scripts and shot lists

Scripts and shot lists only — **you record these on your phone.**

You have one piece of footage no other project in this cluster can get: **two real students doing a case together.** Two faces, one talking, one thinking, a structure being drawn. It explains your product in two seconds with no words.

**Get consent from both people before you record anything**, every time. Blur or cut anyone who declines. One complaint about being filmed without permission ends your standing on a campus where your entire product lives.

All vertical 9:16, captions burned in.

---

## Video 1 — The hook (your most important asset)

**Length:** 22–30 seconds · **Where:** campus WhatsApp groups, LinkedIn, Instagram, the landing page

### Script

| Time | On screen | You say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–4s | Your face, close | "I read the casebook four times and did two cases." |
| 4–9s | Still your face | "Not because I was lazy. Because doing a case needs another person, and finding that person is the actual hard part." |
| 9–17s | **Split screen: two students mid-case. One talking, one writing a structure. Real audio for two seconds.** | "So: you get matched with someone at your level, you do a live case, and you both get feedback after." |
| 17–24s | The feedback report | "Thirty minutes. One of you interviews, then you swap." |
| 24–30s | Your face. Text on screen: **Thursday 9pm** | "Thursday, 9pm. caseforgeprep.in" |

### Shot list

1. **Talking head, 0–9s.** Propped phone, eye level, window light, quiet room.
2. **The two-person case, 9–17s — this is the video.** Screen-record a real session, or film two friends at a table. **Let two seconds of real audio through** — someone mid-sentence, slightly unsure. That's the texture an AI demo can't fake.
3. **The feedback report**, zoomed so it's readable on a phone.
4. **End frame with the day and time as text**, not a signup prompt.

### Rules

- **The first nine seconds are the whole video.** "Finding that person is the actual hard part" is a problem everyone has and nobody has articulated. Open on it.
- **End with a day and a time, never "sign up".** Your product needs attendance, not registrations. Every video in this kit ends with Thursday 9pm.
- **Show two humans.** Your entire differentiation against ten competitors is that the other person is real. Any frame without a second person is a wasted frame.
- No logo intro, no music under speech.

## Video 2 — "Why your case partner arrangement died" (your shareable asset)

**Length:** 45–60 seconds · **Where:** LinkedIn, Reddit comments, "looking for a case partner" threads, YouTube

This is `03` #7 as video. It's useful on its own, which is why people who'll never use your product will still send it to someone.

> **(0–6s, your face)** "If you've ever had a case partner arrangement fall apart, it wasn't a commitment problem. Here's what actually kills them."
>
> **(6–18s, text on screen: SCHEDULING)** "One. Scheduling. You agree to do cases, you manage three in a fortnight, then one of you gets a shortlist and it quietly ends. The thing that kills it is arranging each session — every single time you have to decide when, you might not."
>
> **(18–32s, text: LEVELS)** "Two. Mismatched levels. If one of you is three months ahead, it's charity in one direction and demoralising in the other. Both of you stop, and neither says why."
>
> **(32–44s, text: FIXES)** "What works: a fixed recurring time you both default to, instead of arranging. Match on level, not friendship — your closest friend is usually the wrong partner. Swap roles every time. And have a third person, so it survives one person's shortlist week."
>
> **(44–56s, the two-person session footage)** "I built the matching because of the levels thing. But the fixed-time part is free and it's most of the benefit."
>
> **(56–60s)** "caseforgeprep.in. Thursdays and Tuesdays, 9pm."

**Why this is your best video:** it's a diagnosis, not a pitch. Everyone in your audience has lived this exact failure and nobody has named it for them. **Pin it to your LinkedIn profile.**

## Video 3 — The campus recruitment video (made for one group chat)

**Length:** 20–25 seconds · **Where:** your batch group, section groups, other campuses' club groups

Shot specifically to be forwarded inside one campus. **Remake it for each campus you open** — the specificity is the whole point.

> **(0–5s, you, on campus, somewhere recognisable)** "There are three hundred people on this campus doing consulting prep and we can't find each other."
>
> **(5–13s, the two-person session footage)** "Thursday 9pm, you get matched with someone from our batch at your level. Thirty-minute case, feedback after."
>
> **(13–20s, you)** "I need about ten people for it to work. Reply 👍 in the group and I'll send you the link."
>
> **(20–25s, text: **THURSDAY 9PM** · caseforgeprep.in)**

**Why it converts:** it's visibly on their campus, it names their number, and it asks for a reaction rather than a signup. **"I need about ten people for it to work" is the most persuasive line available to you** — it turns a user into a participant in making the thing work.

## Video 4 — The club secretary video

**Length:** 40–50 seconds · **Where:** attached to the DMs in `03` #3

Different audience, different register. He doesn't care about your founder story — he cares that pairing members for mocks is an admin job he does badly by hand.

> **(0–8s, you, seated, shirt)** "Hi — I'm [name]. This matches your club members with each other for case practice, free for your season. Under a minute."
>
> **(8–20s, the matching screen)** "Members pick a practice night. They get matched with someone at their level — no spreadsheet, no pairing by hand, no chasing no-shows."
>
> **(20–32s, two-person session footage, then the report)** "Thirty-minute live case, they swap roles halfway, and both get a feedback report. You can see who's actually practising."
>
> **(32–44s, you)** "One ask: pick one evening and have your members all show up at once. Twenty people on one night is ten matches; twenty across a week is none. I'll run the first session myself."
>
> **(44–50s)** "caseforgeprep.in, or just reply to my message."

**Register:** shirt, quiet room, no music, no jump cuts. Say **"free for your season"** and **"I'll run the first one myself"**. Under a minute.

## Video 5 — Practice night recap (weekly, 25–35 seconds, with consent)

Once nights are running, this is your cheapest and most convincing content, and it's also recruitment for the next one.

> **(0–6s, the room or the grid of pairs)** "Case night three. Six pairs, twelve people."
> **(6–20s, a real moment — someone making a sharp point, or visibly stuck then unstuck)** "[Something specific that happened.] And two of them have kept going on their own since, which is honestly the point."
> **(20–30s, you)** "Tuesday 9pm, same thing. Bring someone who's been meaning to start."
> **(30–35s, text: **TUESDAY 9PM**)**

**"Bring someone who's been meaning to start" is your referral ask**, and it's aligned with the user's own interest — every extra person makes matching better for everyone already in. **That alignment is your best growth property; say it out loud every week.**

## Video 6 — The honest one (make 1, week 2)

**Length:** 25–35 seconds · **Where:** LinkedIn, Peerlist, replies to "is anyone actually on there?"

Your biggest objection is liquidity. **Address it head-on rather than letting people discover it.**

> **(0–6s, your face)** "The honest problem with what I built: it needs two people at once."
>
> **(6–16s)** "Ten other tools give you an AI case interviewer, which is available at 2am. Mine needs an actual person at your level, which means if your campus isn't live, you won't get matched. I'm not going to pretend otherwise."
>
> **(16–28s)** "So I'm doing one campus at a time with fixed practice nights, rather than an open matching page that quietly fails. [N] campuses so far."
>
> **(28–35s)** "If yours isn't one, get eight people from your batch to pick an evening and I'll run your first night myself. caseforgeprep.in"

**Why post this:** it converts your weakness into a credible reason to recruit, and it pre-empts the "I signed up and nothing happened" review. It also recruits exactly the person you want — the one who'll bring eight people.
