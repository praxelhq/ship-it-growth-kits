# Case Forge — Positioning

**Live:** https://caseforgeprep.in/ · **Category:** Education · **Audience:** B2C → **1,000 signups in 30 days** ([`TARGET-POLICY.md`](../../TARGET-POLICY.md)) · **Profile:** https://shipped.money/p/case-forge

---

## Three things to know before anything else

**1. Your landing page is 295 characters long.**

That's the whole thing — a headline, a subheadline, one sentence about who it's for, and a login button. There is no explanation of how matching works, no pricing, no FAQ, no screenshot, no proof, no answer to a single question a visitor would have. Both CTAs go to `/login`.

**2. You have a college-email gate and you don't say which emails work.**

*"Join with your college email →"* with no list of accepted domains. Every visitor whose domain isn't recognised hits a dead end with no explanation, and anyone who isn't currently enrolled — pre-MBA candidates, lateral candidates, people whose college email expired — is excluded before she's understood what you do.

**3. Your product has the hardest liquidity problem in this cohort.**

Live peer practice needs **two people, online at the same time, at the same level.** Ten cohort-mates sell solo practice that works at 2am with nobody else awake. You need a match. A thousand signups scattered across a month and a hundred campuses produce almost no matches — while sixty signups who all show up at 9pm on Thursday produce thirty excellent sessions.

**None of these is fatal. All three are the first week's work**, and they're in `04` in that order.

## And here is why it's worth fixing: your wedge is the best unmet need in the market

> **"Crack the case. Together."**
> *"Practise consulting cases live with peers at your level, and get instant AI feedback after every case."*

**"I have no one to practise with" is the single most common complaint in case preparation.** It comes up in every thread, on every campus, in every lateral candidate's story. Ten projects in this cohort answer it with *"practise with our AI instead"*, which is a substitute, not a solution — because what a case interview actually tests is being in a room with a person who pushes back and doesn't want to help you.

**You are the only one solving the real problem.** Peer matching *by level* is also genuinely smart: being matched with someone far better than you is demoralising, and someone far worse teaches you nothing.

So the job of this kit is to get you enough density that a match actually happens.

## Who your first 1,000 users actually are

**Not individuals. Cohorts.** This is the most important strategic sentence in your kit.

In priority order:

1. **Your own campus, first and hardest.** Fifty people from one batch, in one timezone, with one placement calendar, produce matches constantly. **Saturate one campus completely before you touch a second.** A hundred users across twenty campuses is worthless; forty users on one campus is a working product.
2. **Consulting clubs, one at a time, as whole cohorts.** A club secretary who forwards your link gives you 30–150 people who share a calendar. **This is the only way you reach match density** — and it's why `02` is almost entirely club outreach.
3. **Pairs who already know each other.** Two friends who've agreed to prepare together and need structure. You don't have to solve matching for them at all — they arrive matched. **This is the easiest win in your kit and your page doesn't speak to it.**
4. **The person who has nobody.** Your truest user emotionally, and the hardest to serve, because she needs you to have solved liquidity already. She's who you're *for*; she is not who you acquire first.

**Who is not your user, this month:** anyone outside a campus cohort. Not because she wouldn't want it — because you cannot match her, and a user who signs up and never gets a session is worse than no user. She leaves and tells people it doesn't work.

## The one-line pitch

Lead with the pain, because it's universal and unaddressed:

> **No case partner? Get matched with someone at your level, practise live, and get AI feedback after.**

For a campus group, where density is everything:

> **[Campus] case practice — get matched with someone from our batch at your level. Live, 30 minutes, AI feedback after.**

For a pair who already have each other:

> **You already have a partner. This gives you the cases, the console and the feedback.**

For a club secretary:

> **Your members get matched with each other at their level. No scheduling, no WhatsApp spreadsheet.**

## The wedge use case

**"I've read the casebook and I have nobody to do it with."**

Everything flows from that. Not "AI case practice" — the opposite. **Your product's whole value is that the other person is a person.**

The moment you're selling: she gets matched with someone from her own batch at her level, they do a case, and both get feedback. **She has just solved the problem she'd been stuck on for weeks**, and the obvious next thing she does is tell two friends — because she now wants more people in the pool, which is also exactly what you want. **That alignment is your best growth property and it only exists once matching works.**

## Why someone picks you over the alternatives

1. **The ten others in this cohort.** You beat all of them on the thing that matters most: a real person who pushes back. You lose on availability — they work at 2am, you need a match. **So your copy should be honest about the trade:** *"an AI is available instantly; a person is better. Here's a person at your level."*
2. **Finding a partner yourself.** This is your real competitor, and it's free. People try, it works for two weeks, then schedules drift and it collapses. **You beat it on matching and on structure** — a scheduled session with cases ready and feedback at the end survives where "let's do a case sometime" doesn't. Say that explicitly; everyone has lived it.
3. **Nothing.** Most candidates. They read, they don't practise out loud, and the first time they say a structure to a human being is in the actual interview.

## The two objections that decide everything

1. **"Will there be anyone to match with?"** The killer, and your honest answer today is *probably not, unless you're on my campus.* Which is exactly why `02` saturates one campus first and `04` Fix 2 replaces open-ended matching with **scheduled practice windows** — a fixed time when everyone comes, so matching has something to work with.
2. **"Will my college email work?"** Currently unanswerable from your page. `04` Fix 3.

## One housekeeping thing you must do today

**`caseforge.in` exists. It is a different product, in your own Ship It cohort, one word from your name.**

They sell non-consulting functional case practice; you sell live peer matching. **You are more confusable than you are competitive** — and they have the stronger search position, a full landing page and published pricing.

So:

- **Always post the full URL `caseforgeprep.in`.** Never just "Case Forge".
- **Consider renaming.** You are thirty days old with 295 characters of website and almost no brand equity to lose. "Case Forge" will cost you traffic to `caseforge.in` for as long as you keep it, and the cheapest moment to change a name is now. Something that says what you do — pairing, matching, partners — would work harder than a name you have to defend.
- **Lead with the words they can't use: "live", "peers", "together".** That's your actual difference and it's already in your headline.

## What NOT to say

- **Don't say "AI feedback" first.** Ten competitors own that phrase and it points away from your only real differentiator. The person is the product; the AI is the bonus.
- **Don't promise instant matching** until it's true. One empty match screen loses a user permanently and she tells her batch.
- **Don't expand to a second campus** until the first one has sessions happening daily. Density is the entire strategy.
- **Don't keep the email gate vague.** Either list the domains or drop the requirement.
