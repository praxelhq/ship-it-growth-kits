# shotgun — Distribution map

**Lead with existing friend organizers.** They already have a group and an occasion, so you can test whether the tool actually replaces planning friction.

| Route | Scheduled action / permission | Assumed result | Copy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ten willing group organizers | Ask them to try one real plan with their own friends; private links stay private. | 10 organizers × 3–5 willing participants = 30–50 people at most, before overlap; 5–10 active crews assumed. | #1 |
| Three existing alumni/friend groups | Admin-approved single product invitation. Do not post actual crew access links publicly. | 3 × 30 relevant readers × 20–40% try = 18–36 sessions. | #2 |
| Four own-account clips | Vote, role rotation, split and repeat plan on Days 8, 13, 20, 26. | 4 × 300–3,000 views × 0.5–2% visits × 50% start = 3–120 sessions. | #3 |
| Two LinkedIn posts | Organizer-friction/product lesson on Days 11/24 at 12:30 IST. | 2 × 200–1,000 impressions × 1–3% visits × 50% start = 2–30 sessions. | #4 |
| [Peerlist](https://peerlist.io/) | Maker feedback after real two-phone create/join/vote tests, current rules/slot checked. No votes requested. | 0–30 sessions, uncertain upside. | #5 |

No paid group hosts, influencer, venue discount, unverified subreddit or newsletter placement is assumed. An alumni newsletter can carry a free invitation only after permission, zero forecast until accepted. Reply to actual planning questions with useful help, not a repeated app pitch.

At 50% visit-to-first-crew-session, 1,000 requires 2,000 relevant unique visits. The base may yield tens or low hundreds. Measure whether one invitee later creates a new real plan before assuming a viral loop.

Use Days 17–20 for the second organizer wave, with a different contact list from Crewup, Kahi Chalo and Adda. Their jobs are board-game/study hosting, outing discovery and fair travel zones; yours is an existing crew's decisions, roles and split.
