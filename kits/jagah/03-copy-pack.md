## 4. Reddit — r/india data post 🔴

**Do the actual research first.** 30 phones, with permission. The number must be real.

> **I scanned 30 Indian phones to find out what's actually eating the storage. 31% of the average camera roll is WhatsApp forwards.**
>
> My phone has been full for about two years and I'd been deleting photos one at a time like an idiot. So I got slightly obsessive and, with permission, went through 30 phones belonging to friends, family and classmates — a mix of 64GB and 128GB, mostly Android, a few iPhones.
>
> Here's the breakdown of the average camera roll:
>
> - **31% — WhatsApp / Telegram forwards.** Good Morning images, festival greetings, devotional forwards, birthday graphics. Auto-downloaded, never opened again.
> - **22% — screenshots.** Payment confirmations, Amazon order screens, boarding passes, OTPs, memes. Median age: 14 months.
> - **18% — near-duplicates.** Bursts, retakes, the same photo sent back to you by someone else at lower quality.
> - **9% — blurry or accidental.** Pocket shots, failed night photos.
> - **20% — actual photos people want.**
>
> **One in five photos on the average Indian phone is a photo somebody wanted to take.**
>
> Some other things that surprised me:
>
> - Phones with family WhatsApp groups averaged **2.4x** the forward volume. Unsurprising in hindsight, brutal in practice.
> - The **same image often exists 3–4 times** — once from WhatsApp, once from a screenshot of it, once from being re-forwarded at a different compression.
> - 17 of 30 people were paying for Google One or iCloud. **All 17 would have fit comfortably in free tier after a cleanup.** That's roughly ₹1,500/year each, to store Good Morning flowers.
>
> **What actually works, if you want to do this manually today:**
> 1. In WhatsApp: Settings → Storage and Data → Manage Storage → "Larger than 5MB" and "Forwarded many times". Takes 10 minutes, usually the single biggest win.
> 2. Turn off WhatsApp media auto-download for groups. Prevents the problem recurring.
> 3. Gallery → search "screenshot" → sort oldest first → delete anything over a year old.
> 4. **Before you pay for more cloud storage, do the above and check whether you still need it.**
>
> Happy to share the method or the raw category definitions if anyone wants to replicate it.
>
> *(Disclosure: I'm building an app in this space — Jagah — so I'm biased. The 30-phone study is real and the manual steps above work without any app. Mods, remove if this breaks rules.)*

**The manual steps are the point.** Giving away the thing that makes your app less necessary is what gets a post like this upvoted instead of removed — and it is what makes the comments friendly.

## 10. Peerlist / build-in-public post

> **Shipped Jagah: an on-device gallery cleaner that knows the difference between your grandmother and a Good Morning forward.**
>
> The interesting engineering problem wasn't duplicate detection — perceptual hashing is a solved problem. It was two other things:
>
> **1. Ranking quality inside a duplicate group.** "These 12 are similar" is useless; the user still has to pick. "This is the sharpest, and it's the only one where nobody is blinking" is the actual product. Laplacian variance for sharpness, plus face and expression scoring, all on-device.
>
> **2. Classifying a forward.** A Good Morning image is not a photo, but to a generic cleaner it's an identical JPEG. Signals that actually work: compression history (forwards are re-compressed repeatedly), aspect ratios typical of graphic templates, high-saturation synthetic colour distributions, text density and placement, and source directory.
>
> Both run entirely on-device: 10,000 photos in under 90 seconds on a mid-range Android, with no network permission used during a scan.
>
> Happy to go into detail on either if it's useful to anyone.
