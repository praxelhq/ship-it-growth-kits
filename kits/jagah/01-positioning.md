## Who your first 1,000 users actually are

**The Indian Android user with a 64GB or 128GB phone that has been full for months, who deletes photos one by one when the camera refuses to open.**

Your founder bio already states the insight exactly: *"Like everyone I know, I kept seeing 'storage full' and deleting photos one by one."* That sentence is better positioning than most of your landing page.

The four groups that will actually install first:

1. **College students, 18–24, 64–128GB phones.** Highest density of the problem, highest willingness to try a new app, and the group you can reach for free. **This is your beachhead.**
2. **Young professionals, 24–35**, who have been paying ₹130/month for Google One for two years because it was easier than cleaning up. Your best paying customer — they have already demonstrated willingness to pay to avoid this problem.
3. **Parents and family-group members, 35–60.** They have the *worst* version of the problem — a camera roll that is 60% "Good Morning" forwards — and the lowest ability to fix it. Hardest to reach directly, easiest to reach **through their children**.
4. **iPhone users on a 64/128GB device** who will not pay for iCloud. Smaller group in India, higher paying intent.

**The crucial distribution fact: this audience is not in a community. They are on Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts.** There is no "r/myPhoneIsFull". This is a content-reach play, not a community play, and that makes it completely different from every other kit in wave 1.

## The wedge use case

**WhatsApp family-group junk.**

Not "storage cleaning" generally — the specific, ridiculous, universally recognised fact that a quarter of an Indian phone's gallery is Good Morning flowers, festival greetings, devotional forwards and birthday graphics that were auto-downloaded and never looked at once.

Your own page gives this the right name: *"Every Good Morning Forward"*, 4.9 GB. **That is the funniest, most shareable, most specifically-Indian thing about this product, and it should be the front door.**

Why this wedge and not the others:

- **It is instantly recognisable.** Everyone has seen it. No explanation required.
- **It is funny.** Funny travels for free; useful does not.
- **Google Photos and Files by Google do not do it well.** They see "images". They do not know that a JPEG of roses with "GOOD MORNING" in Comic Sans is categorically different from a photo of your grandmother.
- **It is the one thing a generic international cleaner cannot copy quickly**, because it needs Indian-specific recognition.

Duplicates and blurry shots are table stakes. **The forward sweeper is the product.**

## Why someone picks you over the alternatives

This is the honest part, and you need to be ready for it because someone will say it in your first Reddit comment.

**Your real competition:**

| Alternative | What it does | Where it falls short |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Files by Google** | Free, preinstalled on most Androids, finds duplicates, junk files and large media | Weak on near-duplicates and blur, no concept of WhatsApp forwards vs. real photos, no quality ranking within a burst |
| **Google Photos "Free up space"** | Deletes local copies of already-backed-up photos | Only helps if you back up — i.e. if you are already paying |
| **Buying Google One / iCloud** | ₹130+/month, forever | Stores the junk instead of removing it |
| **Doing it by hand** | Free | What everyone currently does, badly, for an hour, twice a year |

**Be upfront: Files by Google is free and already on their phone.** Pretending otherwise makes you look naive and gets you destroyed in comments. Your genuine advantages:

1. **It understands Indian camera rolls.** Good Morning forwards, festival graphics, devotional images, wedding invite PDFs-as-images. Nothing else does this, and it is the single largest category on your own page at 6.2 GB.
2. **Quality ranking inside a burst.** Not just "these 12 are similar" but "this one is the sharpest and nobody is blinking" — *keep this, delete these 11*. That is the difference between a tool that finds duplicates and a tool you actually trust to delete them.
3. **On-device, verifiably.** *"Switch off Wi-Fi and mobile data completely — Jagah will still work."* **This is your strongest trust claim and it is testable by the user in ten seconds.** Make them test it. A claim the user can personally verify is worth more than any privacy policy.
4. **30-day recovery through the phone's own Recently Deleted folder.** Not your trash — *theirs*. This is the right design and it removes the only real fear.
5. **The price comparison.** ₹49/month against ₹2,000+/year of cloud storage for junk. Your line — *"Don't pay rent forever just to store duplicate screenshots"* — is the best sentence on your website. Use it everywhere.

## The objections, and the honest answers

1. **"Files by Google already does this, and it's free."**
   — *"For duplicates and junk files, yes, and it's good. Where it stops is quality: it won't tell you which of 12 burst shots is the sharpest, and it treats a Good Morning forward exactly like a photo of your mother. Try both — the free Jagah clean costs you nothing and you'll see the difference in the WhatsApp category."* **Never be defensive about this.** Recommending a competitor for what it does well buys you credibility for everything else.

2. **"Will it delete photos I actually want?"**
   — The biggest fear by a distance. Your answer is already right: nothing is permanently deleted, everything goes to the phone's own Recently Deleted folder, 30 days to restore, and you review before anything is removed. **Say it before they ask, every time.**

3. **"Are you uploading my photos?"**
   — *"Turn off Wi-Fi and mobile data and run the scan. It works. That's the proof."* Best possible answer in the category: a test they can run themselves.

4. **"Why pay ₹49 a month to delete things?"**
   — Don't defend the subscription. **Lead with the free clean.** The free tier reclaims up to 10GB and is genuinely useful; the subscription is for people who don't want to do it again in three months. If 90% never pay, the plan still works — you need 1,000 users first, not 1,000 subscribers.

5. **"Another app to install, to free up space?"**
   — Acknowledge the irony out loud, it disarms the objection: *"Yes, a 30MB app to get back 15GB. Delete it afterwards if you want — the space stays freed."*

## What NOT to say

- **Don't say "AI-powered".** Every app in India says it; it signals nothing and attracts scepticism about where the photos are going. Say *"on your phone, offline"* instead — it's the more impressive claim anyway.
- **Don't lead with Smart Albums.** It's the nicest feature and the weakest hook. Nobody installs an app to get albums; they install it because the camera won't open.
- **Don't publish the GB averages (3.8, 2.9, 4.6, 6.2) without being able to say where they came from.** Someone will ask. If they're estimates, label them as estimates from your own test devices. **One unsourced number undermines every other number on the page**, and privacy-sceptical commenters hunt for exactly this.
- **Don't claim "100% private" and "zero data collected" if you run any analytics at all.** Precision here is a feature. *"Your photos never leave your phone. We collect anonymous usage counts, nothing about your images"* is both honest and more credible than an absolute.
- **Don't position against Google Photos.** You are not a backup product and that comparison invites a fight you lose. Position against **the ₹130/month you pay to store junk**.
