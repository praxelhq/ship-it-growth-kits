## 7. Quora answer — *"How do I politely ask parents for pending tuition fees?"*

Answer properly. The product goes at the end, disclosed.

> I've asked a lot of tuition teachers this and the pattern is consistent: the problem isn't the words, it's the delay. A reminder on the 7th is easy. The same reminder on the 25th feels like an accusation, so most teachers never send it and quietly absorb the loss.
>
> **Three things that work:**
>
> **1. Make it a routine, not an event.** If every parent gets the same short message on the same date every month, nobody feels singled out. The awkwardness comes from it being unusual.
>
> **2. State the amount and the month, and add "whenever convenient".** Vagueness is what makes it uncomfortable for both sides.
>
> *"Namaste Sir/Ma'am, Asha's tuition fee of ₹2,000 for October is due. Please send it whenever convenient. Thank you."*
>
> That is enough. Don't explain, don't apologise, don't add pressure.
>
> **3. Always send a receipt when they pay.** It turns the whole thing into a transaction with a record, and parents who get receipts pay faster the next month. A lot of them also need it for tax.
>
> **4. Never send it through the child.** It puts a 12-year-old in the middle and it embarrasses everyone.
>
> One practical note: keep the record somewhere other than your memory. Almost every teacher I've spoken to has lost money simply by forgetting a part payment from four months ago.
>
> *(Disclosure: I built a free tool for exactly this — FeeSathi — so I'm obviously biased. But the advice above holds whatever you use, including a notebook.)*
