## Who your first 1,000 users actually are

This is the hardest ICP in wave 1, and it is worth being blunt about why: **your buyer is not online in the places growth advice assumes.** They are not on Reddit, Product Hunt, Hacker News or X. A generic launch playbook will get you zero users. This kit is built around that.

Your first 1,000 are **the independent tuition teacher in an Indian town or city suburb who teaches 15–60 students from home or a single rented room.**

1. **Home tutors** — Maths, Science, English, Accounts, for classes 6–12. One or two batches an evening. Fees ₹500–₹3,000/month per student, collected in cash or UPI, tracked in a notebook or in their head.
2. **Music, dance, art and abacus teachers** — you already have a page for them ("Music, dance & other classes"), and they are an easier sell than academic tutors because their fee cycles are messier.
3. **Small coaching classes** — 2–3 teachers, 40–150 students, currently using a register and a calculator.
4. **Secondary: the teacher's spouse**, who often does the actual chasing.

What unites them: **the most unpleasant part of their week is asking a parent for money.** Not the admin — the *awkwardness*. Your tagline already knows this: *"Awkward asks gone."* That is the single best line on your site and should be the centre of everything.

## The one-line pitch

> **Know who owes you fees, and send a polite reminder on WhatsApp without the awkward conversation.**

In Hindi/Hinglish, which is what most of your outreach should be in:

> **Kaun si fees pending hai, pata chalega. Reminder bhi ready milega — bas Send dabao.**

## The wedge use case

**The first of the month.**

Not "fee management". The specific, dreaded ritual: it's the 5th, four parents haven't paid, you've been meaning to message them for a week, and every day you don't, it gets harder. That is the moment your product exists for and the moment your copy should describe.

Secondary wedge, and possibly your best acquisition hook: **the receipt.** Parents ask for receipts, teachers hand-write them or don't give them at all. Your free receipt generator solves this in 30 seconds for someone who has never heard of you. See `02` — it is the centre of your entire organic plan.

## Why someone picks you over the alternatives

The real competition is **a notebook**, and secondarily a WhatsApp group plus memory. Against those:

1. **It takes three minutes and costs nothing.** Not a system to adopt — a page with your students on it.
2. **It writes the message for you.** This is the actual product. The tracking is table stakes; the *"Hi, Asha's tuition fee of Rs 2,000 is due this month. Please send it when convenient. Thank you."* is what they're buying. It's polite, it's not from them, and it makes the ask feel procedural rather than personal.
3. **No payment handling, deliberately.** Your page says this plainly and it is a strength with this audience, not a weakness: a teacher who hears "we'll collect the money" hears "a cut, and GST, and a company between me and the parent". You are a notebook that writes messages. Say that.

Against the app-store alternatives (Classplus, Teachmint, Myclassboard): those are platforms that want to run the teacher's whole business and are sold by salespeople. You are a page. Lean into small.

## The thing that will cost you users, and you need to fix it

> *"Your private link is your only key. Anyone with it can edit your page… There is no password reset."*

For this audience specifically, this is a serious problem, stated honestly on your own page:

- A teacher who loses the link loses their entire fee book with no recovery. One WhatsApp clear-chat and the data is gone.
- Anyone who gets the link — a student, a family member borrowing the phone — can edit everything.
- It is the first thing a cautious user will balk at, and your "BEFORE YOU START" section puts it right in front of them.

**Add email-based magic-link recovery before you start acquisition in volume.** You already ask for an email at signup. One "send me my link" email flow removes the single biggest objection and the single biggest churn cause. This is an engineering item, not a marketing one, and it belongs in week 1. Everything in `06` assumes you've done it.

## What NOT to say

- Don't say "SaaS", "platform", "dashboard", or "onboarding". Say *page*, *fee book*, *list*.
- Don't say "automate". Teachers hear "it will message parents without me", which is exactly the fear. Say **"you review and send it yourself"** — your site already does this well.
- Don't lead in English-only. Most of your outreach should be Hinglish.
