## Your first person

A personal-Mac user who already enjoys decorating their workspace, studies or works alone, and wants a pet that reacts to their day. Lead with battery hunger and quiet mode, not a PDF utility comparison. They can see the personality in one clip and decide whether $5 is worth it.

**Pitch:** A little Mac pet that gets hungry with your battery, naps at night and keeps you company above the Dock.

**Bio:** Your battery gets a tiny, very expressive friend.

Your wedge is the plug-in moment: a hungry pet perks up when charging begins. The website demo is useful, but an actual Mac recording shows the product being bought. Be precise about file behaviour: this app moves a fed file to Trash. Do not copy dumdum's different promise that it only takes a copy.

## Alternatives and the honest tradeoff

[DeskPet](https://www.deskpet.app/) offers 14 days free then $2 once, with focus, water and task tools on Apple silicon/macOS 14+. [dumdum](https://getdumdum.app/) has a free Windows tier but no current Mac build; don't call it a Mac alternative. You are selling a battery-reactive personality and useful local tools, not the cheapest animal on a screen.

The page says everything works offline after one licence activation, with update checks online, and includes quiet modes and hiding from screen sharing. Those are meaningful features to demonstrate, not independently audited privacy or performance claims.

The cohort still asks for **1,000 signups**, counted as unique first launches for this no-account Mac app, beside successful pet adoption. A paid-only $5 install is a much harder conversion than a free web toy. Say so. A hundred browser-demo interactions are not a hundred installed pets.
