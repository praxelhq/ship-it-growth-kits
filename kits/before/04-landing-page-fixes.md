# Before — Landing-page fixes

Read [the public page](https://trybefore.me/) on 29 October 2026. Current headline: **“Find out if people will actually buy it, before you make 5,000 of them.”** Current CTA: **“Start free”** / **“Start a test”**. Curious $0, Serious $9/month, Obsessed $29/month. Authentication, publishing, report generation and payment remain untested.

## 1. Put audience responsibility beside the CTA

Current FAQ says the owner brings respondents and the tester network starts empty. It is too important to leave as a late discovery.

Replacement: **“Bring your own target customers. Share one test link; we organize their answers. Our new tester network is not a ready-made panel.”**

Verify that signup does not imply automatic respondent matching or an included audience.

## 2. Narrow the buying promise

Current headline asks whether people will actually buy; the FAQ explicitly says answers do not guarantee sales.

Replacement headline: **“Compare product concepts before you commit to a batch.”** Support: **“See stated preference, price choices and objections. Confirm demand with a real offer before ordering inventory.”**

Keep the distinction in the report and social preview, not only legal copy.

## 3. Explain the response threshold before publication

Current FAQ requires 20 responses for a verdict and recommends 30–50.

Replacement: **“Plan your recruitment first. A verdict opens after 20 responses; smaller samples remain incomplete. More answers do not remove audience bias.”**

Test 0, 19 and 20 response states using labelled internal records. Never seed a real owner's report with fake respondents.

## 4. Make the free allowance precise

Current pricing gives two concepts on Curious while the general setup description says up to four.

Replacement strip: **“Free: one live test, two concepts, unlimited responses, full report. Four concepts require a paid plan.”**

Paid buttons should show the real currency, billing period and any tax before checkout. Do not invent an annual saving or included respondent credits.

## 5. Make the verdict inspectable

Current report promises Build/Modify/Retest/Drop, winning design and best price.

Replacement report note: **“A recommendation from this sample. Inspect who answered, the count at each price, objections and uncertainty before deciding.”**

Check what the displayed margin actually measures. An opt-in convenience sample cannot support an unexplained population-confidence claim. Rename “best price” to a description of the observed sample if necessary.

## 6. Show a real no-account respondent route

Current page promises two minutes on a phone and no login.

Replacement CTA: **“Try a labelled sample test.”** Build the sample before advertising it. Show consent, clear concept images, price units and completion confirmation. Time five genuine attempts before claiming a typical duration. Test narrow screen, slow connection, duplicate answers and owner access separately.

Before distribution, use a fresh account with permitted fictional materials to verify creation, share link, response, closing and report. Log exact failure routes; a working public landing page does not prove the signed-in product works. Never call a failed research fetch proof the site is dead.
