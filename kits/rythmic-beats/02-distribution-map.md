# Rythmic Beats — Distribution map

**Lead with student rehearsal deadlines and a real export demonstration.** A dance-society organizer can find five relevant editors faster than a broad AI-tool launch.

| Route | Action / boundary | Planning outcome, not benchmark | Copy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Campus dance/cultural-society organizer | Ask for a free 20-minute rehearsal-edit clinic in Days 4–7. Invite ten interested performers after permission; bring original/authorized sample audio. | 10 invited × 60% attend × 50% export = 3 activations. |#1,#4|
| Existing student choreographers | Ten individual notes across Days 9/16. Ask about the next track deadline, not for a testimonial. No imported contact list. | 10 × 40% response × 50% signup = 2 accounts. |#5|

Watch [BandLab's own blog](https://blog.bandlab.com/) and official learning material for mixing questions, not an audience to spam. Your organizer's rehearsal bulletin can share #1 only after agreement. No external newsletter inclusion or event booking is assumed. [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/launch) is optional after a complete export test, with a personal maker account and no upvote request; first check for an existing listing.

Coordinate student sessions in Days 4–7 with the dance-operations writers. Do not send a second pitch into the same organizer's group that week. Use one clinic around a practical transition, not a bundle of all cohort dance tools.

The first plan supports tens of accounts, not a proven 1,000. At an assumed 25% qualified-visit→signup rate, 1,000 requires 4,000 visits. A free editing clinic is useful only if performers return for their actual final track and tell another team. Measure those referrals before forecasting a campus network you do not control.
