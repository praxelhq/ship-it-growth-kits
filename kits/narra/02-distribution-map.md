# Narra — Distribution map

**Lead with rights-cleared chapter demonstrations and campus reading circles.** A book-specific sample gives readers something concrete to discuss. A generic promise about reading faster will mostly attract people who never try a chapter.

## A month that adds up

The calendar schedules three circles, six clips (Days 4,8,12,18,23,27), four LinkedIn posts (Days 6,13,20,29), twenty personal invitations and one optional Peerlist launch. Their simple scenarios suggest 57–124 starts before overlap. Plan on 40–100 distinct starters until real data replaces this estimate. That is well below 1,000. A larger reading partner or a clip with demonstrably wider response is the missing route, not an assumed viral multiplier.

Do not add views to signups. Deduplicate people who come through a clip, a friend and a live session. A forwarded link is an invitation, not an activation. If the forecast misses 1,000, the missing distribution must come from a measured partner or stronger observed response, not a new label for the same traffic.

## Contact and posting rules

Use your own public accounts. Reply where the discussion actually concerns this use case; disclose that you made the product. Do not paste links under unrelated popular posts, send automated DMs or add people to groups. For private campus or WhatsApp groups, ask the administrator first and send one invitation. Five individual opt-in invitations in a work session is a workload cap, not a platform-approved quota. Stop when someone declines; one follow-up after seven days is enough for a relevant organizer.

Use Days 10–13 for the first shared-campus reading invitation. Infolio, Footnotes and C1’s reading products serve different jobs: explainers, curated reading and community. Ask organizers which single activity they want; do not send three “read more” pitches to the same group.

## Directories, letters and accounts

Peerlist is the verified builder listing. Project Gutenberg is a free alternative and source reference, not a distribution partner or a publisher that has agreed to feature you. No book newsletter submission route or third-party reading-community promotion permission was verified, so neither contributes forecast users.

Paid listings, sponsorships, prizes and promoted posts are outside your zero budget. An editor accepting a story is upside, not a scheduled conversion event. When a page's posting rules cannot be read, do not infer permission from another creator's post.
