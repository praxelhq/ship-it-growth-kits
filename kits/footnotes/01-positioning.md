## Your first person

A student who wants a little fiction at night but keeps opening social feeds instead. They want to enter a story, not get an exam summary or join a book club before reading. Start with one classic whose opening genuinely interests them.

Do not broaden this to everyone who might someday enjoy the category. Find twenty people with this exact situation, watch five use the product, then repeat the invitation that produced an unprompted return.

## The pitch

> Read the first episode of a classic on your phone, then decide whether you want another.

For a bio or a short reply:

> Classic fiction in short scrollable episodes, free to read.

## Your opening use case

White Nights, Episode 1: read until the end, then choose the next episode. The useful first result is an actual stretch of reading. Do not call the short episode a whole completed book or promise that one minute captures the original work.

## The real alternative

[Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/) offers free full texts with no registration, mainly older works whose US copyright expired. It wins on complete editions and download options. Footnotes must earn the habit through episode layout and continuation, not a claim that classics are unavailable free. Check rights for the exact editions/translations where you distribute; an author’s age alone does not establish every translation’s status.

## What you need to prove

Show the actual first episode and next link at normal scroll speed. Ask five readers whether they finished and wanted another. Keep original text, edited material and any generated summary labelled distinctly, and verify source/edition attribution before expanding the library.
