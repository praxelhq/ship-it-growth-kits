Scripts and shot lists only — **you record these on your phone.**

## Format A — The guesstimate walkthrough (your workhorse; make 12)

**Length:** 25–35 seconds · **No face, no voice needed** · **Where:** Instagram, Shorts, LinkedIn, Reddit comments, your SEO pages

Your case library is the script. **These double as the content for the 12 guesstimate pages in `02`** — one afternoon writes both.

### Template

| Time | On screen |
| --- | --- |
| 0–4s | **"How many tennis balls are sold in London every year?"** |
| 4–6s | **"20 seconds. Go."** |
| 6–9s | *(beat — nothing moves)* |
| 9–14s | **Your tree builder, branching live:** Consumer → players × frequency × replacement rate |
| 14–19s | **The second branch most people forget:** Institutional → clubs, schools, coaching |
| 19–25s | The total, then **"Sanity check: balls per Londoner per year. Believable?"** |
| 25–31s | **"The follow-up a real interviewer asks: did you account for Wimbledon?"** |
| 31–35s | **"ballpark.chat"** |

### The twelve

Use your own library, which is already strong:

1. Tennis balls sold in London annually
2. Daily electricity consumption of Berlin's electric buses
3. Annual revenue of a high-end sushi restaurant in Manhattan
4. Annual revenue of a luxury gym chain in NYC
5. Smartphones replaced in India each year
6. Price points for electric scooter rentals in Paris
7. Market size for cloud software in healthcare
8. ATMs in Mumbai
9. Cars sold in India per year
10. Litres of petrol one city uses daily
11. Hotel rooms occupied in a city on a Tuesday
12. Coffee cups sold on a university campus daily

### Rules that make this format work

- **The tree building on screen is the whole video.** It's your unique visual and it explains the product without a word of copy.
- **Always end on the sanity check and the interviewer's follow-up.** Those two beats separate you from ten thousand framework videos, and they're literally what your product does.
- **The "forgotten branch" is the payoff.** Every one of these needs the thing most people miss — institutional demand, capacity constraints, seasonal spikes. **That's the insight people screenshot.**
- **No face, no voice** — twelve in one afternoon.

### Shot list

1. **Screen recording of the tree builder**, zoomed so the branches are readable on a phone. Speed up building 1.5–2×.
2. **Text cards** for the question, the sanity check and the follow-up.
3. **Hold the final insight for two full seconds.** That's the screenshot and the share.

## Format B — The Datasheet video (your best acquisition piece)

**Length:** 30–40 seconds · **Where:** LinkedIn, Reddit, Instagram, Discord, your landing page

**Your Datasheet is a gift, and a gift is the easiest thing to make a video about.**

> **(0–6s, your face)** "Every market-sizing case, I'd get two steps in and need a number I didn't have. So I'd make one up."
>
> **(6–14s, screen: a tree with a made-up figure highlighted in red)** "Average UK household income. Rent per square foot in Manhattan. How often people replace a phone. I'd invent something plausible and keep going."
>
> **(14–22s, the number propagating through the whole tree)** "The problem is a made-up number at step two doesn't make your answer slightly wrong. It makes every step after it meaningless — and when the interviewer asks you to sanity-check the total, you can't, because you don't know which figure was the lie."
>
> **(22–32s, the Datasheet, scrolling: incomes, rents, occupancy rates, replacement cycles)** "So here they are. With sources. Free, no account."
>
> **(32–40s, your face)** "ballpark.chat. Tell me which number you always end up guessing and I'll add it."

**Why this is your strongest video:** the confession is specific and universal — **everyone does this and everyone assumes they're the only one.** The ask at the end is for help, which is what makes people comment.

**Pin this to your LinkedIn profile.**

## Format C — The MECE tree builder demo

**Length:** 30–40 seconds · **Where:** landing page, Peerlist, "how does it work" replies

Your tree builder is the thing no cohort-mate has, and it's visual.

> **(0–5s, a blank tree)** "The rule everyone quotes and nobody checks: branches must not overlap, and must sum to a hundred percent."
>
> **(5–16s, building a tree — Revenue → Price × Volume, Cost → Fixed + Variable)** "Mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive. If your branches overlap you'll double-count. If they don't add up, you've missed a chunk of the market and the interviewer will find it."
>
> **(16–26s, a deliberately bad tree with overlapping branches, flagged)** "Here's one that fails. 'Online buyers' and 'young buyers' — those overlap, and now your total is wrong in a way you can't see."
>
> **(26–36s, the fixed version)** "Fix the axis and it works. Build it visually and you catch this before you say it out loud."
>
> **(36–40s)** "Free: ballpark.chat"

**The deliberately bad tree is the teaching moment** and the reason this gets shared — people recognise the mistake as their own.

## Format D — The "invisible product" video (make 1, week 1)

**Length:** 25–35 seconds · **Where:** LinkedIn, Peerlist, builder communities

**This one is for a different audience — builders — and it's the most relatable mistake on the internet.**

> **(0–6s, a terminal running `curl -s https://ballpark.chat/ | wc -c`, returning 1024)** "This is what my entire product looked like to Google three weeks ago."
>
> **(6–14s, the raw HTML on screen: a title and a div)** "A single-page React app. No server-rendered content, no meta description, no Open Graph tags. One thousand and twenty-four bytes and a div."
>
> **(14–22s, a WhatsApp link preview rendering as bare text next to a competitor's card)** "So: no search presence at all, and every link I shared looked broken. Deep links 404'd, which meant a refresh mid-case lost your work."
>
> **(22–30s, the fix — the `_redirects` file and the meta tags)** "One redirects file and a block of meta tags. The whole fix took an afternoon."
>
> **(30–35s)** "If you're shipping an SPA, run that curl before you launch. ballpark.chat"

**Why post this:** it's a genuinely useful warning, it's honest, and it gets your product in front of a builder audience on Peerlist who'll respect the confession. **It also pre-empts anyone who saw the broken version.**

## Format E — The specific-feedback video (make 1)

**Length:** 25–35 seconds · **Where:** LinkedIn, Reddit, landing page

Every visitor's doubt about AI scoring is *"will it just say 'good structure!'"*. **You have the counter-evidence already written.**

> **(0–6s, your face)** "The thing I distrust most about AI case tools is vague feedback. 'Good structure.' Useless."
>
> **(6–18s, screen: real feedback from your evaluator, held long enough to read)** "So here's what mine actually said on my London tennis case: *'Did not explicitly account for tourist and Wimbledon peak summer visitor spikes.'*"
>
> **(18–28s, your face)** "That's a real gap, in a real case, in London specifically. It's the kind of thing a partner would actually ask. That's the bar."
>
> **(28–35s)** "Free: ballpark.chat"

**Use real feedback from a real attempt of your own, including one where you scored badly.** Your product's feedback is unusually specific — **that's a genuine asset and the only way to prove it is to show it.**
