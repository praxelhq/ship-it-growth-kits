# AI Hunt — Landing-page fixes

Checked against the public homepage, index, submission page, guidelines and How it works on 29 October 2026. Reading a route is not a completed signup test.

## 1. Make editorial, illustrative and community evidence impossible to confuse

Current: How it works discloses seeded starter votes, while index cards identify editorial listings. The homepage's timely-discovery promise does not explain that distinction near the cards.

Add above the index: **“Editorial releases are checked by AI Hunt. Demo entries are labelled and excluded from community rankings. Votes show genuine signed-in reader activity.”** Use the final sentence only after demo votes really are excluded. Otherwise say **“Community ranking is being rebuilt; these example counts are not reader votes.”**

## 2. Replace the completeness promise with a verifiable job

Current homepage introduction: “Meet AI Hunt.” The page title says “Every new AI. The day it ships.” Replace the hero with **“Find a new AI release you can actually use.”** Subheading: **“Models, features and apps, with original release links, dates and clear access labels.”** Keep the three categories; they are more useful than a claim to cover everything.

## 3. Make the reader action primary

Current submission route tells users to sign in before submitting. That is appropriate for makers but not the reader's first task.

Primary CTA: **“Find a release for my task”**, going to the public index. Secondary: **“Submit a release for review”**, going to `/submit`. Check both on a fresh mobile browser. Do not add an email gate to reading the first entry.

## 4. Give every card the information needed to leave safely

Current cards show category and an editorial date/status but the starter examples explicitly lack maker websites/details. Replacement card fields: **“Original source · Released · Access: open / paid / waitlist · Checked on”**. Link to the official release, not an unaffiliated download. If no source is verified, label **“Illustrative example — not a verified release”** and omit the try button.

## 5. Prove value with a real task, not a vote counter

Add a small, current example: **“Need to summarize a paper? Here are three releases we checked and the access limits we found.”** Publish only after personally checking those three. Screenshots should show the original source and your explanation. Do not borrow provider logos without permission.

## 6. Test authentication and return states

Current voting rules require one signed-in account per launch. Replacement sign-in prompt: **“Sign in to save your vote. You can keep browsing without an account.”** After login, return to the same entry. Verify vote removal and duplicate-click handling yourself; neither was exercised in this research.

## 7. Make a shared link useful on a phone

Use title **“AI Hunt — sourced AI releases by task”** and description **“Check the original release, availability and first-use cost before trying a new AI.”** Preview one index link in your own WhatsApp chat. On a narrow screen, access status and the source link must appear before secondary ranking decoration. Measure load on mobile data; no performance score was measured here.
