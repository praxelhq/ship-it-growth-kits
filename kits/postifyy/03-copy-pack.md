# Postifyy — Copy pack

Use true project details and your normal language. The copy below never claims a placement, recruiter response, conversion rate or testimonial you have not earned. `[name]` and `[campus]` are intentional recipient fill-ins, not draft markers.

## 1. Organizer DM or email

**Subject:** A project-to-first-post session for your students

> Hi [name], I built Postifyy for people who can explain their work out loud but get stuck writing about it on LinkedIn.
>
> Could I run a free 30-minute session for [campus]? Students bring one project or internship lesson, record a short explanation, and leave with a draft they have edited themselves. Nobody has to publish, connect a LinkedIn account or buy anything to attend. The product has three free posts; I will explain that limit upfront.
>
> The useful part is learning to say what they did without inventing achievements. I can show the exact exercise first so you can decide if it fits your group.
>
> If it is not relevant, no problem. I will not add anyone to a list.

One follow-up after seven days: “Following up once on the project-writing session. Happy to send the three-question exercise even if a session does not fit.” Stop after that.

## 2. Permissioned campus broadcast or student bulletin

> Have a project you can explain to a friend but have never written about on LinkedIn?
>
> Bring it to a free 30-minute project-to-post session. We will answer three questions: what you tried, what was difficult, and what changed. Then you will edit a draft in your own voice. You decide whether to publish it.
>
> I built Postifyy, the tool we will use. It offers three free posts and one free scheduled post; no card is needed. You can also do the writing exercise in a notes app.
>
> Try the public demo first: https://postifyy.in/#try
>
> Reply to the organizer if you want the session details. Please do not forward into other groups without their admin's permission.

The organizer adds the agreed time and venue in their normal event message. Do not announce a fictional booking or call it a placement-committee recommendation.

## 3. The live exercise, spoken verbatim

> Don't tell me you're passionate. Tell me what you made. What was one part that failed? What did you change yourself? What is still unfinished?
>
> Record that explanation as if I am your teammate. Now read the draft. Underline every number, result and claim about your role. Can you defend each one? Delete anything you cannot.
>
> Choose the opening that sounds like you. Copy it or approve it only after you are happy with the exact words. A polished false claim is worse than an awkward true one.

## 4. LinkedIn demonstration post

> Your first project post does not need a success story.
>
> It needs one real decision.
>
> Say what you built. Explain one thing that broke. Tell us what you changed and what you still do not know.
>
> That gives someone a way to understand how you work. “Thrilled to announce” usually does not.
>
> I built Postifyy to help with the blank-page part: talk through the work, get draft choices, edit the wording, then decide whether to publish. The video shows the original explanation next to the draft, including the sentence I removed because it claimed too much.
>
> You get three free posts. There is also a public demo without signing in: https://postifyy.in/#try
>
> What is one project you can explain easily but have never posted about?

Attach Video A only after you have recorded that actual edit. If you did not remove a claim, change that sentence to the true edit you made.

## 5. Referral request after the user exports a draft

> You have your first draft. If a classmate has the same blank-page problem, you can send them Postifyy: https://postifyy.in/
>
> Please tell them what helped and what still needed editing. No need to recommend it if it did not work for you.

Do not turn this into “tag three friends” or withhold the user's text until they share.

## 6. Peerlist project description

**Tagline:** A voice note into a LinkedIn post you can stand behind.

> Postifyy helps students and individual professionals turn spoken project stories into LinkedIn drafts. Compare up to three versions, review the hook and wording, and approve the exact text before scheduling. The free plan includes three posts and one scheduled post. A public demo lets you try the writing flow without signing in.
>
> I would especially like feedback on where the draft loses your voice or introduces a claim you did not say. Please try it with a real project, not a generic topic.

## 7. Product Hunt maker comment

> I made Postifyy because explaining a project aloud can be easier than writing the first line about it. The workflow starts with your own explanation, offers draft choices and checks, then waits for your approval before anything goes out.
>
> It is not a promise of interviews or clients. If it gives you a clear, honest post faster, it has done its first job. The free allowance is three posts total, including one scheduling trial. The Chrome extension is still coming soon, so this launch demonstrates the web app.
>
> Tell me where it sounded unlike you. That is more useful than a score.

Share the launch to invite feedback, never votes.

## 8. Useful comment / guide introduction

> One way to start: write down a choice you made, the alternative you rejected, and what happened. That is already a useful project post. You do not need to invent a dramatic lesson or a big result.
>
> I work on a writing tool, so I am biased toward making the first draft easier. The part I would do manually either way is checking every claim against the actual project.

Only add the student guide link if the original poster asks for examples or the organizer permits resources. Do not paste it repeatedly under recruiters' posts.

## 9. X thread, secondary channel

> 1/ A project post can be four sentences: what I tried, what broke, what I changed, what I still need to learn.
>
> 2/ The weak version says “built an innovative platform.” The useful version explains one decision someone else can learn from.
>
> 3/ Read every AI draft for invented numbers and a role you did not actually play. Delete those first. Your first post does not need to sound senior.
>
> 4/ I made Postifyy to turn the spoken explanation into a draft you edit yourself. Three posts free; public demo: https://postifyy.in/#try

Use this only if you already participate on X. Do not create a second daily content job at the expense of student activation.

## Reusing the work

For the next post change the project, the decision and the actual edit on screen. Reposting the same before/after with new adjectives is not new evidence. Ask permission before showing anyone else's voice, draft or LinkedIn profile; blur identities in public examples.
