# The Steady Post — Video scripts

Use your own project/work observation and a source you actually opened. Record a browser with large text, your phone microphone and hard cuts. Show dates; hide resumes and account details. No effects or paid assets are required.

## A. Source to useful post — 60 seconds

**Where:** LinkedIn, alumni sessions, hero demonstration. **Hook, first two seconds:** “A headline is not your opinion.”

| Time | Shot / text | Voice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–8s | Open an actual article with date visible. Text: “Read the source first.” | “A headline is not your opinion. I check what the story actually says before drafting.” |
| 8–20s | Show the relevant source passage, then your one-sentence work observation. | “This is the fact. This is where it connects to work I have actually done. They are different sentences.” |
| 20–35s | In The Steady Post select the topic and show the real draft. | “Now I ask for an angle at my level. I do not need to sound like the head of the industry.” |
| 35–48s | Edit a specific unsupported or overly broad line. | “I change this because I cannot defend it. The useful draft is the one I can stand behind.” |
| 48–60s | Actual approval/export, then URL and trial limit. | “One post this week. Try five drafts for up to 14 days, then decide whether it belongs in your routine.” |

Film the product steps only after a real successful test. If publishing has not been verified, show a completed draft and say so rather than inventing a success screen.

## B. The credibility hook — 25 seconds, vertical

**Where:** personal LinkedIn, optional Shorts. **Hook:** “You don't have to have a hot take.”

| Time | Shot / text | Voice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5s | A loud opinion draft, clearly labeled example; cross out the absolute claim. | “You don't have to have a hot take.” |
| 5–14s | Replace with a question tied to one real observation. | “If you have not seen this at work, ask a useful question. Do not borrow confidence from an AI draft.” |
| 14–21s | Source and edited draft side by side. | “The Steady Post helps me find and shape an idea. I still decide what I can say.” |
| 21–25s | URL; “Source / experience / edit.” | “One true observation is enough to start.” |

## C. The second-week test — 30 seconds, vertical

**Where:** alumni follow-up and LinkedIn after an actual second-week session. **Hook:** “The first draft is the easy test.”

| Time | Shot / text | Voice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–6s | Two real dates on your own calendar. | “The first draft is the easy test. Did I come back next week?” |
| 6–18s | Choose a new source, add a new observation, edit a new draft. | “I need a different idea from my field, not last week's paragraph rewritten. I add something I noticed this week.” |
| 18–26s | Compare the two completed posts; no engagement numbers needed. | “Two posts with something real in them. That is the habit I am testing.” |
| 26–30s | Trial limit and URL. | “Try the workflow. Keep it only if week two is easier.” |

Do not fabricate a returning-user story. Until the second date exists, record only the intended exercise and label it a demonstration.

## D. The trial explanation — 20 seconds, vertical

**Where:** pricing FAQ clip. **Hook:** “Five drafts or 14 days. The first limit wins.”

| Time | Shot / text | Voice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–6s | Trial card with both limits highlighted. | “Five drafts or 14 days. The first limit wins.” |
| 6–14s | Saved draft and Pro price card. | “Your saved work stays available. More AI drafting needs Pro: ₹499 a month or ₹4,999 paid yearly.” |
| 14–20s | Actual cancel/plan information if verified, otherwise price card only. | “Read the full payment total and plan terms before choosing. You do not need a card to try it.” |

Capture source opening, selecting an angle, factual edit and confirmation once. Reuse those clean clips, but change the source and observation for each new teaching post. Do not show another person's resume or unpublished draft without permission.
