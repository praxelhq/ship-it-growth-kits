# The Steady Post — Measurement

**30-day target: 1,000 signups.** Report **unique writers with an approved/exported or published first draft** beside it, plus **writers who return in a later week**. See [the target policy](../../TARGET-POLICY.md).

## Define a real person and a real result

Count one newly created individual account once. Do not count sign-in clicks, trend views, generated drafts, repeated logins, your test accounts or clinic seats as signups. Activation is a person who reviews a draft and exports it or approves publication. Keep successful publications separate from scheduled attempts.

Retention is a second independently initiated completed draft in a later week. A four-week sample badge in the hero is not measured retention. A reminder-driven return can be useful, but keep it distinguishable from independent adoption.

## Useful events

`landing_view`, `signin_started`, `account_created`, `field_context_saved`, `trend_opened`, `source_opened`, `angle_selected`, `draft_generated`, `draft_edited`, `draft_approved`, `draft_exported`, `publish_succeeded`, `trial_limit_reached`, `next_week_draft_completed`.

Store no resume text or draft body in analytics. Count source opening without storing private browsing history. Tag campaigns coarsely, such as `alumni_session_1`; no names or contact information in public URLs. A heatmap cannot prove an account exists or a post went live.

## The arithmetic you must not hide

`02` schedules three accepted sessions, six owned posts, twenty personal invitations and one Peerlist listing. Its assumptions yield **26–128 new accounts before overlap**. Those figures are not a sufficient acquisition plan for 1,000. If visitor-to-account is 25%, the target needs 4,000 unique qualified visitors; if it is 10%, it needs 10,000. Use your measured rate after the first fifty visitors.

To expand, first prove that the source/angle workflow produces a second week of use. Then ask satisfied organizers for introductions to additional professional circles. Do not solve the gap by counting every trend impression or paying for traffic.

| Day 21 | 500 | Real session permissions and accounts, introductions, voluntary forwarding. | Reforecast required visitors; report the gap if partner expansion is absent. |
| Day 28 | 850 | Second-week writers, successful publications, trial exhaustion and paid outcomes. | Help users finish; stop promoting a misleading or unreliable publication flow. |
| Day 30 | 1,000 | Activated writers and independent later-week writers. | Publish actual totals, including a missed target, and a measured next plan. |

These are decision milestones, not predictions. Each week's ledger should contain unique visitors, new accounts, activated writers, successful publishers, later-week writers, time to first useful draft, source rejection reasons, errors and hours spent.

## Diagnose by the missing step

If visitors do not start sign-in, ask whether connecting LinkedIn feels premature. If accounts do not save field context, shorten the first task. If trends are opened but not selected, relevance is failing. If drafts are generated but not approved, inspect five real rejected drafts with permission; do not add more trend volume. If approved posts are not published, inspect permissions and the final confirmation flow. If week one works but week two does not, simplify the weekly ritual before adding notifications or a longer streak ladder.

The final handoff is: unique new accounts; activated writers; confirmed publishers; independent later-week writers; trial-to-paid starts; refunds; best acquisition source; most common rejected angle. A source-linked draft is valuable only when the person finds something true of their own to say.
