# The Steady Post — Copy pack

Use one actual source and one true observation in every public example. Never claim a source proves your opinion when it does not. Recipient fill-ins `[name]` and `[group]` are intentional.

## 1. Alumni organizer invitation

**Subject:** A free source-to-post session for your early-career members

> Hi [name], I am building The Steady Post, a tool for turning a story from your field into a LinkedIn draft you can edit and approve.
>
> Could I show a 30-minute exercise to [group]? Each person chooses a source, checks what it actually says, adds one observation from their work, and leaves with a draft. Nobody needs to publish or buy anything to participate. The free trial is 14 days or five AI drafts, whichever comes first.
>
> I am looking for people who want to write once a week without pretending to be experts. Happy to show you the exercise before you decide whether it belongs in your group.

Follow up once after seven days, then stop. Do not tell the organizer their members “need personal branding.”

## 2. Permissioned bulletin / group announcement

> Read interesting stories in your field but never know what to say about them?
>
> Bring one article and one work observation to a free source-to-post session. We will separate the source's facts from our own opinion, then edit a short LinkedIn draft. Publishing is optional.
>
> I built The Steady Post, which we will demonstrate. Trial: 14 days or five drafts, whichever comes first, no card. You can also do the exercise in a notes app.
>
> Details through the organizer. Product: https://thesteadypost.app/

Only the organizer adds the agreed date/time after accepting. Do not pretend an event is booked.

## 3. LinkedIn launch post

> A headline is not a point of view.
>
> Before I write about a trend, I want to answer three questions: what does the source actually say, where have I seen this in real work, and what am I uncertain about?
>
> The first answer is a fact check. The second is my contribution. The third stops the post sounding more confident than I am.
>
> I am building The Steady Post around that weekly routine: source-linked ideas from your field, a draft shaped by your context, and your approval before anything is published.
>
> The attached recording shows the source and my final edit, not just an AI score. Try it if the blank page keeps winning: https://thesteadypost.app/
>
> Trial is 14 days or five drafts, whichever comes first. What makes you reject a suggested post idea?

Attach a real recording. Do not say a trend is current if the source is old.

## 4. Useful source-checking post / comment

> Three things I check before turning an article into a LinkedIn post:
>
> The date. Whether the claim is measured or predicted. Whether my draft says something stronger than the source.
>
> Then I add a work example I can actually defend. If I do not have one, I write a question instead of pretending to have a lesson.
>
> I build a writing tool, so I am interested in this. I would still do these checks without one.

In someone else's thread omit the link unless asked. Change the example each time; repeated identical comments are not conversation.

## 5. One-to-one invitation

> Hi [name], I noticed you write/read about your field. I am testing a small weekly exercise: pick a source, add one observation from work, edit a short post. Is choosing an angle something you find annoying, or is writing already easy for you?

If they ask to try:

> Here it is: https://thesteadypost.app/ . It uses LinkedIn sign-in and gives a 14-day or five-draft trial, whichever comes first. I would value a rejected idea as much as a good draft: tell me where it misunderstood your field. No need to publish the result.

Do not send a reminder to someone who declines. Do not use a scraped career history to fake familiarity.

## 6. Peerlist entry

**Tagline:** A sourced idea from your field into one weekly LinkedIn post.

> The Steady Post helps individual professionals choose a credible angle, draft in their own voice and confirm every publication. It brings together source-linked trends, personal context, editable drafts, carousels and a weekly goal. The trial ends after 14 days or five AI drafts, whichever comes first; Pro is ₹499/month.
>
> Please test whether the suggested angle fits your actual experience. A polished draft on a topic you cannot defend is not a success.

## 7. Optional Product Hunt maker comment

> I built this for people who read about their field but disappear from LinkedIn for weeks. The difficult part is not scheduling; it is choosing something worth saying and keeping the draft honest.
>
> The workflow starts with a source and your context, then waits for your edits and confirmation. Scheduling is optional. I am looking for feedback on irrelevant trends, unsupported claims and the second-week return experience. The free trial is limited to 14 days or five drafts, not unlimited generation.

Do not ask for upvotes. Do not claim that the tool makes someone a thought leader.

## 8. Returning-user note, only to opted-in users

> Did last week's draft help you say something you actually meant? If you want to write again, choose one new source and add one observation from this week. Your next post does not need to outperform the last one.
>
> If the suggestions missed your field, reply with what felt wrong. You can stop these notes at any time.

Use the actual unsubscribe control. A reminder-driven return is useful, but report it separately from someone returning unprompted.
