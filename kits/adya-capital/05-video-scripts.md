# Adya Capital — Video scripts

Record your own voice and screen. Use a synthetic deck explicitly labelled or a redacted deck with permission. Never expose an applicant's identity, file link, contact details or fundraising material.

## A. What the fee buys — 75 seconds

**Hook:** “₹499 should buy feedback, not a funding promise.”

| Time | Screen / on-screen text | Spoken line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–10s | Fee and terms together | “An open Adya application costs ₹499. Invited applications are free. Neither buys admission.” |
| 10–28s | A clearly labelled example review | “Here's the review output. We examine the deck against these dimensions and explain the priorities.” |
| 28–45s | One unclear traction slide, then dated evidence | “This is one change a founder can act on: define the number, give it a date, and show what it supports.” |
| 45–60s | Status route, synthetic ticket hidden | “Receipt, feedback and admission are separate states. The queue and expected date must be clear before payment.” |
| 60–75s | FAQ and sample-review link | “Read the terms and sample first. Apply only if the feedback itself is worth it to you.” |

Use on the landing page and accepted organizer session. If the sample review or queue is not live, record a build update, not a product-ready claim.

## B. Three traction questions — 25 seconds

**Hook:** “What does your ‘active user’ number mean?” Shots: 0–7s synthetic slide saying “users”; 7–17s add definition and reporting period; 17–25s add the next milestone. Say: “A reader needs the unit, date and behavior. Fix these before you ask for a bigger number.” Post to LinkedIn; no product pitch until a final plain-text URL.

## C. Invitation is not investment — 20 seconds

**Hook:** “An invite is not an investment.” Show three separate cards: invitation, review, admission. Say: “An invitation changes the application route. A review gives feedback. Admission is a separate decision. None is a promise of capital.” Final screen repeats **“₹499 open review; invited applications free.”** Use with #2 in `03`.

## D. Keep your deck private — 20 seconds

**Hook:** “Don't put a confidential deck in a public demo.” Film yourself replacing real information with a synthetic example, then opening the privacy explanation. Say: “Ask who sees your deck and how it is removed. A useful review should not require public exposure of your company's private details.” Post as a trust explanation, not a claim that every safeguard was audited.

Use large captions, steady cuts and a quiet room. No investor logos, mock endorsement clips or images of real founders without consent. Keep a recording of the actual application terms shown when the clip was made.
