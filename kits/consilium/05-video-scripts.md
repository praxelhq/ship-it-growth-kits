# Consilium — Video scripts

Use a clearly synthetic firm and fictional applicant brief. Never expose actual applicant data or imply a real consultant authorized the demo without permission.

## A · A claim is not an endorsement · 60 seconds · firm walkthrough / LinkedIn

0–2s hook: **“What does this Verified badge actually mean?”** Show the label enlarged. 2–15s: open a synthetic profile, text “Example firm”; say “A business owner checks the programme and service information against their official pages.” 15–30s: show the claim/correction review path using a controlled account: “Signing in doesn't prove ownership. The correction still needs review.” 30–45s: show a Sponsored slot separate from match results: “Paid visibility is labelled. It doesn't buy a better fit score or a nicer review.” 45–60s: “Basic public inclusion is free; owner editing is a listed paid Verified benefit. Check exact entitlement and current paid terms before purchasing. Consilium is a comparison starting point, not an admissions guarantee.” Hold URL.

If a private claim workflow isn't ready, film the public explanation and label the pending step; do not simulate an approval as a completed real event.

## B · Price on request · 20 seconds · own feed

Hook 0–2s: **“Unknown price doesn't mean cheap.”** Show an example unknown-price card. 2–10s: “Ask what services, coach time and application count the quote includes.” 10–17s: show the linked source and missing field: “A directory should show what it knows and what you still need to confirm.” 17–20s: “Compare facts before choosing.” URL optional.

## C · The paid-ranking line · 25 seconds · consultant objections

Hook: **“Can a firm pay to become your best match?”** 0–8s: show fit results and a separate Sponsored card. 8–18s: “On Consilium, sponsorship must stay outside the fit ranking. Verified checks business details, not outcomes.” 18–25s: “If a display blurs those lines, tell me. That's a product error to fix, not a clever sales offer.”

Record large readable labels and one comparison at a time. Captions and hard cuts are enough. No university logo should imply a partnership or acceptance guarantee.
