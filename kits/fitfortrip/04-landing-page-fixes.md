# FitForTrip — Landing-page fixes

[Home](https://fitfortrip.in/) and [actual app entry](https://fitfortrip.in/app) were inspected 29 October. The app opens an account form; no account, private trip or retailer purchase was completed by this kit.

## 1. Make live-app and waitlist language agree

Current H1: **“Never pack a bad fit again.”** CTA: **“Start planning in the app.”** The page still contains update/waitlist language, while `/app` offers **“Create account.”**

Use one primary route: **“Plan my first trip”**, with **“Create a free account to save your plan”** before the handoff. Keep updates secondary. Don't make users wonder whether the app exists or whether a signup only joins a list.

## 2. Prove one actual trip-to-list flow

Current page shows attractive Paris mockups. Add a real supported demonstration from destination/dates through daily looks to saved packing list, labelled **“Actual app example.”** If that flow isn't ready, say which steps work rather than letting a mockup imply completion.

## 3. Replace false competitor absolutes

The comparison suggests traditional closet apps give generic styling and lack trip focus. Whering actually offers planning/packing features. Replacement: **“Trip-first setup: begin with destination and days, then choose looks. No full-closet catalogue required to begin.”** Describe your workflow advantage without inventing missing competitor features.

## 4. Keep samples labelled at every shopping/weather decision

Retail cards explicitly show illustrative products/prices; keep that label beside the action. Use **“Explore similar items at the retailer; price and stock checked there.”** Sample temperatures should read **“Illustrative destination guidance—check a current forecast before departure.”** No guaranteed weather fit.

## 5. Give a useful no-purchase path

Let the packing list distinguish **“Already own”** and **“May need”** without pressuring a purchase. If the actual app doesn't support this fully, avoid promising it until built. A shopping click is not proof the outfit plan helped.

## 6. Explain saving, editing and private input

Test fresh signup, save/reload, edit and failed-network recovery with your own sample trip. Say what is stored and how to delete it. Don't request passport/ticket data or precise accommodation details for outfit planning. Do not infer cloud persistence from a successful account screen.

## 7. Check the phone end-to-end, not only the hero

Metadata: **“FitForTrip — plan outfits for the trip you've booked.”** Description: **“Daily looks and a usable packing list. Start with one trip and clothes you own.”** Test date input, keyboard, text zoom, moodboard loading and the actual saved-list state on a phone. Keep the core list usable before decorative imagery finishes.
