# lowercase — Landing page conversion fixes

**Fix 1 is not an optimisation. It is the precondition for the entire kit.** Your window in the cluster schedule is days 17–21, which gives you two weeks — spend the first on this.

**Your page is well written.** A clear H1 (*"Learn case interviews by doing"*), a subheadline that actually explains the product, three genuinely differentiated levels, a six-part rubric, honest localised pricing, and the best-explained peer feature in the cluster. Two of the three fixes below are about things you have and don't say.

---

## Fix 1 — Open a free level. Nothing else matters until you do. 🔴🔴

**There is no free tier, no trial, no demo and no sample case.** The only way to experience lowercase is to pay ₹3,499.

Every competitor in your cohort has a door: CaseForge gives a free case per function, CaseCrack five free items, CaseFlex three reps a week, Skill Prep a free-forever tier plus an ungated question in the hero, Role.via a free interview and a free GD.

**You have no door, and a 1,000-signup target.** 1,000 × ₹3,499 is ₹35 lakh in thirty days from students four weeks before their interviews. The target is not reachable by distribution — this is arithmetic, not pessimism.

**It's also the wrong shape for what you sell.** You are the only product here whose claim is *"I can teach you"*. That claim can only be tested by being taught something. A paywall in front of a teaching product asks a beginner to believe the one thing she has no way to check.

### What to open

**Foundations — free, permanently.** The concept lessons, the mastery checks, and the focused one-or-two-concept cases.

That's a real amount of value and it's the right amount, because:

- **It proves you can teach**, which is the only objection that matters.
- **It qualifies exactly the user you want.** Someone who finishes Foundations is a beginner who's engaged with a curriculum — the highest-intent buyer for levels 2 and 3 you could possibly have.
- **It's the content that makes your SEO work** (`03` #5). Free concept pages and a free first level are the same asset.
- **Levels 2 and 3 are still worth ₹3,999.** The hard cases, the spaced review, the voice interviewer and the unlocking issue tree are all still paid. You are not giving away the product.

**Say it plainly in the hero:** *"Foundations is free. Always."*

**Expect this trade, and tell the board about it in advance:** signups go up a lot, revenue per visitor goes down. That is the correct trade for a month whose target is signups, and `07` reports both numbers so nobody argues about it in retrospect.

## Fix 2 — Answer "does my friend need to pay?" in the hero 🔴

Your page says *"Add friends by username, see who's online, and send a case invite"* and *"Unlimited cases with friends"*. **It never says whether the friend needs a paid plan.**

That silence is expensive in both directions:

- A prospective buyer assumes yes, and concludes the feature is useless to her — because she cannot get a friend to spend ₹3,499 either.
- And **if the answer is no, you have a referral loop built into your product that nobody knows about.** One paying user becomes two accounts, a shared habit, and far better retention than either would have alone.

**Make it a no, and make it a headline:**

> **One plan covers both of you.** Invite anyone by username and run unlimited cases together — they don't need to pay.

**This is the cheapest 1,000 signups available to anyone in this cluster** and you're the only one who can run it. It's also the single best retention mechanic in the cohort: a shared commitment with a specific person is far stickier than a streak.

If the answer is currently yes, **change it.** The lost revenue from a free partner is trivially smaller than the value of every user bringing one.

## Fix 3 — Lead with the beginner, by name 🔴

> *"Learn case interviews by doing"* / *"lowercase teaches you the concepts, then puts you inside real interview cases until breaking down a problem feels natural."*

That's good copy and it still doesn't say who it's for. A beginner reading it can't tell whether she's qualified, and **"am I too far behind for this?" is the exact anxiety stopping her.**

Name her:

> # Opened a casebook and didn't understand page one?
> ### That's not you — it's that a casebook is an exam and nobody gave you the syllabus. lowercase teaches the concepts first, then the cases, until breaking down a problem feels natural.
>
> **Foundations is free. Always.**
> `[Start Foundations]`   `[See how it works]`

**Why this is worth the change:** every other landing page in this market implicitly tells her she should already know. Yours can tell her it's fine that she doesn't, and that's a segment nobody is competing for.

---

## Fix 4 — Put "switch roles" in the hero 🟠

Buried in your page is the best single insight anyone in this cluster has written:

> *"Switch roles, because interviewing teaches you what good looks like."*

That's true, it's non-obvious, and **no competitor can offer it.** It's also a reason to bring a friend, which makes it a growth feature as well as a product one.

Put it above the fold with the console:

> **Run the case, not just answer it.** The interviewer works from a full console — the facts, the hints, the issue tree. Then you switch. **Interviewing someone else is the fastest way to learn what a good answer sounds like.**

---

## Fix 5 — Make the levels concrete, with a free sample of each 🟠

Your three levels are a real differentiator and currently described abstractly. Make them visible, with a real prompt from each:

| Level | What it is | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 · Foundations** *(free)* | Short lessons on the ideas every case relies on, then focused cases using one or two | *"A café's profits fell 15%. Revenue is flat."* |
| **2 · Applied** | More than one moving part. Lead with a hypothesis, prioritise branches, synthesise mid-case | *"A D2C brand is growing revenue and losing money faster."* |
| **3 · Final Round** | Ambiguous prompts, several issues at once, pushback from the interviewer | *"Our client is considering an acquisition. Thoughts?"* |

**The level-3 example should look intimidating.** It makes level 1 feel like a sensible place to start, which is exactly what you want a beginner to conclude.

---

## Fix 6 — Say the pricing honesty out loud 🟠

Your pricing is the most honest in the cluster and you state it like fine print: *"Every plan is a one-time payment and ends on its own."*

**That's a selling point. Lead with it:**

> **One payment. No subscription. No auto-renewal. It ends on its own.**

Students have been burned by trials that auto-charge, and your nearest cohort-mate charges Indian students $19/month on a recurring card. **Make the contrast explicit** — it costs you nothing and it's true.

**And fix the Sprint anomaly:** ₹3,499 for 4 weeks against ₹3,999 for 3 months makes Sprint look like a trap, and students discuss pricing with each other. Either drop Sprint or widen the gap to something defensible.

---

## Fix 7 — Two small things 🔵

- **Check the `#` anchors resolve.** Several section links on your page are bare anchors. If *"See how it works"* doesn't scroll anywhere, your main secondary CTA is broken.
- **Make the pricing page reachable from the homepage.** It exists at `/pricing` and isn't clearly linked. **A visitor who can't find the price assumes it's expensive and leaves** — which, given your pricing is your honest differentiator, is a loss you're choosing for no reason.

## The test to run before you launch

**Give your phone to someone who has never done a case and say nothing.** Three things:

1. Does she work out within ten seconds that this is for someone who doesn't know anything yet?
2. **Does she start a free lesson without hitting a paywall?**
3. Does she finish the lesson and understand one concept she didn't before?

If all three, you have the strongest product in this cluster and a month of distribution ahead of you. **If the second one fails, nothing else in this kit will work** — and no amount of posting will fix it.
