# lowercase — Video scripts and shot lists

Scripts and shot lists only — **you record these on your phone.**

Your video strategy follows your product: **teach, don't demo.** You are the only project in this cluster whose claim is "I can teach you", and a video is the cheapest possible proof of that claim — someone watches 40 seconds, understands something she didn't, and now believes you.

**So your main format is a concept explainer, not a product tour.** Those videos are also your best content for the beginner communities in `02`, because they're useful with no product attached.

All vertical 9:16, captions burned in.

## Format A — The concept explainer (your workhorse; make 10)

**Length:** 35–50 seconds · **Where:** Instagram, LinkedIn, YouTube Shorts, Reddit comments, your concept pages

Teach one idea completely. **No product shot until the last four seconds.**

### Template — "What is an issue tree?"

| Time | On screen | You say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–4s | Text: **"What is an issue tree?"** | "Everyone uses this phrase and nobody explains it. It takes thirty seconds." |
| 4–12s | Paper, you writing `Profit` | "Start with the thing you care about. Profit." |
| 12–22s | Branching to `Revenue` − `Cost` | "Break it into parts that don't overlap and together cover all of it. Profit is revenue minus cost. That's it. That's an issue tree." |
| 22–34s | Revenue branching to `Price × Volume` | "Then break those down. Revenue is price times volume. Cost is fixed plus variable. Now you have somewhere to look instead of a feeling." |
| 34–44s | The full tree, and the word **MECE** written beside it | "When people say MECE, they mean exactly this — the branches don't overlap, and nothing's missing. It's a description, not a technique." |
| 44–50s | End card | "Foundations is free. trylowercase.app" |

### The ten concepts

In posting order — the first three are the ones beginners search for most:

1. **What is an issue tree?** (and what MECE actually means)
2. **What is a hypothesis in a case?** — *a guess at the answer you then test, not a list of things you'll look at.* The most commonly misunderstood idea in case prep.
3. **How to size a market from a population base**
4. **Profitability decomposition** — the one tree that solves a third of all cases
5. **Fixed vs variable cost**, and why interviewers care
6. **Price vs volume trade-off**
7. **How to structure a market-entry case**
8. **What "synthesise" means** when an interviewer asks you to
9. **How to do mental maths in a case** without losing the thread
10. **Why you should say your structure out loud before you start**

### Rules

- **Teach it completely.** Never "full explanation at the link". The whole point is proving you can teach — a teaser proves the opposite.
- **Paper and a marker beats a screen recording** for this format. Handwriting a tree is the most watchable thing you can film and it looks like teaching rather than marketing.
- **"It takes thirty seconds" in the opening** is the hook. It promises the thing beginners most want: that this is less complicated than they fear.
- **No face needed**, which means you can batch five in an afternoon.
- **Name the jargon and then deflate it.** *"MECE is a description, not a technique"* is the kind of line that gets screenshotted, because it relieves anxiety.

### Shot list

1. **Overhead paper shot.** Phone propped on books, lens straight down, daylight from a window. Black marker, white unlined paper.
2. **Write at normal speed, speed up 1.5–2× in editing.**
3. **Hold the finished tree for two full seconds.** That's the screenshot.
4. Text overlay for the concept name and the jargon word.

## Format B — "I didn't understand page one" (your most important video)

**Length:** 40–50 seconds · **Where:** LinkedIn (pinned), Instagram, r/MBA comments, your landing page

This is `03` #2 on camera. It's the video that defines who you're for.

> **(0–6s, your face, close)** "I didn't understand the first page of the casebook. Not the hard part. The first page."
>
> **(6–16s, screen or paper: a real casebook page with jargon circled)** "It used the word MECE like I'd know it. Drew something it called an issue tree without explaining what made it one. Then asked me to solve a profitability case."
>
> **(16–26s, your face)** "I assumed that meant I wasn't cut out for consulting. I know a lot of people who quietly decided the same thing and stopped."
>
> **(26–38s, your face)** "The actual problem took me months to see: **a casebook is an exam, and nobody gave me the syllabus.** Every tool I tried scored me 34 out of 100 and told me I lacked structure. That's true and completely useless. I didn't need to be told I lacked structure. I needed someone to explain what structure was."
>
> **(38–48s, the Foundations lessons, briefly)** "So that's what this is. Concepts first, with checks that you've actually got them. Then small cases. Then hard ones."
>
> **(48–52s)** "Foundations is free. trylowercase.app. If you ever felt stupid opening a casebook — it wasn't you."

**Pin this to your LinkedIn profile.** The last line is the whole positioning and it's the reason people will send it to a friend.

## Format C — The role-switch video (your differentiator, and a referral ask)

**Length:** 35–45 seconds · **Where:** LinkedIn, campus groups, "find a case partner" threads, the landing page

**Film this with an actual friend.** Two people on two phones is the point.

> **(0–6s, you and a friend, split screen or side by side)** "The most useful hour of case prep I did wasn't doing a case. It was running one."
>
> **(6–18s, screen recording of the interviewer console)** "One of us has the console — the facts, the hints, the issue tree. The other only knows what they've uncovered. It's the real asymmetry of an interview, which you can't get practising alone."
>
> **(18–30s, your friend flailing slightly, then you deciding whether to push)** "And when you're the interviewer, you watch someone take the exact wrong turn you take. You see it from outside. That's the bit that changed how I answer."
>
> **(30–40s)** "Then you switch."
>
> **(40–45s, end card)** "**One plan covers both of you.** trylowercase.app"

**That last line is the growth mechanic** (`04` Fix 2) and it belongs on screen, not just spoken — it's the reason someone sends this video to the person they're preparing with.

## Format D — The honest comparison (make 1, week 2)

**Length:** 30–40 seconds · **Where:** LinkedIn, Reddit replies, anyone asking which tool to use

There are ten case tools in your own cohort. Pretending otherwise insults the audience.

> **(0–6s, your face)** "There are about ten case-prep tools. Honest answer on when to use mine and when not to."
>
> **(6–18s)** "If you already know what an issue tree is and you want reps — timed cases, scored, daily — use one of the others. Several are genuinely good and some are cheaper. I'm not the right thing for you."
>
> **(18–30s)** "If you opened a casebook and didn't understand page one, every one of those tools will score you badly and tell you that you lacked structure. That's where this is different — it teaches the concepts first, then builds up."
>
> **(30–40s)** "Foundations is free, so you can find out which of those two people you are before paying anything. trylowercase.app"

**Why this is one of your strongest videos:** telling a segment of your audience to use a competitor is the most credible thing you can do, and it makes the beginner trust the part that applies to her.

## Format E — The pair diary (optional, weekly, from week 2)

Once you have pairs using it, film a 25-second update with one of them (with permission).

> **(0–6s, two people)** "Week 2 of going through Foundations together."
> **(6–18s)** "We do two a week. He's better at the maths, I'm better at the structure, and swapping roles made both of us notice it."
> **(18–25s)** "trylowercase.app — one plan covers both of you."

**Why this earns its place:** your product is better for two people than one, and the only way to show that is to film two people. It also makes the referral ask implicit rather than stated.

## Production rules

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Teach completely, every time** | Your claim is that you can teach. A teaser disproves it. |
| **Overhead paper, black marker, daylight** | Handwriting a tree is the most watchable and most on-brand shot you have |
| **Vertical 9:16, captions burned in** | Muted autoplay everywhere |
| **Deflate the jargon** | "MECE is a description, not a technique" relieves anxiety, and relief gets shared |
| **No urgency, ever** | You're a curriculum. "Your interview is Friday" brings the wrong buyer. |
| **"One plan covers both of you" on screen** in every peer video | It's the referral mechanic and it has to be seen, not just heard |
| **No face needed for Format A** | Which is why you can batch five in an afternoon |
| **Full URL `trylowercase.app`** captioned | Four cohort-mates have confusable names |

**Make Format B first** — it defines who you're for and it's the one to pin. **Then batch five Format A explainers in one afternoon** and post two a week; they double as the content for your concept pages (`03` #5), so the same afternoon produces both your video calendar and your SEO. Format C comes as soon as you have one friend to film with, and it's the one that brings you pairs.
