# lowercase — Distribution map

**Read this first:** two things make your channel plan different from everyone else's in this cluster.

1. **Nothing here works until there is a free way in** (`04` Fix 1). Driving 10,000 visitors at a ₹3,499 hard paywall produces a handful of sales and no signups. **This is the one kit in the cluster where the page fix isn't an optimisation — it's the precondition.**
2. **Your audience is beginners, not panicking finalists.** That means you fish in different water: the people asking *"how do I start"*, not the people asking *"my interview is Friday"*. Those are different threads, different communities, and — usefully — **far less contested by your nine cohort-mates.**

**Your window in the [cluster schedule](../../shared/case-prep-cluster.md) is days 17–21**, the last slot, and it suits you: beginners aren't a seasonal audience and you lose nothing by going late.

Reach estimates state their basis. **Verify before planning around them.**

## Tier 1 — Where beginners and pairs are 🔴

This is your whole positioning and it's a question people ask constantly in public.

**The pattern across all of these: answer the beginner's question completely, for free, and the product is the obvious next step.** You are the only one in this cluster who can do that credibly, because teaching is what you actually sell.

### 2. Pairs and friend groups (your referral loop)

**Your product is better for two people than for one, and no cohort-mate can say that.** So stop acquiring individuals and start acquiring pairs.

| Play | Reach | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The "bring a friend" offer** | Multiplies everything else | *"Your plan covers both of you."* Make it the headline of every piece of copy (`04` Fix 2). One paying user becomes two accounts and a shared habit. |
| **Campus pair recruitment** | 60–400 per group, 30–60% open | Post in batch groups asking *"who wants a case partner?"* — not *"try my product"*. **You are solving the actual problem they have**, which is that they can't find a partner. |
| **"Find a case partner" threads** on Reddit, Discord, campus groups | 50–500 each | These exist everywhere and they're full of people whose problem your product solves. Be the answer in the thread. |
| **Existing study pairs** | — | Ask every user: *"who are you preparing with?"* Then invite that person directly. Highest-converting message you'll send. |

### 3. Campus case clubs — a curriculum is what they need

**You are the only project in this cluster with a syllabus.** That's the pitch.

**The honest read, and it's blunter than the other kits in this cluster.**

**With a hard ₹3,499 paywall, none of this converts into signups.** 16,000 visitors at a no-trial paywall produces maybe 30–80 paying users — a respectable small business and a catastrophic miss against a 1,000-signup target. **The number is not reachable by distribution.**

**With a free Foundations level, the same traffic converts at 10–14%** — beginners sign up readily when the first lesson is free — and the high end of this plan clears 1,000.

So the ranked levers for your month are:

1. **A free way in** (`04` Fix 1). Nothing else is close. This is a product decision, not a marketing one, and it's day 1.
2. **"Your plan covers both of you"** (`04` Fix 2). Your referral loop is already built and currently unadvertised. It is the cheapest 1,000 signups available to anyone in this cluster.
3. **Four or five clubs adopting the curriculum.** A syllabus is what a club actually needs and can't write, and you're the only one with one.

**One thing to tell the board plainly:** signups and revenue pull in opposite directions for you this month in a way they don't for your cohort-mates. A free level will raise signups and lower average revenue per visitor. `07` reports both, so the trade is visible rather than argued about later.
