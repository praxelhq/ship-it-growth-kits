## 1. The beginner answer (your highest-volume play — use it 30+ times)

On Reddit, Quora, Discord and YouTube, whenever someone asks how to start. **Search `how to start case prep` and `beginner case interview` sorted by new, every single day.**

> The thing nobody tells beginners: a casebook assumes you already know about six concepts it never explains. That's why page one feels impossible and you conclude you're bad at this. You're not — you've been handed the exam before the syllabus.
>
> **The order that actually works:**
>
> **1. Learn what an issue tree is**, properly, before you touch a case. It's just breaking a problem into parts where the parts don't overlap and together cover everything. Profit = revenue − cost. Revenue = price × volume. That's an issue tree. That's the whole idea.
>
> **2. Learn five concepts cold:** profitability decomposition, market sizing from a population base, fixed vs variable cost, price vs volume trade-off, and what a hypothesis actually is (a guess at the answer you then test — not a list of things you'll look at).
>
> **3. Then do cases, but tiny ones.** One or two concepts at a time. A full-length ambiguous case as your first attempt teaches you nothing except that you're lost.
>
> **4. Only then do full cases**, and only then with pushback.
>
> **5. Interview someone else.** Genuinely the fastest thing on this list. Running a case for a friend — holding the facts, watching them flail where you flailed — teaches you what good looks like faster than ten more attempts of your own.
>
> I built a tool that does this in that order (trylowercase.app — I'm the founder, so weigh it accordingly). But the sequence above is free and it's the actual content of this answer. Most people fail case prep because they start at step 3.

**Why this works everywhere:** it's a complete, genuinely helpful answer that works with the link deleted, it diagnoses a real and rarely-named experience, and it demonstrates that you teach. **Post variants of this thirty times this month.** It's the highest-return hour in your kit.
