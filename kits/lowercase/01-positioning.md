# lowercase — Positioning

## Start here: you have no free tier, and that is the whole problem

**There is no free trial, no demo, no sample case, and no free tier.** The only way a student can experience lowercase is to pay **₹3,499**.

Every competitor in your own Ship It cohort has a way in:

| Project | Free entry |
| --- | --- |
| **CaseForge** `caseforge.in` | One free case per function |
| **CaseCrack** `casecrack.live` | Five free items, unlimited retries |
| **CaseFlex** `caseflex.app` | 3 reps/week free, forever |
| **Skill Prep** `skillprepp.space` | Free forever tier, plus a live question in the hero |
| **Role.via** `rolevia.xyz` | 1 free interview + 1 free GD |
| **lowercase** | **Nothing** |

**Your 1,000-signup target is arithmetically impossible with a hard paywall.** 1,000 students × ₹3,499 is ₹35 lakh in a month, from an audience with no money, four weeks before their interviews. That is not a conversion problem you can fix with copy.

**So the single most important decision in this kit is `04` Fix 1: open a free level.** Everything else here assumes you do. If you don't, tell the board honestly that the target is unreachable by design, and measure something else — because no amount of distribution rescues a funnel with no entrance.

The good news: **you have the strongest product thesis in the cluster**, and two assets nobody else has.

## What you have that nobody else does

**1. You're the only one who teaches.** Your own line: *"lowercase teaches you the concepts, then puts you inside real interview cases until breaking down a problem feels natural."* Ten competitors test. **You're the only one with a curriculum** — concept lessons with mastery checks, three levels (Foundations → Applied → Final Round), spaced mixed review, and the issue tree made visible.

That matters because **a case-practice tool is useless to someone who doesn't yet know what she's doing wrong.** A beginner who gets scored 34/100 and told she "lacked structure" learns nothing. You're the only product in the cluster that answers *what should I have done instead*.

**2. Peer practice with an interviewer console.** *"The interviewer runs the case from a full console. The candidate works from what they've uncovered"*, plus *"switch roles, because interviewing teaches you what good looks like."*

**That last clause is a genuine insight and it's buried.** Interviewing someone else is the fastest way to learn what a good answer sounds like, and you're the only product that makes it possible. It is also — and this is the part to act on — **a growth mechanic**, because practising with a friend means bringing a friend. See below.

**3. Honest, localised, well-structured pricing.** ₹3,499 / ₹3,999 / ₹4,999, in rupees, one-time, *"ends on its own"*, no auto-renewal. Compare CaseFlex, which charges Indian students $19/month. **You got this right and you should say so louder** — "no auto-renewal, no lock-in" is a real differentiator to a student who has been burned by a subscription.

## Who your first 1,000 users actually are

**The beginner.** Specifically: **the first-year MBA or final-year student who has decided to try for consulting, has opened a casebook, and found it assumed knowledge she doesn't have.**

In priority order:

1. **Beginners, 3–6 months out from interviews.** The person who doesn't know what an issue tree is. **Every competitor fails her** — they test, and testing a beginner just tells her she's bad. Your Foundations level is the only thing in this market built for her, and she is the largest underserved segment in it.
2. **Pairs and friend groups.** Two people who've agreed to prepare together. **This is your highest-value acquisition unit** because your product is better for them than for anyone else, and one of them brings the other.
3. **Pre-MBA candidates with months, not weeks.** Your 3- and 6-month plans are built for a horizon nobody else in this cluster serves — everyone else sells a four-week panic. **Your plan lengths are a strategic asset, not just SKUs.**
4. **Campus case clubs** running structured preparation programmes. A curriculum is exactly what a club needs and can't write.

**Who is not your user:** the candidate with a shortlist on Friday. She needs reps, not lessons, and four cohort-mates serve her better. **Don't write urgency copy — it's the wrong product and it attracts the users who'll be most disappointed.**

## The one-line pitch

Lead with teaching, because it's the only thing that's actually yours:

> **Everyone else tests you. lowercase teaches you first — the concepts, then the cases, until breaking down a problem feels natural.**

For the beginner, who is your real market:

> **Don't know what an issue tree is? Start at Foundations. Every other case tool assumes you already know.**

For a pair:

> **Practise with a friend — one of you runs the case from the interviewer console, then switch. Interviewing someone else is the fastest way to learn what a good answer sounds like.**

## The wedge use case

**"I opened a casebook and didn't understand the first page."**

That is an extremely common, rarely-admitted experience, and you are the only product in this market that answers it. Everyone else's landing page implicitly tells her she should already know — yours can tell her it's fine that she doesn't.

Lead with that and you own a segment nobody is competing for.

## The growth mechanic you already built and aren't using

**Peer practice is a referral loop, and it's sitting idle.**

*"Add friends by username, see who's online, and send a case invite"* means a user's natural next action is **to bring someone.** No other project in this cluster has that.

**But one question decides whether it's a growth engine or a dead end, and your page doesn't answer it: does the friend need a paid plan to join a case?**

- **If yes** — you have no loop. A paying user cannot practise with anyone until she convinces a friend to spend ₹3,499, and she won't.
- **If no** — **this is your entire growth strategy and nowhere on your site says so.**

**Make it a no, loudly** (`04` Fix 2). *"Your plan covers both of you — invite anyone, free."* One paying user becomes two accounts, a shared habit, and far better retention than either would have alone. That is the cheapest 1,000 signups available to anyone in this cluster, and you're the only one who can run it.

## Why someone picks you over the alternatives

1. **The nine others in this cohort.** You beat them on teaching, on the peer console, and on honest non-renewing pricing. **You lose to all of them on trying it** until there's a free level.
2. **A casebook.** Free, trusted, and the thing that defeated her. You beat it by explaining the concepts it assumes — which is literally your product.
3. **ChatGPT.** Can explain a concept, can run a rough case. **You beat it on sequence** — a curriculum that knows what she should learn next, mastery checks, and spaced review. "It can answer any question but it can't tell you which question to ask next" is your line.
4. **A friend to practise with.** Normally better than any tool — and your product is the one that *includes* this rather than competing with it. **That's a strong position and your copy doesn't claim it.**

## What NOT to say

- **Don't say "AI case interview practice".** All ten of you say it, and it points at your weakest differentiator. Say **"teaches you first"**.
- **Don't write placement-panic urgency.** You're a curriculum; your buyer has months. Urgency copy brings the wrong user and she churns loudly.
- **Don't hide the plan lengths.** 4 weeks / 3 months / 6 months is unusual and good — it says *we know this takes time*, which is true and which no competitor admits.
- **Don't launch anything until there's a free way in.** It's the one fix that gates everything else.
- **One thing to fix quietly:** ₹3,499 for four weeks against ₹3,999 for three months makes Sprint look like a trap, and students notice and discuss pricing. Either widen the gap or drop Sprint.
