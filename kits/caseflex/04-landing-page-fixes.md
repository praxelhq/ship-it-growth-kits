# CaseFlex — Landing page conversion fixes

**Do these before you drive traffic.** Your window in the cluster schedule is days 15–19, so you have two weeks — use them.

**Your page is among the better ones in your cohort.** Strong H1 (*"Build your business muscle."*), a genuinely good subheadline (*"Every unsolved case is a question you'll freeze on later."*), a real pricing table with three tiers, a progression system, a five-dimension score, and — unusually for this cohort — **actual testimonials**. The problems are one pricing decision and one gate.

---

## Fix 1 — Ungate "Score my 60 seconds" 🔴

You have the best low-commitment hook in your cohort: *"Structure this problem in 60 seconds."* It's a complete, satisfying, 60-second challenge, and it's the right size for someone who will never commit to a forty-minute case.

**Then you charge an account for the answer.** She structures the problem, and the only way to find out how she did is `[Sign up for feedback]`.

That is the exact moment you've earned her attention, and you spend it asking for something.

**Give her the payoff first:**

> **Structuring: 7/10.** You split supply and demand cleanly, which most people don't.
> **The one thing you missed:** you never asked whether capacity changed in that period.
>
> `[See all five dimensions →]`

One dimension scored, one real line of feedback, then the ask. She now knows your scoring is specific rather than generic, and she has a reason to want the other four.

**This is the single highest-leverage change on your site**, and it's a day's work. Measure `60_second_attempted` → `signup` before and after.

---

## Fix 2 — Name your testimonials 🔴

You have three quotes, which already puts you ahead of nine of your eleven cohort-mates. But:

> *"I stopped re-reading frameworks and started doing reps. Huge difference in mocks."* — **Final-year student**

**An anonymous testimonial is weak precisely because it's anonymous.** This audience assumes the founder wrote it, and in a market where everyone's page is generated copy, they're not being unfair.

**Get names and campuses from the three people who said these things.** They already said them — you're only asking for attribution:

> *"I stopped re-reading frameworks and started doing reps. Huge difference in mocks."*
> — **[Name]**, final year, **[campus]**

If one of the three won't be named, replace them. **Two named beats four anonymous**, every time.

And note: *"I stopped re-reading frameworks and started doing reps"* is a better line than anything on your page. **Move it up into the hero area as a pull quote.** It's the whole argument against passive preparation in eleven words, and a user said it, which is what makes it land.

---

## Fix 3 — Show rupees to Indian visitors 🔴🔴

**Pro is $19/month. That is about ₹1,700.**

Your nearest cohort competitor, `caseforge.in`, sells at **₹499/month and ₹3,999/year**. You are priced at three and a half times the local market, in a currency your buyer doesn't think in, to an audience of students.

**This is the biggest conversion leak on your site and no amount of channel work in `02` compensates for it.** Student channels at $19 give you traffic and no conversions — which is worse than no traffic, because you'll misread it as a copy or product problem.

**Do this:**

1. **Geo-detect and show ₹ to Indian visitors.** A currency toggle is second-best; your cohort-mate `rolevia.xyz` runs one and it works, but a default is better than a choice.
2. **Price Pro at ₹399–₹599/month.** ₹499 is the defensible number — it matches the market and it's roughly one casebook.
3. **Add an annual option.** ₹3,999–₹4,499/year. Students in placement season think in seasons, not months, and annual pricing also fixes your retention problem for free.
4. **Keep $19 for non-Indian visitors.** It's a reasonable US price and your professional audience will pay it.

**If you genuinely don't want to localise**, then you must stop pitching Indian students, and `02`'s student channels become dead weight. That's a coherent strategy, and it's a much smaller business. **Pick deliberately** — don't end up in the third position, where you keep the dollar price and keep pitching students and can't work out why nothing converts.

---

## Fix 4 — Answer the casebook question above the fold 🟠

Your FAQ asks *"How is this different from reading a casebook?"* — the right question, buried in the wrong place. The people who bounce fastest never scroll to a FAQ.

You already have the answer in a testimonial. **Put it in the hero:**

> **Reading about cases is not practising cases.** Three scored reps a week, the same five dimensions every time, so you can see whether you're actually improving.
>
> *"I stopped re-reading frameworks and started doing reps."* — [Name], [campus]

Same for *"Why not just ask ChatGPT?"* if your FAQ has it: the honest answer is consistency across attempts, and it belongs above the fold.

---

## Fix 5 — Put the progression system above the fold 🟠

**Rookie → Associate → Manager → Partner** is one of your three real differentiators and it's a genuinely good one, because it answers the question a beginner actually has: *where do I start, and how will I know I'm getting better?*

Show the ladder visually, in the hero, with the current level marked. And show the five dimensions with sample scores next to it — a real scorecard, **with a middling total.**

**Use a 71, not a 94.** A 71 proves the thing scores honestly, which is the doubt that actually stops people.

---

## Fix 6 — Give the Team plan a real page 🟠

Your Team plan — cohort leaderboards, admin dashboard, custom cases, $12/seat — is **the most underexploited asset in your kit.** Every project in this cluster says a club secretary is worth more than any subreddit, and you are the only one with a product built for that conversation.

But right now it's a column in a pricing table.

**Build `caseflex.app/clubs`:**

- A screenshot of the cohort leaderboard. **This is what a club secretary actually wants** — he wants to see who's practising.
- A screenshot of the admin dashboard.
- **"Free for your club this placement season."** Not discounted. Free.
- One line on custom cases for their own competition.
- A single CTA: *"Set up your club — I'll do it for you."*

Then send *that* page in the 68 DMs from `03` #1, not your homepage. It converts a cold pitch into a product demo aimed at exactly his job.

---

## Fix 7 — Pick one audience in the copy 🟠

Your page currently addresses B-school students, pre-MBA candidates, early-career professionals, managers and founders. Five audiences in one hero means nobody sees themselves.

**Lead with the early-career professional** — she has money, she has no campus, and no cohort-mate is competing for her:

> **You don't have a campus giving you mock interviews. This is how you get reps anyway.**

Then let the muscle groups do the segmenting below the fold. Someone looking for Product or Founder's Office will find themselves there.

---

## Fix 8 — Two small things 🔵

- **Check the FAQ answers actually render.** An earlier read of your page showed FAQ question headers with no visible answers. If the accordions are broken, five objections are going unanswered on the page where you handle objections. **Test it on a phone.**
- **Check every nav and section link resolves.** Some earlier reads suggested category links (Concept Reviews, Consulting, Operations, M&A, Product Strategy) may be placeholders. **A dead link in your own feature list tells a visitor the product is unfinished** — either wire them up or remove them until they work.

## The test to run before you launch

**Give your phone to an Indian B-school student who has never seen CaseFlex and say nothing.** Two things to watch:

1. Does she attempt the 60-second exercise, and does she get a score without being asked to sign up?
2. **When she reaches pricing, does she react to the number?**

If she visibly flinches at the price, Fix 3 isn't done, and nothing in `02` will save the month.
