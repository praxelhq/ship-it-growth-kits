## Start here: your price is in dollars and your audience is in rupees

**Pro is $19/month. Team is $12/seat/month.**

$19 is about **₹1,700 a month**. The audience every channel in this cohort reaches is Indian B-school students, for whom ₹1,700/month is not a considered purchase — it's roughly what a month of mess fees costs, and it is far above what any competitor in your own cohort charges. `caseforge.in` sells at ₹499/month and ₹3,999/year. You are priced at three and a half times your nearest cohort competitor, in a currency your buyer doesn't think in.

**You have two honest options and you have to pick one this week:**

| Option | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **A. Localise.** Show ₹ to Indian visitors, price Pro at **₹399–₹599/month**. | Your signup target becomes achievable through the Indian student channels in `02`. **This is what the kit recommends.** |
| **B. Keep $19 and change who you sell to.** | Lean entirely into working professionals and international candidates, accept a much smaller addressable audience, and stop optimising for Indian student channels — because they will not convert at that price. |

**The kit assumes A, with one deliberate twist from B:** localise the price, *and* keep the professional audience as your positioning edge, because it's genuinely yours and nobody else in the cluster has it.

Do not do the third thing, which is to keep $19 and keep pitching Indian students. That's the path where you get traffic and no conversions and can't tell why.

## What you already have that nobody else in the cluster does

Three real assets, and you should know what they're worth:

1. **Testimonials on the page.** Nine of the eleven case-prep projects in this cohort have no social proof at all. You have three quotes. They need names (`04` Fix 2), but you are ahead.
2. **A non-consulting muscle-group library** — Strategy, Consulting, Marketing, Product, **Founder's Office.** That last one is a genuinely distinctive category nobody else offers.
3. **A Team plan for B-school clubs and L&D.** **This is the most underexploited asset in your entire kit.** Clubs are the best channel in this cluster, and you are the only project with a product built for selling into them. More on this below.

## Who your first 1,000 users actually are

Your page addresses "B-school students, pre-MBA candidates, early-career professionals, managers and founders" — which is four audiences, and in a thirty-day sprint that's three too many.

**Pick the one that is yours: the early-career professional switching into strategy, product or consulting, who has 2–6 years of experience, no campus, and a salary.**

In priority order:

1. **Early-career professionals targeting lateral consulting, strategy and product roles.** 2–6 years in. **They have money, they have no campus to give them mocks, and no other project in this cohort is built for them.** They are also the only audience for whom $19 was ever a plausible price.
2. **Pre-MBA candidates** with CAT/GMAT behind them and interviews ahead. Your "Rookie → Associate → Manager → Partner" progression is written for someone starting from zero, which is exactly her.
3. **B-school students via their clubs, not individually.** Your Team plan is the right instrument here — don't chase them one at a time at a price they won't pay.
4. **Founders and founder's-office candidates.** Small, distinctive, and the "Founder's Office" category is a real wedge nobody else has — there's a growing Indian founder's-office hiring market and literally nobody prepares people for it.

**Who is not your user:** the final-year undergraduate with ₹0. Four cohort-mates serve her better and cheaper. Chasing her at $19 is the fastest way to waste this month.

## The one-line pitch

Lead with the metaphor — it's yours, it's good, and it carries the habit:

> **A gym for business reasoning. Three reps a week, scored on the five things an interviewer actually judges, from Rookie to Partner.**

For the professional audience, which is your highest-value segment:

> **You don't have a campus giving you mock interviews. This is how you get reps anyway — scored, consistent, and on your own schedule.**

For a club, where your Team plan lives:

> **Cohort leaderboards and an admin dashboard for your members' case practice. Custom cases for your own competition.**

## The wedge use case

**"Score my 60 seconds."**

You already have the best-named CTA in this cohort. *"Structure this problem in 60 seconds"* is a complete, satisfying, low-commitment challenge — and it's the right size for someone who will not commit to a forty-minute case.

**But you gate the payoff.** She structures it, then has to sign up to find out how she did. That's the exact moment you've earned her interest and you ask her to pay for the answer with an account.

**Give her a partial score for free** (`04` Fix 1) — one dimension scored, one line of feedback, then *"see all five →"*. Payoff first, account second. That one change is worth more than any channel in `02`.

## Why someone picks you over the alternatives

1. **The nine others in this cohort.** You beat them on the progression system, the non-consulting muscle groups, and having any testimonials at all. **You lose to them badly on price in India** until Fix 3 ships.
2. **ChatGPT.** Your real competitor. You beat it on a consistent five-dimension score across attempts, pushback that doesn't let you hand-wave (your own testimonial says this better than your copy does), and a streak that makes you show up. Your page's FAQ already asks this question — answer it above the fold.
3. **A casebook.** Your own testimonial is the best answer anyone in this cohort has written: *"I stopped re-reading frameworks and started doing reps."* **Put that line in your H1 area.** It's the whole argument against passive preparation in eleven words.
4. **Nothing.** Most of your professional audience. They have no campus, no case partner, and no idea where to start — which is why the Rookie level matters.

## The Team plan is your quiet advantage. Use it.

Every kit in this cluster says the same thing: **a campus club secretary is worth more than any subreddit.** One warm introduction reaches 80 people who are all exactly your user, during the only month they care.

**You are the only project in the cluster with a product designed for that conversation** — cohort leaderboards, an admin dashboard, custom cases. Everyone else has to ask a club for a favour. You can offer them something a club actually wants.

So the move for this month: **give the Team plan away free to clubs for the season.** Not discounted — free. You are buying 1,000 signups and a case study, not $12 a seat. `03` #1 is written for that conversation and `06` puts it in week 1, because it's your single highest-leverage channel and nobody else can run it.

## What NOT to say

- **Don't say "AI-powered case interview platform".** Ten of you say that. Say "a gym for business reasoning" — the metaphor is yours and it carries the habit.
- **Don't address four audiences at once.** Pick the professional, with clubs as the volume channel.
- **Don't show $19 to an Indian visitor.** It's the single biggest conversion leak on your site.
- **Don't keep unnamed testimonials.** *"Final-year student"* is weak proof precisely because it's anonymous. Get names and campuses (`04` Fix 2).
- **Don't drift into GD, CAT prep or non-consulting functional interviews.** Three cohort-mates own those and do them better.
