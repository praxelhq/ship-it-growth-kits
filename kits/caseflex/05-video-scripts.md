# CaseFlex — Video scripts and shot lists

Scripts and shot lists only — **you record these on your phone.**

You have the best video premise in this cluster and it's sitting in your own CTA: **"Structure this problem in 60 seconds."** That's not a product demo, it's a challenge — and a challenge is the only thing that reliably stops a scroll. Most of your video plan is that one idea, repeated.

All vertical 9:16, captions burned in.

## Format A — The 60-second challenge (your workhorse; make 12)

**Length:** 30–40 seconds · **Where:** LinkedIn, Instagram, Reddit comments, your landing page

### Template

| Time | On screen | You say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–4s | The prompt, large text, nothing else | "Structure this in sixty seconds. A regional airline's load factor is down six percent. Competitors are flat." |
| 4–8s | A visible 60-second timer counting down | "Go. Pause the video and actually do it." |
| 8–18s | Your structure appearing as a tree | "Mine: supply or demand. Supply — did they add capacity, change the schedule, change the fleet. Demand — route mix, pricing, seasonality, a competitor's move." |
| 18–28s | **The trap, highlighted** | "The trap is going demand-first because it's more interesting. If they added capacity, the drop is arithmetic and demand has nothing to do with it. You just spent your best ten minutes in the wrong place." |
| 28–36s | The five-dimension scorecard with an honest total | "Scored version gives you five dimensions. I got 74 on this one — lost it on synthesis." |
| 36–40s | End card, URL captioned | "caseflex.app" |

### The twelve prompts

Spread across your muscle groups so the library does the segmenting:

| # | Muscle group | Prompt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Consulting | Regional airline, load factor down 6%, competitors flat |
| 2 | Consulting | Profits down 20% while revenue is flat |
| 3 | Strategy | Should a D2C skincare brand open physical stores? |
| 4 | Marketing | CAC has tripled on the main paid channel |
| 5 | Marketing | Market share falling while the category grows |
| 6 | Product | Daily actives flat, installs up 40% |
| 7 | Product | A feature shipped and nobody used it |
| 8 | **Founder's Office** | **Should we enter Pune before Hyderabad?** |
| 9 | **Founder's Office** | **Is this ₹40L senior hire worth it right now?** |
| 10 | Strategy | A competitor just cut prices 30% |
| 11 | Consulting | Two factories, one is half as productive |
| 12 | Guesstimate | How many kilograms of coffee does Bengaluru drink monthly? |

**Make the two Founder's Office ones early.** Nobody in your cohort — and almost nobody in India — produces content for that hiring market, and there's a real audience with nothing to watch.

### Rules

- **"Pause the video and actually do it" is the line that makes this format work.** It converts a viewer into a participant, and a participant remembers your product.
- **The trap is the payoff.** Every prompt needs one specific, non-obvious trap. Without it this is a framework video and there are ten thousand of those.
- **Post an honest score.** A 74 proves the scoring works; a 95 proves you can game your own product.
- **Visible timer.** It's free urgency and it makes the 60-second claim literal.

### Shot list

1. **Text-only opening card**, 0–8s. No face. The prompt alone stops the scroll better than a person does.
2. **Screen or paper for the structure tree.** Overhead paper shot with a black marker is fine and reads as honest.
3. **Highlight the trap** — a circle, an arrow, a colour change. This is the frame people screenshot.
4. **Real scorecard**, zoomed so the five dimensions are readable on a phone.
5. **Talking head optional.** This format works entirely without your face, which means you can batch twelve in an afternoon.

## Format B — "Nobody is giving you mock interviews" (your most distinctive video)

**Length:** 40–50 seconds · **Where:** LinkedIn, professional communities, r/consulting replies

This is `03` #2 on camera, and it speaks to an audience **no other project in this cohort addresses.**

> **(0–6s, your face)** "If you're switching into consulting or product from a job, here's what nobody warns you about."
>
> **(6–18s, split screen: a campus case club room vs. one person at a desk)** "B-school students get a case club, a buddy system, seniors who've done it, forty people in the same process. You get a casebook PDF and a Sunday afternoon."
>
> **(18–30s, your face)** "I've watched good candidates lose lateral processes — not because they couldn't reason, but because the first time they said their reasoning out loud to anyone was in the actual interview."
>
> **(30–42s, product: a rep, then the scorecard)** "So: three reps a week, fifteen minutes, scored on the five things that actually get judged. Rookie up to Partner. There's a streak, because the only real failure mode here is not showing up."
>
> **(42–50s, your face, URL captioned)** "Sixty-second exercise on the homepage, no account needed. caseflex.app"

**Why this is your best video:** it names a real, specific, unaddressed disadvantage for an audience with money. **Pin it to your LinkedIn profile.**

## Format C — The progression video

**Length:** 35–45 seconds · **Where:** landing page (`04` Fix 5), Peerlist, anyone asking "where do I start"

Your Rookie → Associate → Manager → Partner ladder answers the beginner's real question, and it's hard to convey in text.

> **(0–6s, the ladder graphic, Rookie highlighted)** "If you've never done a case, the problem isn't frameworks. It's that nobody tells you where to start or how you'd know you're improving."
>
> **(6–18s, a Rookie-level rep)** "Rookie is just: break this problem into parts. No framework names, nothing you're assumed to know."
>
> **(18–30s, the ladder advancing, score curve rising)** "You move up when the scores say you should, not when you decide you're ready. Associate, Manager, Partner."
>
> **(30–40s, the five dimensions)** "Same five dimensions the whole way, which is the point — your score in week one and week six mean the same thing, so the curve is real."
>
> **(40–45s)** "caseflex.app. Free tier is three reps a week."

**The score curve is the shot.** A rising line across six weeks is the most persuasive image you can show someone deciding whether practice works.

## Format D — The honest pricing video (make 1, week 1)

**Length:** 25–30 seconds · **Where:** LinkedIn, Peerlist comment, anyone who says "too expensive"

You launched in dollars at a price Indian students won't pay. **Fixing it publicly is better content than fixing it quietly.**

> **(0–6s, your face)** "I launched this at nineteen dollars a month. For an Indian student that's about seventeen hundred rupees. That was a mistake and I should have seen it immediately."
>
> **(6–16s)** "My nearest competitor charges four ninety-nine. I was at three and a half times the local market, in a currency nobody here thinks in."
>
> **(16–26s, the new ₹ pricing on screen)** "It's ₹[number] in India now. Dollar price stays for everyone else. Free tier didn't change — three reps a week, and it isn't a trial."
>
> **(26–30s)** "caseflex.app"

**Why post this:** founders admitting a pricing mistake get a disproportionately good reception, and it reaches exactly the people who bounced off $19 and will now come back. It's also just true, which is the main reason.

## Format E — The club leaderboard video (for club secretaries)

**Length:** 40–50 seconds · **Where:** attached to the 68 DMs in `03` #1, and on `caseflex.app/clubs`

Different audience, different register. A club secretary doesn't care about your founder story — he wants to know whether his members are practising.

> **(0–8s, you, seated, shirt)** "Hi — I'm [name]. This is the club version of CaseFlex, free for your placement season. Under a minute."
>
> **(8–22s, the cohort leaderboard)** "Your members get scored case practice. You get a leaderboard — who's practising, who's improving, who hasn't started. That's the part most club secretaries tell me they actually want."
>
> **(22–34s, the admin dashboard)** "Admin dashboard shows reps per member and score trends across the cohort. If you're running a mock programme, this tells you who needs a slot."
>
> **(34–44s, custom cases)** "You can add your own cases if you run a competition."
>
> **(44–50s, you)** "Free for the season, and I'll set it up for your members myself. caseflex.app/clubs, or just reply."

**Register:** shirt, quiet room, no music, no jump cuts. Say **"free for the season"** twice and **"I'll set it up myself"** once. Under a minute.

## Production rules

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Vertical 9:16, captions burned in** | Muted autoplay everywhere |
| **"Pause and actually do it"** in every Format A | Turns viewers into participants |
| **Every prompt needs a specific trap** | Without it you're making generic framework content |
| **Honest scores only — post the 74s** | The only credible way to sell a tool that scores people |
| **Format A needs no face** | Which is why you can batch twelve in one afternoon |
| **Format E is formal; the rest are not** | Club secretaries are a different audience |
| **Full URL `caseflex.app` captioned**, never spoken | Three cohort-mates have near-identical names |
| **No logo intro, no music under speech** | |

**Make Format E first** — the club DMs in `06` week 1 are your highest-value channel and they need it. **Then Format B**, which is your most distinctive piece and the one to pin. Then batch twelve Format A reels in one afternoon and post two a week for six weeks. Format D goes out the day the rupee pricing ships.
