# Place a Brick — Landing-page fixes

## 1. Resolve the real prelaunch policy gaps

Current headline **“Put your name in stone. One brick at a time.”** and preview language about being etched “forever” can imply physical permanence. The actual terms/privacy pages say they are drafts needing replacement before launch. Do not run a broad paid campaign while that publication gap remains. The owner should publish accurate final product/payment/privacy explanations and verify the real checkout/placement/refund paths; no legal review was completed here.

Replacement supporting copy: **“A digital piece on a shared virtual monument. Your chosen name is public. No physical item, investment or charitable donation.”** Replace “forever” with a truthful service-duration explanation, such as **“Your piece stays visible while this monument is hosted”**, only if that matches the actual commitment.

## 2. Keep inspect and buy separate

Actual monument page has piece quantities, **“Name to etch”**, $1 total and **“Sign in to build”**, linked to the same-monument return route. Keep **“Explore the monument”** available without purchase. Test signup→return-to-monument→correct total→payment→actual piece privately before promising a successful purchase. Do not invent a thank-you screen or claim a reservation guarantees delivery.

## 3. Make currency and public name unmistakable

Use **“US$1 for one digital brick”** and the actual final checkout total. The chosen name is public on every element in the order; preserve that warning directly beside the field. Suggest **“Choose a display name you are comfortable making public.”** Do not prefill a real name from authentication without an informed choice.

## 4. Explain stock and failure honestly

The page says pieces are held for thirty minutes during payment. Verify concurrent availability and actual failure/refund handling with the owner. Terms currently distinguish sold-out payment refunds from final etched contributions: do not advertise an unconditional refund promise. Show a clear next action if payment succeeds but placement is delayed.

## 5. Proof, phone and privacy

Homepage progress counters are site claims, not audited revenue. Keep progress labels accurate and remove test data from public totals. Test dragging the monument, tapping a piece, quantity buttons and login return on a phone. Existing privacy mentions Clarity, Supabase and Vercel; exclude etched-name typing, identity and checkout from replay. No fake donor quote or university badge is needed.
