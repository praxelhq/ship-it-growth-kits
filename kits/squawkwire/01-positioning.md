## Your first reader

An adult learning to follow one public financial-news source, who wants to open the original item and distinguish its publication time from the dashboard's time and machine score. Start with source literacy, not day traders demanding low latency or people seeking a stock pick.

> Read a financial headline with its original source and timing visible, then inspect the automated label as a fallible interpretation.

Bio: **The source before the score.**

The first test is one genuinely supported source and a small current feed sample. Can the reader open the original, find the date, notice delay and understand the score isn't a prediction? If the current private feed can't do that, fix it before offering a pilot. No real-money action is needed to demonstrate value.

## Actual alternatives

[The Federal Reserve's official press-release page](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases.htm) is a public no-extra-software-fee route to its own announcements. Readers can bookmark an official source or use an existing reader. That is narrower than a multi-source terminal but a legitimate alternative to an unexplained login. No comparable paid terminal price was verified, so don't invent a Bloomberg or newswire comparison.

Your difference must be useful source organization, honest delay and checkable categorization, not the implication that official information is available only through you. Automated sentiment is not market impact, and a bold score doesn't establish accuracy or a trade opportunity.

## The growth gate

Publish actual operator/support/privacy commitments and a synthetic or properly sourced public preview before asking strangers to register. Verify source rights and linkbacks; don't redistribute full publisher content without permission. Keep **1,000 individual accounts** as the target, with **one source-and-timestamp review completed and a later independent news-reading return** beside it. Login visits, score cards and alerts sent aren't that activation.
