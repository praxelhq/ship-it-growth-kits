# Expiry Radar — Landing-page fixes

[Entry](https://exptrack.site/) and the [actual auth destination](https://life-expiry-guide.lovable.app/auth) were opened 29 October. The site is live despite the old roster label. Account creation/private tracking and notification delivery were not tested.

## 1. Make the domain transition predictable

Current H1: **“Use it before you lose it.”** CTA: **“Create your free account.”** It leads from the branded domain to a Lovable app auth screen headed **“Make room for more.”**

Keep the real destination visible and add **“Continue to the Expiry Radar app.”** Use consistent product identity across entry/auth. A working handoff can still lose trust if it looks like an unrelated destination; don't label the redirect a broken link.

## 2. Give the broad headline a concrete first task

Subhead replacement: **“Track one credit, pass or trial with its actual expiry/reset date and remaining use.”** Primary action: **“Track my first item.”** Explain the account requirement before the handoff, not after someone begins writing a long tracker.

## 3. Differentiate expiry, reset and renewal

The page groups monthly credits, daily limits and one-time offers. Give each a clear rule and example. Use **“Expires once”**, **“Resets on a schedule”** and **“Renews/charges”** only if the app actually supports that distinction. Don't silently turn every item into a monthly reminder.

## 4. State reminder/integration reality

The public page doesn't prove email/push delivery, provider sync or automatic cancellation. Say exactly what works today and test it with your own example. Interim copy: **“Your tracker shows the dates you enter. Check the actual provider terms before acting.”** No guaranteed savings or missed-charge prevention claim.

## 5. Keep sensitive input out of the first task

Use **“A label, amount/usage and date are enough. Don't enter passwords or full account/card details.”** Explain storage, export and deletion accurately before private data is requested. Don't invent a security badge or claim all data stays local merely because the landing page is simple.

## 6. Make empty/success states useful

After a tested save, show the item, next date and what remains; offer correction. If signup/save fails, preserve non-sensitive draft details where the privacy policy allows. The kit hasn't established the private flow, so validate it before a launch rather than claiming a defect or success.

## 7. Share a product preview, not a private balance

Title: **“Expiry Radar — keep credits, passes and dates in view.”** Description: **“A focused personal tracker for what expires or resets next.”** Don't put actual item names/amounts into public previews. Test phone keyboard, date picker/time zone and slow-network auth return.
