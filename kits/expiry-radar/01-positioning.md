## The first person

An adult paying for a few subscriptions or holding passes/credits who has missed a reset or expiry at least once. Start with one item they already know the terms for, not a request to upload their whole financial life.

**Pitch:** Put the credit or pass you keep forgetting beside its real expiry date.

**Bio:** Remember what you already paid for.

The wedge is “one thing that expires next,” with its source terms checked by the user. A recorded unused value is not money saved until it is genuinely used or a charge is legitimately avoided. Don't promise universal automated cancellation, account sync or notifications unless the current product supports and proves them.

[Notion's personal Free plan](https://www.notion.com/pricing) costs $0 and can hold an expiry table. A paper list or an existing calendar is another no-additional-software-cost substitute. Your reason to choose Expiry Radar is a focused view of reset/expiry and usage, without designing a database first.

Target stays **1,000 signups**, counted as unique real accounts created, with **one genuine item entered with a checked date/reset rule** alongside them. The free-account promise is public; a whole-product forever-free or future paid price is not. Ten sample passes in one person's account are one user, not ten.
