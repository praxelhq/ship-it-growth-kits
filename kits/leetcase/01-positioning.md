## You have the clearest product thesis in your cohort and the worst URL

Of the eleven case-prep projects in this Ship It cohort, **you are the only one who isn't selling practice.** You're selling the *system around* practice, and your own copy nails it:

> **"The Operating System for Consulting & Strategy Prep"**
> *"Replace unorganized mock partner calls and static PDF casebooks with an intelligent, rubric-driven interactive platform."*

Everyone else gives her cases. **You give her a tracker, a streak, an error log, curated sheets, a leaderboard and a readiness score against her actual interview date.** That's a different product — it's the LeetCode model applied to cases, and the name says so.

**That is a genuinely strong position and the name is an asset, not a liability.** A large share of Indian MBA students are ex-engineers who ground LeetCode for placements. They know exactly what "LeetCase" means in half a second, and no competitor can borrow that.

**And you have the most aggressive price in the cluster by a factor of five.** ₹100/month against CaseForge's ₹499, lowercase's ₹3,499 and CaseFlex's $19. A free-forever tier with 150+ questions and 10 AI evaluations a month sits under it. **Nobody in this market can undercut you**, which makes paid conversion your easiest win and your best weapon.

### Then there's the URL

**`leetcase.rambhardwaj.in`**

- It cannot be said out loud in a WhatsApp group. Try it.
- It cannot be typed from memory.
- It is a subdomain of somebody's personal site, which reads as *side project* to the exact audience you need to trust an AI with their interview preparation.
- **Your own product screenshots say `leetcase.com/track-prep`** — so you already know what the domain should be.

**This is your single highest-leverage fix** (`04` Fix 1). Every channel in `02` is a link someone has to paste, remember or type, and you're paying a tax on all of them. A `.in` or `.app` is a few hundred rupees.

## Who your first 1,000 users actually are

**The grinder.** The person who, left alone, will open your product 25 times in a month — because she's the sort of person who kept a LeetCode streak.

In priority order:

1. **Ex-engineer MBA students targeting MBB and Big 4.** Your core user, and she's enormous — a large share of every Indian B-school batch is ex-engineering. **She has already lived this exact product once, for coding, and it worked.** You don't have to explain the concept to her; you have to tell her it exists.
2. **The disorganised earnest preparer.** She's doing cases and has no idea whether she's improving, which cases she's done, or what she keeps getting wrong. Your error log and readiness score are the only answer to that in the whole cluster. **This is the emotional core of your product.**
3. **Students 2–4 months out from interviews.** Your product rewards accumulation — a streak, a completion map, a score trend. Someone with a shortlist on Friday can't accumulate anything, so she's the wrong buyer.
4. **Case-prep groups and consulting clubs.** You have a leaderboard; a cohort makes it work. Clubs are the best channel in this cluster and your product has a built-in reason for them to care.

**Who is not your user:** the beginner who doesn't know what MECE is (lowercase serves her), the GD candidate (Role.via), the non-consulting functional candidate (CaseForge), the CAT aspirant (Skill Prep). **Your cohort is big enough for all of you if each stays in lane**, and yours is the grinder.

## The one-line pitch

Lead with the system, because the practice is commoditised and the system isn't:

> **LeetCode for case interviews. Track every case, log every mistake, keep the streak, and see your readiness score against your actual interview date.**

Shortest version, which is the one that travels:

> **LeetCode, but for case interviews. Free tier, ₹100/month. leetcase.[domain]**

For the disorganised preparer, who is the deepest need:

> **You've done fourteen cases and you have no idea if you're getting better. This tells you.**

## The wedge use case

**The error log.**

Not the AI rubric — nine competitors have one. **The error log is the thing nobody else in this cluster built**, and it's the feature that makes the rest matter:

> *"Dump Your Error Log"*

A candidate's real problem isn't that she can't do cases. It's that **she makes the same three mistakes for two months and never notices**, because each case feels like a fresh event. Writing the mistake down and seeing it recur is the whole mechanism of improvement, and it's what a mock partner can't give her.

**Lead with it.** The moment you're selling is week three, when she opens her log and sees *"didn't check capacity before blaming demand"* for the fifth time. That's the moment she stops making it — and the moment she tells her batch about you.

## Why someone picks you over the alternatives

1. **The nine others in this cohort.** You beat them on everything structural — tracking, streaks, error logs, sheets, leaderboard, readiness score — and on price, by 5×. **You are not really competing on practice quality**, and you shouldn't try to.
2. **A spreadsheet.** Your actual competitor for the organised preparer, and it's free. You beat it because it can't score her, can't surface a pattern, and she stops updating it by week two. Say that; everyone has abandoned a prep spreadsheet.
3. **ChatGPT.** Can run a case. **Cannot remember that she did fourteen of them, or that she keeps making the same error.** That's your whole argument and it's unusually strong — memory is the one thing a chat window structurally lacks.
4. **Finding a mock partner.** Your own testimonial puts it better than your copy does: *"What used to take hours finding case partners now happens in 20 minutes."* **Put that line in the hero.**

## Your three weaknesses, in order

**1. The URL.** Covered above. Fix it this week.

**2. You were down.** Your domain didn't resolve on 28 Oct, during placement season. For a product whose pitch is *"the operating system for your prep"*, **being unavailable is worse than it would be for anyone else in this cluster** — an operating system that isn't there is not an operating system. `04` Fix 2: uptime monitoring and a status check, and don't skip it because it feels like infrastructure rather than growth.

**3. One testimonial, unnamed.** You have a good quote and no attribution. Get the name and the campus (`04` Fix 4). For a product asking her to trust an AI's judgement of her thinking, a named person is worth more than any feature.

## What NOT to say

- **Don't say "AI-powered case interview platform".** All ten of you say it, and it points at your most commoditised feature. Say **"LeetCode for cases"** — four words, instantly understood by your core user, unavailable to any competitor.
- **Don't lead with the AI rubric.** Lead with tracking, the error log and the streak. The rubric is table stakes; the system is yours.
- **Don't apologise for ₹100.** It is the correct price for a student market and it is a weapon. **Do not raise it this month** — your month-1 job is signups, and you have the cheapest paid tier in the market by five times.
- **Don't chase beginners.** Your product rewards accumulation, so a beginner with no history gets the least from it. lowercase serves her properly.
- **Don't promise MBB outcomes you can't evidence.** "Land the Offer at MBB" is fine as an aspiration in an H1; a claimed conversion rate would not be, and this audience checks.
