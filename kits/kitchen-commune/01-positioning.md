# kitchen commune — Positioning

**Live:** https://kitchencommune.in/ · **Category:** Online cooking companionship · **Profile:** https://shipped.money/p/kitchen-commune

The actual headline checked 29 October is **“Don't just find a recipe. Find someone to cook it with.”** The CTA is **“Match for tonight.”** The site describes matching by intent/time/vibe, adding pantry items, a shared menu suggestion and a private video room. It says **free while in beta**, with later pricing from **₹99/$3 per session**. Do not advertise that later price as a live charge or promise beta access stays free forever.

Your first users are two adults cooking simple dinners alone at the same time. They already want company, have a kitchen and can commit to a half-hour window. Start with a small scheduled cohort, not an empty worldwide feed.

**Pitch:** Find a willing home cook free at the same time, choose something you can both make, and cook together online.

**Short version:** Two kitchens. One dinner window. Some company.

## Your wedge

The first useful event is an accepted same-time session that both people actually attend. Recipe generation is support, not the whole product. The page explicitly frames friendship, not dating. Don't use romantic-match copy to manufacture demand.

[WhatsApp](https://www.whatsapp.com/) provides free video calls, so two friends can already cook together at $0 using a recipe they know. Kitchen Commune earns interest through finding a willing same-time person, shared pantry planning and a deliberate session. Do not claim ordinary video calls cannot connect two kitchens.

## The uncomfortable bit

A thousand profiles with nobody available at the same hour is a failed matching product. Recruit one narrow dinner window, check both people's availability and confirm the actual room flow before widening invitations. Show illustrative profiles as examples, not proof of live matching supply.

The site claims email verification, private rooms, report/block and dietary checks. This research did not register, match, join a room or verify those controls. Test them. Dish suggestions still require users to check their own ingredients, preferences and restrictions; don't promise an AI menu is safe for every allergy.

Keep the B2C target at 1,000 accounts, with accepted sessions and two-person attended cooks beside it. A match request, demo profile or room link alone isn't a completed shared dinner.
