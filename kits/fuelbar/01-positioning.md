# FuelBar — Positioning

**Live:** https://fuelbar.app/ · **Category:** Developer tool · **Profile:** https://shipped.money/p/fuelbar

Your first buyer is a solo builder using two or more AI coding subscriptions on Windows, who keeps interrupting work to check which tool has capacity left. Start there rather than “everyone using AI.” The Mac market already has a strong free alternative; a Windows pill and the visible session-finished state give you a more specific first demonstration.

The actual headline is “FuelBar. Every AI coding limit, on the edge of your screen.” “Download” scrolls to the price block, then “Pay & download” begins the purchase path. The checked public [price endpoint](https://fuelbar.app/api/price) returns **₹249 / $2.99**, one time. The source's ₹999/₹499 animation stops are not the price. Checkout and downloaded builds were not tested by this writer.

## The pitch

> See the remaining capacity of the AI coding tools you already use, without leaving your work. One small Windows or Mac pill, bought once.

Short version: **Which coding tool has room left? Glance, don't tab-hop.**

Your first demo is the moment a long coding session finishes and a builder checks what to use next. Show the active tool, its particular limit window, reset time and last update. A colored ring with no window or freshness information is decoration, not a reliable decision.

The page lists Claude, Codex, Cursor, Copilot, Freebuff and Antigravity. Don't broaden that to “every AI tool,” universal token counts or exact bill prediction. Limits can mean sessions, weekly windows, monthly requests or spending, and they are not interchangeable units.

## The weakness that earns trust

Numbers can be old. [Your help page](https://fuelbar.app/help.html) explicitly describes expired sign-ins, sources that update only while their app is open and a stale-reading label. This is the most important objection to answer. A builder needs to know “read three minutes ago,” not just “84%.” Show the stale state in the marketing demo and how to refresh through the provider's normal sign-in flow.

You also read sensitive local sign-in material to ask provider services for usage. The page says tokens only go to those services and are not stored by FuelBar. That's a product claim to explain and verify, not a reason to claim an independent security audit. Never put credentials or logs in a public demo.

Keep the **1,000 consumer-signup target**, counted as new installs, with first useful connected reading reported alongside. A paid-only utility with a small audience will need much stronger reach than this first experiment provides. State the gap; do not count views of the animated price as installs.
