**Pitch:** Ask a reading question and inspect the original writing behind the answer.

**Short version:** Find the passage, not an invented opinion.

## The wedge

One narrow question, one cited passage, one click back to the original. Your live page covers five thinkers and explicitly says an author may never have addressed a topic. Make that refusal a demonstration: when the corpus does not support a claim, the answer should say so.

The $0 alternative is reading/searching the originals: [Berkshire's shareholder letters](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/letters.html) and [Paul Graham's essays](https://www.paulgraham.com/articles.html) are public primary sources. Mimir earns attention by narrowing the search and preserving context. Do not claim it is the only way to find a quote or that those authors endorse the tool.

## Your trust problem

The page uses absolute language about hallucinations and guaranteed citations. A citation-looking link can still point to the wrong passage. Prove answer-to-source alignment on a small test set before inviting a larger audience. Show unsupported questions and the limit, not only your best example.

The public page was read, but no signed-in question was submitted during research. Corpus counts and coverage are site claims, not independently audited totals. Do not extend the product into investment recommendations or impersonated advice from living/deceased authors.

Keep the B2C target at 1,000 accounts. Report a first answer with a source actually opened beside it. A thousand accounts that trust the summary without checking the text are a weaker outcome than a smaller reading group that repeatedly follows evidence.
